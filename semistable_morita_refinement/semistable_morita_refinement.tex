\large A supported comparison for a subdivided annulus}

\begin{abstract}
We formulate a supported Morita comparison for an explicit modification
of a thickness-two nodal model.  The modification introduces an exceptional
projective line and two thickness-one nodes.  We compute its charts and
identify the complete inverse-image support, including the smooth part of
the exceptional component.  Using standard generation and recollement mechanisms for actual rational
unipotent coefficients on the algebraic normal pair, we establish unit generation, a finite-descent generator, explicit link
reconstruction, closure under the supported operations, and duality of
the attaching datum.  We also realize its unit and localization projectors
by geometric diagonal kernels.  A $K_0$ obstruction shows that the full
exceptional constructible category cannot itself be refinement invariant.
We also identify the modification as an fs log blow-up and apply
Fujiwara--Kato acyclicity to obtain an actual finite-coefficient
logarithmic Morita comparison, before forgetting base inertia.
For geometric rational unipotent coefficients we identify the actual
nearby image on the exceptional curve by an intrinsic costalk-vanishing
condition, and identify its actual complete-support partner by nearby
Verdier duality.  This gives a module-valued star comparison and its
supported partner for the single modification.  For each fixed thickness
$xy=t^m$, we give pairwise supported Morita comparisons for integer
cut models over the same base.  Their branchwise coefficient categories
have geometric generators, and the specified proper maps compare
ambient attaching maps, localization triangles, Verdier partners and
minimal diagonal kernels.  The coefficient passage is constructed in
selected adic systems before rationalization.  On the common rational
unipotent source, an explicit one-modification bridge identifies these
maps with the companion paper's local Kummer realization and trace
pairing, with node support interpreted as a costalk.  For a fixed coarse support index we organize the fibrewise-cartesian
ambient categories, localization, tensor, duality and minimal kernel
actions into a coherent refinement diagram, and extend the local
realization bridge naturally to that diagram.  An unrestricted
support/correspondence profile remains a separate target.  Neither this target nor arbitrary model or
base-change independence is claimed as proved.  The logarithmic
finite-coefficient comparison and the geometric rational nearby
comparison have different sources; base inertia, analytic realization
and integral supported refinement are not identified between them.
\end{abstract}

\section{The supported comparison and its scope}
Let $R$ be a complete strictly henselian discrete valuation ring with
uniformizer $t$, algebraically closed residue field $k$ of characteristic
$p$, and fraction field $K$.  Fix $\ell\ne p$ and a complete algebraically
closed valued extension $\Omega/K$.  Put
\[
 X=\Spec R[x,y]/(xy-t^2),\qquad Y=V(x,y,t),
 \qquad \mu:X'=\operatorname{Bl}_{(x,t)}X\longrightarrow X.
\]
We keep the base $R$ fixed.  Thus the first example works also when $p=2$;
no claim about a tame quadratic base extension is required.  The singular
model $X$ is a toroidal nodal model of thickness two, not the regular
thickness-one model of the preceding local-realization paper.

\begin{target}[The general supported refinement problem]\label{target:refinement}
Construct from intrinsically characterized actual nearby-admissible
geometric coefficient categories a cellular Morita datum $\mathcal C(X,Y)$ and $\mathcal C(X',\mu^{-1}Y)$, together with a
comparison induced by $\mu$.  Prove that the comparison is a Morita
equivalence after the specified cellular descent, compatible with
restriction, costalk, the localization attaching maps and Verdier duality.
Identify its stratumwise cochain algebras and dualizing bimodules with the
local realizations wherever the latter's hypotheses have been verified.
Prove compatibility with compositions of the allowed modifications and
finite geometric descent, and with a diagonal unit and the basic
normal-pair correspondence.
\end{target}
The intended invariant is the entire coefficient datum on the stratified
model: the stratum categories and their $A^*$-algebras, the supported
$A^!$-partners, actual restriction/costalk/localization functors and
attaching cells, and normal inertia.  Supported functors may have
different source and target support categories; they are not all
endomorphisms of a single coefficient category.  In this sense the
intended comparison has the schematic form
\[
 \mu_*^{\mathrm{cell}}\mathcal C_{\Sigma_{X'}}
       \simeq_{\mathrm{Mor}}\mathcal C_{\Sigma_X}.
\]
Here $\Sigma$ records actual strata and their incidence geometry.
Theorem~\ref{thm:cellular-refinement} proves pairwise equivalences for
integer-cut models of a fixed $xy=t^m$, with the stated geometric
rational coefficients and complete inverse-image supports.  It includes
the specified module, support and attachment maps.  It does not establish
the displayed schematic formula as an equivalence of fully coherent
profiles; that stronger construction is Target~\ref{target:enhanced-profile}.

The local finite-coefficient inputs are the explicit geometry and
Nakayama's Kummer calculation.  Lemma~\ref{lem:selected-adic-comparisons}
constructs the selected adic comparisons; Theorem~\ref{thm:cell-reconstruction}
proves geometric generation; Lemma~\ref{lem:cell-algebra-unit} constructs
the algebra and supported module mates; and
Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre-cartesian-ambient} compares arbitrary retained
attaching maps.  These supply the pairwise theorem and
Corollary~\ref{cor:cell-kernels}.  The logarithmic finite-coefficient
comparison is a separate application of Fujiwara--Kato acyclicity.
Proposition~\ref{prop:one-modification-realization-bridge} connects
the one-modification comparison to the companion paper's already
proved rational unipotent interface, including the specified
coefficient functor, module actions and support types.
Theorem~\ref{thm:anchored-coherent-descent} organizes these maps on a
fixed coarse support index; Corollary~\ref{cor:family-realization-bridge}
places the coefficient interface in the same diagram.  In this paper
``canonical'' refers to this specified cut/support system, not to a
higher model-independent pseudofunctor on arbitrary correspondences.

\paragraph{Inputs and contribution.}
Fujiwara--Kato log-blow-up acyclicity and Nakayama's constant nearby
calculation are external inputs, in the form recorded by Illusie
\cite{IllusieOverview}.  Achinger--Ogus describe exterior cohomology
and connecting/monodromy data \cite[PDF version, \S6.3]{AchingerOgus};
we use this as background, not as an adic or module comparison input.
Scherotzke--Sibilla--Talpo's Morita invariance concerns coherent
parabolic coefficients \cite{SSTMcKay}, not the constructible
coefficients used here.  Recollement, thick generation from finite
filtrations, one-generator Morita reconstruction and transport of an
operation diagram along equivalences are standard mechanisms.

The geometric comparisons supplied here are the actual two-branch
Kummer maps with their coefficient actions, the intrinsic admissible
nearby image on the complete exceptional support, and the comparison
of arbitrary retained attachments across coarse fibres.  We organize
these specified maps, their supported partners and the minimal kernel
diagrams over a fixed coarse support index, and identify the common
coefficient interface with the fixed companion manuscript.
This is the contribution claimed, without a priority claim for the
standard mechanisms or for general model independence.

\paragraph{Kummer classes and Tate twists.}
For an invertible coordinate $z$, the notation $[z]$ means its actual
Kummer class in $H^1(-,\Lambda_r(1))$ at finite precision and in
$H^1(-,Q(1))$ after the specified adic passage and rationalization.
Calling this a degree-one generator means the induced isomorphism
$Q(-1)\to H^1(-,Q)$ in the rank-one situations below, not a scalar
basis of untwisted cohomology.  The Kummer morphism is
$Q\to Q(1)[1]$.  Identities such as $[w]=-[z]$ are identities in
twisted cohomology and of these induced maps.  We do not choose a
trivialization $Q(1)\simeq Q$; compatible roots of the uniformizer
used for coordinate transport do not constitute such a trivialization.

\section{A modification that changes the model}
\begin{proposition}[Charts and the exceptional support]\label{prop:charts}
The modification $\mu$ is proper and is an isomorphism on the generic
fibre.  It has affine charts
\[
 U_x=\Spec R[x,v]/(xv-t),\quad y=xv^2,
 \qquad U_t=\Spec R[u,y]/(uy-t),\quad x=tu.
\]
On their overlap $u=v^{-1}$.  The complete scheme-theoretic inverse image
of $Y$ is a reduced exceptional curve $E\simeq\mathbf P^1_k$.
The special fibre of $X'$ has two nodes $q_0,q_\infty$ on $E$, and
$E^\circ=E\setminus\{q_0,q_\infty\}\simeq\mathbf G_{m,k}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The ring $B=R[x,y]/(xy-t^2)$ embeds in $K[x,x^{-1}]$ by $y=t^2/x$.
The $x$-chart of the blowup is the subalgebra $B[t/x]$ of its fraction
field.  Setting $v=t/x$ gives $xv=t$ and $y=xv^2$.  This identifies it
with the displayed $R$-flat domain; using the subalgebra description
removes the spurious torsion that would result from retaining only the
uncancelled equation $x(y-xv^2)=0$.  Similarly the $t$-chart is $B[x/t]$,
with $u=x/t$ and $uy=t$.  The two ratio coordinates are inverse on the
overlap.  Properness follows from the projective Rees construction, and
$(x,t)$ is the unit ideal after inverting $t$.

On $U_x$ the ideal $(x,y,t)$ pulls back to $(x)$; on $U_t$ it pulls back
to $(y)$.  These give $\Spec k[v]$ and $\Spec k[u]$, glued by $uv=1$.
Thus the inverse image is the reduced $\mathbf P^1$, with no support
components omitted.  The special fibres have equations $xv=0$ and
$uy=0$.  Their two intersections are $q_0=(x,v)$ and
$q_\infty=(u,y)$.  These equations also verify that the new nodes have
thickness one and that the models are regular there.
\end{proof}

Let $T=]Y[_X$ denote the tube in the generic fibre of the $t$-adic
completion.  Normalize the valuation by $w(t)=1$.
\begin{proposition}[The full tube under subdivision]\label{prop:tubes}
The generic-fibre identification carries $]\mu^{-1}Y[_{X'}$ onto $T$.
In the $x$ coordinate,
\[
 T=\{\,|t|^2<|x|<1\,\}.
\]
The tubes of $q_0,E^\circ,q_\infty$ partition $T$ by the conditions
\[
 0<w(x)<1,\qquad w(x)=1,\qquad 1<w(x)<2,
\]
respectively.  The middle domain has coordinate $u=x/t$ with $|u|=1$
and reduction in $\mathbf G_{m,k}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Specialization commutes with the formal morphism, so membership in the
tube of the complete inverse image is equivalent to specialization in
$Y$.  Alternatively, $|x|<1$ and $|y|<1$ give $|t|^2<|x|<1$ directly.
On $U_x$, specializing to $q_0$ requires $|x|,|v|<1$ and $xv=t$, giving
the first interval.  On $U_t$, specializing to $q_\infty$ requires
$|u|,|y|<1$ and $uy=t$, giving the last interval.  Specialization to
$E^\circ$ requires $u$ and $u^{-1}$ integral, hence $|x|=|t|$.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
This partition is not an open analytic cover.  In particular it supplies
no ordinary open-cover Cech descent proof.  The middle domain cannot be
discarded, and compact-support duality for boundaryless open annuli
cannot be copied onto it without checking its support convention.
The rank-zero relative normal direction on $E^\circ$ does not erase its
residue $\mathbf G_m$ geometry or the monodromy of its coefficients.
\end{remark}

\section{A logarithmic refinement comparison already available}
\begin{proposition}[The modification is an fs log blow-up]
\label{prop:log-blowup}
Equip $X$ with the chart $P\to R[x,y]/(xy-t^2)$, where
\[
 P=\langle a,b,c\mid a+b=2c\rangle,\qquad
 a\mapsto x,\quad b\mapsto y,\quad c\mapsto t.
\]
Then $P$ is fine, saturated, sharp, with torsion-free groupification.
The ordinary blow-up $\mu$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:charts}, equipped
with its displayed chart log structures, is the fs log blow-up of
$J=(a,c)\subset P$.  The induced morphism
\[
 p:E^{\log}\longrightarrow Y^{\log}
\]
on the complete inverse-image support is again that log blow-up after
strict base change.  The logarithmic structures here are induced from
the models, rather than the trivial structures on their underlying schemes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Represent $a=(1,0)$, $c=(0,1)$ and $b=(-1,2)$ in $\mathbf Z^2$.  Then
\[
 P=\{(m,n)\in\mathbf Z^2:n\geq0,\ 2m+n\geq0\}.
\]
Indeed for $m\geq0$ write $(m,n)=ma+nc$; for $m<0$ write it as
$(-m)b+(n+2m)c$.  This proves saturation as well as the asserted
presentation and groupification.

The log blow-up chart where $a$ generates the ideal has monoid
$P+\mathbf N(c-a)=\mathbf Na+\mathbf N(c-a)$, with
$c=a+(c-a)$ and $b=a+2(c-a)$.  It is already saturated and free.
The other chart has monoid
$P+\mathbf N(a-c)=\mathbf N(a-c)+\mathbf Nb$, where
$c=(a-c)+b$ and $a=2(a-c)+b$.  Both changes of lattice basis have
unimodular determinant.  On imposing $c\mapsto t$ their rings are
exactly $xv=t$ and $uy=t$.  Thus the saturation in the definition of
fs log blow-up introduces no further modification.  The chart
construction and universal property are those of
\cite[\S6.1]{IllusieOverview}.

Give $Y$ the pulled-back log structure, so $Y^{\log}\to X^{\log}$ is
strict.  Its fs fibre product with $X'$ has the ordinary underlying
fibre product and the induced log structure.  Proposition~\ref{prop:charts}
identifies the underlying scheme with the full reduced $E$.
The universal property of invertibility of the pulled-back ideal
shows that this base change is the same log blow-up on $Y^{\log}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A finite-coefficient logarithmic Morita comparison]
\label{thm:log-refinement}
Let $\Lambda_r=\mathbf Z/\ell^r$.  For either $f=\mu$ or $f=p$, use
the actual Kummer-etale topoi of its source and target.  Then
\[
 F\longrightarrow Rf_*f^*F
\]
is an equivalence for every $F\in D^+(-_{\mathrm{ket}},\Lambda_r)$.
Consequently $f^*$ is fully faithful on this category.  In particular,
putting
\[
 \mathsf T_r(Z)=\thick_{D(Z_{\mathrm{ket}},\Lambda_r)}(\Lambda_r),
\]
actual pullback induces a Morita equivalence
$\mathsf T_r(Z)\simeq\mathsf T_r(Z')$ and identifies the derived
endomorphism algebras of their units by the actual pullback map.
For $r=1$, $\mathsf T_1(Z)$ is also the geometrically described category
of bounded complexes with finite unipotent locally constant cohomology
on the indicated Kummer-etale topos.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:log-blowup} verifies the fs log blow-up hypothesis.
The ideal is coherent and nonempty at every geometric stalk; where a
generator becomes a unit the blow-up is simply the identity.  The
Fujiwara--Kato acyclicity theorem in
\cite[Theorem 6.2]{IllusieOverview} therefore applies, and states
precisely that the displayed adjunction unit is invertible for these
bounded-below torsion complexes.  It is stronger than an equality of
global cohomology groups.  The unit belongs to the actual derived
adjunction on complexes of sheaves; its being a quasi-isomorphism
makes it an equivalence in the stable localization as well.  Adjunction
now gives, on the specified objects,
\[
 \operatorname{Map}(f^*F,f^*G)
 \simeq\operatorname{Map}(F,Rf_*f^*G)
 \simeq\operatorname{Map}(F,G).
\]

Pullback is exact, sends the unit to the unit, and preserves retracts.
Its fully faithful image on the thick unit subcategory is closed under
finite cones and retracts and contains the target unit; hence it is
exactly the target thick unit subcategory.  The induced algebra map is
an equivalence since it is this same comparison on the generator.
For $r=1$, finite locally constant sheaves are finite-dimensional
representations of the topos fundamental group on each connected
component.  The filtration, kernel/cokernel and Postnikov argument of
Proposition~\ref{prop:unit-generation} applies verbatim to unipotent
locally constant $\mathbf F_\ell$-sheaves.  This gives the geometric
description independently of the thick-subcategory notation.
For $r>1$ we make no such assertion for every finite module: the theorem
uses the explicitly selected finite-cell subcategory $\mathsf T_r$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[What coherence follows at this stage]
\label{cor:log-coherence}
These comparisons commute with derived coefficient reduction on
$\mathsf T_r$, preserve tensor products and duals of these finite-cell
objects, and are compatible with compositions of actual log blow-ups.
Precisely, put
\[
 \mathsf T_{\mathrm{sys}}(Z)=
 \varprojlim_{r,\,-\otimes^L_{\Lambda_{r+1}}\Lambda_r}\mathsf T_r(Z).
\]
This is a limit of stable categories.  An object has specified reduction
equivalences and all their composition cells; no uniform amplitude is
implicit.  Pullback induces an equivalence of these system categories.
This is not an assertion about the ordinary inverse-limit sheaf.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Inverse image on module sheaves is exact and strong monoidal; its
derived extension-of-scalars comparison is the usual associativity of
tensor.  Reduction takes the unit to the unit and therefore preserves
the thick subcategories.  Their objects are dualizable, being built
from the unit by finite cones and retracts, so the same inverse image
preserves their duals and evaluation maps.  For $g$ followed by $f$,
the unit of the composite adjunction is the composite of their units,
with the canonical composition cell for inverse image.  This proves
compatibility of the specified equivalences, not merely the existence
of some equivalence for each modification.  For the limit statement, the transition is derived scalar extension;
it preserves the unit, finite cones and retracts, hence $\mathsf T_r$.
For a chain of coefficient maps its comparison with pullback is the
associativity map of the same derived tensor product.  The unit and
associativity identities for tensor define a natural transformation of
the entire diagrams of categories.  Its components are equivalences
by Theorem~\ref{thm:log-refinement}; taking their categorical limit is
therefore an equivalence.  This proof does not use a sheafwise limit
or assert coefficient base change for arbitrary direct images.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The ordinary direct-image shadow]
\label{cor:log-ordinary-shadow}
Write $\varepsilon_Z:Z_{\mathrm{ket}}\to\underline Z_{\acute et}$
for the morphism forgetting the logarithmic site and $\underline f$
for the underlying scheme map.  The actual log-refinement unit induces
\[
 R\varepsilon_{Z,*}F\xrightarrow{\sim}
 R\underline f_*R\varepsilon_{Z',*}f^*F.
\]
For $f=p$ the right side is the direct image from the complete exceptional
curve, with its logarithmic coefficient realization retained.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The natural square of forgetful and direct-image morphisms gives
$\varepsilon_Z\circ f=\underline f\circ\varepsilon_{Z'}$.
Apply $R\varepsilon_{Z,*}$ to the actual unit of
Theorem~\ref{thm:log-refinement} and use composition of derived right
adjoints.  This identifies the displayed map and its inverse, naturally
in $F$.  No non-strict Kummer-etale proper base-change assertion is
needed for this argument.
\end{proof}
This shadow is not a full-faithfulness assertion for the ordinary
realization, and it is not a comparison of ordinary exceptional
restrictions.  Nor has $R\varepsilon_*$ here been identified with the
neutralized relative nearby functor.

This is a genuine comparison for a nontrivial model modification, using
an existing geometric theorem.  Its scope is crucial.  On $Y^{\log}$
the characteristic group is $P^{\rm gp}$ of rank two, and the base
class $c$ is still retained.  Although $P^{\rm gp}/\mathbf Zc$ has
rank one, passing to a neutralized relative log fibre and identifying
its coefficient and support realizations is an additional step.
Also $E^{\log}$ is not ordinary $E_{\acute et}$: forgetting logarithmic
inertia can destroy full faithfulness.  No ordinary cellular Morita
invariance, exceptional functor, Verdier-support comparison or
common-refinement independence is obtained merely by erasing these
qualifications.  In particular tensor duality here is not a proof of
Verdier duality with geometric supports.

\section{Geometric coefficients and their generators}
\subsection{The ambient category and geometric input}
Put $Q=\mathbf Q_\ell$.  For the finite-type $k$-schemes below we use the
stable category of constructible adic complexes, with rationalized
coefficients, and its bounded subcategory $D_c^b(-,Q)$.  Mapping objects,
fibres and adjunctions are taken in this enhancement, not in its homotopy
category alone.  The scheme case of adic six operations and its enhanced
version are the external foundations \cite{LOAdic,LZEnhanced}.
We use their localization, projection and Verdier duality maps.  These
are not assertions about an analytic specialization morphism or an
infinite-root exceptional functor.

The additional geometric inputs are the Kummer computation for
$\mathbf G_m\hookrightarrow\mathbf A^1$ and codimension-one purity,
recorded in \cite[\S6.4, Theorems 7.1--7.2]{IllusieOverview}.  They are
stated there with invertible finite coefficients.  In the uses below
the constant computations have finite free cohomology in degrees zero
and one (purity in degree two), with the usual compatible reductions;
taking the adic system and then rationalizing gives the stated $Q$
computations.  No general interchange of adic limits with arbitrary
sheaf cohomology is being assumed.

\paragraph{External hypotheses and coefficient passage.}
The base trait is complete noetherian and hence excellent; all model
schemes used here are separated of finite type over it, and all
support schemes are of finite type over the algebraically closed
residue field.  The integer $\ell$ is invertible throughout.
Fujiwara--Kato is used only on the Kummer-etale site, with a coherent
nonempty log ideal on an fs locally noetherian scheme and
$D^+(-,\Lambda_r)$ coefficients, as checked in
Theorem~\ref{thm:log-refinement}.  No ordinary full faithfulness follows
from that theorem.  Nakayama's constant comparison is used for the
vertical fs log smooth charts verified in
Lemma~\ref{lem:constant-nearby} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:all-thickness-nearby}, first with $\Lambda_r$.
Geometric nearby cycles are taken after geometric generic base change;
forgetting their Galois action is not taking inertia invariants.

Ordinary proper base change is used only for the proper scheme maps
and their cartesian support squares, on bounded constructible
complexes.  Proper nearby comparison uses the same proper model maps.
Their exceptional comparisons are Verdier mates, with dualizing
complexes relative to the geometric points.  Gabber's theorem is
used in the geometric form recorded in
\cite[Corollary 3.2, Remark 3.3]{LuZhengNearby}; the finite-level
constant source is bounded constructible of finite Tor dimension,
a subclass of the stated bounded-above constructible domain.

For passing these constant comparisons to $Q$, form the systems of
actual $\Lambda_r$ complexes and maps in the constructible adic
formalism of \cite{LOAdic,LZEnhanced}.  The Kummer and purity
calculations have compatible derived reductions and uniformly bounded
finite free stalk cohomology.  Their supported constant partners are
obtained by the finite-level duality maps; finite constructible Tor
amplitude is preserved in this use.  The resulting adic systems have
conservative coefficient reductions, so the levelwise equivalences
are adic equivalences; rationalization gives the $Q$ comparisons.
This does not mean an unrestricted sheafwise inverse limit commutes
with every six operation.  For other objects of the rational
unipotent source, the comparison maps are already defined in that
formalism.  Their functors are exact (contravariantly for duality),
and the locus where a given map is an equivalence is closed under
finite cones and retracts.  The proved generation by $Q$, using
finite filtrations and bounded Postnikov towers below, is precisely
the extension argument.  It neither presupposes integral generation
of every rational coefficient nor proves an integral cellular theorem.

\begin{lemma}[The coefficient systems used in the comparisons]
\label{lem:selected-adic-comparisons}
For the constant coefficients on the gap models and their finite
stratified supports, the nearby, branch and proper comparison maps
used below define maps of constructible adic systems.  Their algebra
and module structures are those of these systems before rationalization.
Reduction is conservative on the systems, on their commutative algebras,
and on their module objects.  The constant global cochain algebras on
the allowed supports also have their specified derived reductions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the enhanced adic category
\[
 \mathcal D_a(Z)=\varprojlim_r\mathcal D(Z,\Lambda_r),\qquad
 \Lambda_r=\mathbf Z/\ell^r,
\]
with derived extension of scalars as transition, and then its bounded
constructible part.  This is the construction of
\cite[\S1.2, (1.1)--(1.7), Proposition 1.2.1]{LZAdic}; comparison with
adic complexes is \cite[Theorem 1.3.6]{LZAdic}.  The tensor structure
and inverse image are levelwise, as is shriek direct image on the
stated finite-type, invertible-coefficient scheme domain.  Right
adjoints are the adic right adjoints, not arbitrary sheafwise limits.
In particular proper direct image is levelwise because it equals
shriek direct image.  Our schemes are noetherian, finite dimensional
and separated of finite type over the excellent trait or its geometric
points, and $\ell$ is invertible; these are within that scheme domain.

For clarity put $E_Z=-\otimes^L_{\Lambda_{r+1}}\Lambda_r$.
In the geometric generic/special diagram defining nearby cycles,
write $j$ for the generic map and $i$ for special inverse image.
The coefficient comparison is the mate
\[
 c_r:E_XRj_{*,r+1}\longrightarrow Rj_{*,r}E_\eta
\]
of the composite
\[
 j_r^*E_XRj_{*,r+1}\simeq E_\eta j_{r+1}^*Rj_{*,r+1}
       \xrightarrow{E_\eta\epsilon}E_\eta.
\]
Applying $i^*$ and its scalar-extension identification gives
\[
 \rho_r:E_{X_s}R\Psi\Lambda_{r+1}\longrightarrow R\Psi\Lambda_r.
\]
All mates are taken in the same coefficient-parameterized enhanced
categories.  Tensoring the counits gives the counit for each finite
arity, so $c_r$ and $\rho_r$ are lax symmetric-monoidal transformations,
with substitution and composition cells inherited from those counits.
At the unit they are commutative-algebra maps; on coefficients they
are module maps.  If $a:N\to Rj_{U,*}j_U^*N$ is an open unit, its
naturality reads
\[
 a_r\rho_r=(Rj_{U,*,r}j_U^*\rho_r)\,c_{U,r}\,E(a_{r+1}),
\]
where the inverse-image/scalar identifications are understood.
For proper comparison $\theta:N_I\to Ru_*N_J$, the corresponding
identity is
$\theta_r\rho_{I,r}=(Ru_*\rho_{J,r})c_{u,r}E(\theta_{r+1})$.
Both identities are pastings of the same unit/counit squares.
For a supported evaluation $N^\vee\otimes W\to\omega(-1)[-2]$,
the coefficient square is
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
 E(N^\vee_{r+1}\otimes W_{r+1})&\longrightarrow&E\omega_{r+1}(-1)[-2]\\
 \downarrow&&\downarrow\\
 N^\vee_r\otimes W_r&\longrightarrow&\omega_r(-1)[-2].
\end{array}
\]
Here $N^\vee_r$ denotes the nearby coefficient dual input in the
pairing, not an assertion that all nearby sheaves are tensor dualizable.
The left arrow is the two coefficient comparisons and the right arrow
is dualizing coefficient change.  Commutativity follows from evaluation
and trace naturality.  Their equivalence on the selected constants is
checked below; it is not required to construct this square.

At precision $r$ form $R\Psi\Lambda_r$, its actual open-unit maps,
Kummer morphisms, cup products, and proper comparison.  A coefficient
map gives the natural transformation obtained by scalar extension and
the same adjunction units.  Successive coefficient maps compose by
derived tensor associativity, so these maps define a section, rather
than a list of unrelated objects.  Its cartesianness is a separate
check: on a smooth stratum it is the constant reduction map; at a
node and a branch link it is the reduction of the unit and primitive
Kummer class in Proposition~\ref{prop:all-thickness-nearby}.  The
underlying complexes there have finite free cohomology in $[0,1]$,
so derived reduction has these same reduced generators and the
specified comparison is an equivalence.  The finitely many strata
are conservative on constructible complexes.  The earlier thickness-
one and thickness-two uses follow from Lemma~\ref{lem:constant-nearby};
neither local calculation requires this adic lemma.

The algebra maps are defined by the finite-level cup product and unit,
not lifted from cohomology.  Their coefficient squares are the cup-
product naturality squares.  Limits of commutative algebra objects
and of algebra/module pairs are created on their underlying diagrams.
Thus the resulting cartesian section is an algebra, with its modules,
in $\mathcal D_a$.  A map between such objects is an equivalence
exactly when all its underlying coefficient evaluations are; no
faithfulness assertion about cohomology as an algebra is used.

For the global cochain assertion, ordinary recollement expresses
$R\Gamma(S,H_S)$ as a finite homotopy pullback of the open-stratum
cochains, node cochains and branch links.  This statement applies
at finite precision before any Morita reconstruction.  The open
terms are the actual cochains of $\mathbf G_m$, whose Kummer
calculation has compatible free reduction in degrees zero and one;
the other terms were just checked.  Derived scalar extension preserves
finite homotopy limits in a stable category.  Applying it to this
specified pullback, including its attaching arrows, proves the
global reduction assertion.  This is not inferred by commuting
stalks with $R\Gamma$ or with an inverse limit.

For supported constants, form the finite-level Gabber evaluation
maps and then exceptional restriction.  Their reduction compatibility
can be checked by localization: smooth pieces have the purity factor
$(1)[2]$, and closed pieces are the duals of the bounded free link
fibres.  Finite duals, these localization triangles and their
coefficient maps commute with reduction.  Thus the supported systems
and the comparison of their pairings are cartesian as well.  Proper
support comparisons then use the levelwise proper direct image
above.  This only proves the claim for the constructed constants
and the finite operations on them.

Rationalize these systems.  Other geometric rational unipotent
coefficients are covered by Proposition~\ref{prop:unit-generation}:
the already defined exact natural transformations extend equivalences
from the unit to finite cones and retracts.  Multilinear comparisons
are extended one variable at a time.  No integral lattice for every
rational object, or general integral cellular equivalence, is inferred.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Geometric unipotent coefficients]
For a connected smooth stratum $Z$, a finite-rank lisse $Q$-sheaf is
\emph{unipotent} if it has a finite filtration by lisse subsheaves with
constant successive quotients.  Let $\mathcal U^b(Z)$ be the full
subcategory of $D_c^b(Z,Q)$ whose cohomology sheaves have this property.
For a geometric point put $\mathcal U^b(Z)=\Perf(Q)$.
\end{definition}
This is an absolute geometric starting domain.  For a relative version,
one must specify a base category and replace constant quotients by its
actual pullbacks.  The relative claim is not proved by the results below.

\begin{lemma}[The one-object Morita argument]\label{lem:morita}
Let $\mathcal D$ be an idempotent-complete stable linear category, with
object $G$ and algebra $A=\RHom(G,G)$.  The functor $\RHom(G,-)$ identifies
$\thick(G)$ with $\Perf(A)$, using right $A$-modules by precomposition.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On $G$ the comparison of mapping objects is the regular-module Yoneda
identification.  Exactness, finite sums and retracts extend it in each
variable to $\thick(G)$, proving full faithfulness.  The image contains
$A$ and is closed under finite cones, shifts and retracts; these generate
exactly $\Perf(A)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Geometric unit generation]\label{prop:unit-generation}
For the connected smooth strata used here,
\[
 \mathcal U^b(Z)=\thick_{D_c^b(Z,Q)}(Q_Z)
 \simeq \Perf(A_Z),\qquad A_Z=R\Gamma(Z,Q).
\]
This category is stable, idempotent-complete, and closed under tensor
products and lisse duals.  Pullback between the specified smooth strata
preserves it.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Lisse sheaves correspond to finite-dimensional continuous representations.
Kernels and cokernels of maps between unipotent representations are
unipotent: intersect a unipotent filtration with an invariant subspace
and take its images in the quotient.  Extensions are unipotent by
concatenating filtrations.  The corresponding lisse sheaves have the
same properties.  The long exact cohomology sequence of a cone therefore
shows that $\mathcal U^b(Z)$ is stable; an idempotent summand has cohomology
an idempotent summand of the original representation and remains in the
category.  Thus $\thick(Q_Z)\subseteq\mathcal U^b(Z)$.

Conversely a unipotent sheaf is built from constant finite-dimensional
sheaves by its actual short exact sequences and their extension
triangles.  A bounded complex with these cohomology sheaves is then
built by its finite Postnikov tower.  This proves the reverse inclusion
without defining the category by generation.  Tensor filtrations and
the reversed dual filtration prove the tensor and dual assertions on
sheaves; finite truncations prove them on bounded complexes.  Pullback
preserves the lisse filtration.  Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:morita} for the
Morita equivalence.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
The argument concerns kernels of maps \emph{between lisse sheaves}.
Lisse sheaves are not a Serre subcategory of all constructible sheaves:
$j_!Q_U\subset Q_Z$ for a proper dense open $U$ need not be lisse.
In particular unit generation on a stratum does not prove closure
under supported extension into the whole curve.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Keeping finite descent}
\begin{proposition}[A generator with finite geometric descent]
\label{prop:finite-descent}
Let a finite group $\Delta$ act on a specified scheme with invariant
stratification, and let $\mathcal C$ be one of the geometrically defined
categories below, invariant under pullback.  If $\mathcal C=\thick(G)$,
then its category $\mathcal C^{h\Delta}$ of actual equivariant objects
is generated by $G_\Delta=\operatorname{Ind}_\Delta(G)$ and hence is
Morita equivalent to $\Perf(\RHom_{\mathcal C^{h\Delta}}
(G_\Delta,G_\Delta))$.  The underlying object of $G_\Delta$ is
$\bigoplus_{\delta\in\Delta}\delta^*G$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the derived category of sheaves with their geometric descent data;
finite induction and coinduction agree, as finite sums and products
agree.  For an equivariant object $F$, the counit
$\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{For}(F)\to F$ has an equivariant
section: sum the inverse descent isomorphisms into its components and
divide by $|\Delta|$.  Their composite is the identity, including the
coherent descent structure.  Since $Q$ has characteristic zero this is
valid even if $\ell$ divides $|\Delta|$.
The underlying object of $F$ lies in $\thick(G)$; induction is exact and
preserves retracts, so $\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{For}(F)$ lies
in $\thick(G_\Delta)$.  Its retract $F$ does too.  The reverse inclusion
follows from the geometric category's stability.  Lemma~\ref{lem:morita}
finishes the proof.  Equivalently these constructions take place in the
homotopy-coherent descent category, where the finite induction monad
is the same geometric descent monad.
\end{proof}
For the quotient stack this is its actual equivariant coefficient
category.  The proposition retains representations that would be lost
by using only the equivariant trivial object.  It does not prove integral
averaging or descent between different refinements.

\section{The link of an actual normal pair}
Let $C$ be either $\mathbf A^1_k$ with $Z=\{0\}$, or
$E=\mathbf P^1_k$ with $Z=\{0,\infty\}$.  In both cases put
$U=C\setminus Z\simeq\mathbf G_m$, with immersions $j:U\to C$ and
$i:Z\to C$.  Define the \emph{actual link functor}
\[
 L=i^*Rj_*:\mathcal U^b(U)\longrightarrow D(Z,Q),
 \qquad L_a=i_a^*Rj_* \quad (a\in Z).
\]
Its definition uses a geometric direct image; it is not an exterior
insertion operator.

\begin{proposition}[The local Kummer link]\label{prop:link}
For every endpoint $a$, $B_a=L_a(Q_U)$ is perfect over $Q$, with
$H^0(B_a)=Q$ and $H^1(B_a)=Q(-1)$ and no other cohomology.  The actual
restriction map
\[
 \kappa_a:A_U=R\Gamma(U,Q)\longrightarrow B_a
\]
is an equivalence of cochain algebras.  For every $V\in\mathcal U^b(U)$
its coefficient version $R\Gamma(U,V)\to L_a(V)$ is an equivalence.
In particular $L$ takes values in $\Perf(Q)^Z$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The geometric stalk of $Rj_*$ is computed on the punctured strict
henselization at $a$.  The Kummer computation cited above gives the
asserted local cohomology and the global cohomology of $\mathbf G_m$.
The actual restriction is the identity in degree zero.  In degree one,
the Kummer class of the coordinate $z$ has residue $1$ at zero and
$-1$ at infinity, where the local coordinate is $z^{-1}$.  Consequently
the same restriction map is an isomorphism in every degree.  It is a
map of cochain algebras because it is induced by restriction on the
sites, so the cohomology calculation proves the asserted equivalence;
no formality of either algebra is required.

The coefficient restriction is a natural transformation of exact
functors, constructed before comparing their values.  Its equivalence
locus is closed under finite cones and retracts and contains $Q_U$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:unit-generation} therefore proves the assertion
for all $V$.  This also proves perfectness and boundedness of the actual
link on the whole chosen category.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A geometric supported residue]\label{prop:residue}
For $(\mathbf A^1,\{0\})$, the localization boundary
\[
 H^1(\mathbf G_m,Q)\longrightarrow H^2_{\{0\}}(\mathbf A^1,Q)
\]
is nonzero and is an isomorphism.  With the purity normalization by the
effective divisor $\{0\}$ it sends the Kummer coordinate class to its
cycle class.  For an arbitrary unipotent lisse coefficient $V$ on $U$,
localization also gives the canonical equivalence
\[
 i^!j_!V\simeq L(V)[-1].
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Homotopy invariance gives $H^{>0}(\mathbf A^1,Q)=0$; purity gives
$i^!Q_{\mathbf A^1}=Q(-1)[-2]$.  The localization long exact sequence
therefore gives the first isomorphism.  Kummer boundary and the cycle
class of a parameter give the stated normalization, as in the cited
codimension-one computation.

For the second assertion use the actual triangle
$j_!V\to Rj_*V\to i_*L(V)\to$.  The object $i^!Rj_*V$ is zero:
for every $W$ its maps from $W$ are maps $i_*W\to Rj_*V$, equivalently
maps $j^*i_*W\to V$, which vanish.  Applying $i^!$ to this triangle
identifies its connecting map $L(V)\to i^!j_!V[1]$ as an equivalence.
This determines both the supported object and its attaching map.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A coefficient with nontrivial monodromy]
\label{ex:jordan}
Fix compatible Kummer roots of $z$ and a generator of the relative
$\mathbf Z_\ell(1)$.  Let $V$ be the actual rank-two local system from
the continuous Kummer representation
\[
 T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
 \quad\hbox{on }Qe_1\oplus Qe_2.
\]
The integral lattice is stable, and its reductions factor through finite
Kummer covers, so this specifies a geometric adic local system.  The
punctured-trait link is continuous inertia cohomology, computed by
\[
 L_0(V)\simeq [\,V\xrightarrow{T-1}V\,]
 \quad\hbox{in degrees }0,1,
\]
after the generator choice trivializes the Tate line.  This is the
procyclic resolution: the augmentation ideal of
$\mathbf Z_\ell[[\mathbf Z_\ell]]$ is generated by the chosen
$\gamma-1$, with the two-term free resolution of the trivial module.
For this tame coefficient wild inertia contributes no higher
$\ell$-cohomology; the prime-to-$\ell$ part is exact and acts trivially.
The Kummer punctured-trait description is that of
\cite[\S4.7]{IllusieOverview}.
Thus $H^0(L_0(V))=Qe_1$ and
$H^1(L_0(V))=(V/(T-1)V)(-1)$, a one-dimensional space generated by the
class of $e_2$ with the Tate line restored.  At infinity the action is
$T^{-1}$, not a second independently chosen monodromy.
Proposition~\ref{prop:residue} gives
\[
 H^1(i_0^!j_!V)=Qe_1,\qquad
 H^2(i_0^!j_!V)=(V/(T-1)V)(-1).
\]
In particular the actual supported boundary remains nonzero for a
nonconstant coefficient.  The complex has been obtained from the
geometric link and its localization map, not assigned as its definition.
\end{example}

\section{Constructible reconstruction and operation closure}
\begin{definition}[The stratified source]
Let $\mathcal C(C,Z)$ be the full subcategory of $D_c^b(C,Q)$ consisting
of $F$ for which $j^*F\in\mathcal U^b(U)$ and
$i^*F\in\Perf(Q)^Z$.  These are conditions on restrictions of actual
constructible complexes.  No closure under exceptional operations is
included in the definition.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Actual gluing and its Morita presentation]
\label{thm:gluing}
There is an equivalence of stable categories between $\mathcal C(C,Z)$
and triples
\[
 (V,W,\alpha),\qquad V\in\mathcal U^b(U),\quad W\in\Perf(Q)^Z,
 \quad\alpha:W\longrightarrow L(V).
\]
The arrow and the compatibility of morphisms are homotopy coherent.
Under this equivalence,
\[
 i^!F=\operatorname{fib}(W\xrightarrow{\alpha}L(V)).
\]
The category is generated by the actual constructible object
\[
 G_C=j_!Q_U\oplus\bigoplus_{a\in Z}i_{a,*}Q,
\]
and is Morita equivalent to $\Perf(A_C)$ with
$A_C=\RHom_C(G_C,G_C)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $F$ set $V=j^*F$, $W=i^*F$, and let $\alpha$ be the restriction
of the adjunction unit $F\to Rj_*j^*F$.  Conversely form in the ambient
stable sheaf category
\[
 F(V,W,\alpha)=Rj_*V\mathop{\times}^{h}_{i_*L(V)}i_*W.
\]
Its two restrictions are $V$ and $W$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:link}
and the localization triangle for $Rj_*V$ show that it is bounded and
constructible in the stated source.  The canonical comparison from an
original $F$ to this pullback is an equivalence after $j^*$ and $i^*$;
these functors are jointly conservative by localization.  This proves
reconstruction, naturally on objects and morphisms.  Explicitly the
mapping spectrum between two triples is the fibre of
\[
 \operatorname{Map}_U(V,V')\oplus\operatorname{Map}_Z(W,W')
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Map}_Z(W,L(V')),
 \qquad (f,g)\longmapsto L(f)\alpha-\alpha'g.
\]
Thus the comparison retains the required homotopies, not just commuting
squares in a triangulated category.  Applying $i^*$ to
$i_*i^!F\to F\to Rj_*V$ gives the stated fibre formula.

The geometric definition is stable and closed under retracts by its
restriction conditions and Proposition~\ref{prop:unit-generation}.
The triangle $j_!V\to F\to i_*W$ and that proposition show that every
$F$ is in $\thick(G_C)$.  Each summand of $G_C$ satisfies the defining
geometric conditions, proving the reverse inclusion.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:morita}.
\end{proof}

The full derived endomorphism algebra has the following blocks, including
the nonzero off-diagonal ones:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \RHom(j_!Q,j_!Q)&=A_U, &
 \RHom(j_!Q,i_{a,*}Q)&=0,\\
 \RHom(i_{a,*}Q,j_!Q)&=B_a[-1], &
 \RHom(i_{a,*}Q,i_{b,*}Q)&=
 \begin{cases}Q&a=b,\\0&a\ne b.\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\]
The first two formulas use $j_!\dashv j^*$; the third uses
Proposition~\ref{prop:residue}; disjoint closed supports give the last.
The $A_U$-action on $B_a$ is induced by the actual link functor and
$\kappa_a$.  Thus these are derived blocks with geometric composition,
not merely a matrix of graded cohomology groups.

In the right-module Morita convention the link is
\[
 M\longmapsto M\otimes^L_{A_U}B_a,
 \qquad M=\RHom_U(Q_U,V),
\]
where $B_a$ has its left $A_U$-action induced by $L_a$ on endomorphisms.
The natural evaluation comparison is an equivalence on $Q_U$, hence
on its thick closure.  Together with the mapping formula above this
identifies the coefficient-valued attaching functor itself.

\begin{proposition}[The supported operations really preserve the source]
\label{prop:operations}
For these pairs, $j^*,i^*,i^!,j_!,Rj_*,i_*$ preserve the corresponding
categories $\mathcal C(C,Z)$, $\mathcal U^b(U)$ and $\Perf(Q)^Z$.
The global source is closed under tensor products and Verdier duality.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The restriction assertions follow from the geometric conditions.
For $j_!V$, $i_*W$ and $Rj_*V$ the triples of
Theorem~\ref{thm:gluing} are respectively
\[
 (V,0,0),\qquad (0,W,0),\qquad (V,L(V),\mathrm{id}).
\]
Their membership follows from the proved perfectness of $L(V)$.
The formula for $i^!$ is a fibre of perfect complexes.  Tensor products
are checked on the two strata, where Proposition~\ref{prop:unit-generation}
and ordinary finite-dimensional tensor products apply.

On $U$ smooth-curve duality is
$D_U V=V^\vee(1)[2]$, which preserves $\mathcal U^b(U)$; on $Z$ it is
ordinary $Q$-duality.  The actual identities
$D_Cj_!=Rj_*D_U$ and $D_Ci_*=i_*D_Z$ put the duals of all summands of
$G_C$ in $\mathcal C(C,Z)$.  Exactness, retracts and the proved generation
show that $D_C$ preserves the whole category.  Constructible Verdier
biduality proves that it is an anti-equivalence.  Thus support closure
and duality are consequences, not defining assumptions.
\end{proof}
Every construction is equivariant for geometric automorphisms of the
pair, including permutation of endpoints.  Consequently these closure
statements also hold with finite geometric descent, using
Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-descent}.  This statement concerns a fixed
pair with its actual action, not arbitrary comparison of atlases.

\subsection{Duality of the whole attaching datum}
\begin{proposition}[Duality on reconstructed triples]\label{prop:dual-triple}
For a triple $(V,W,\alpha)$ put $C_\alpha=\operatorname{fib}(\alpha)$.
Its Verdier dual has triple
\[
 \bigl(D_UV,D_Z C_\alpha,\alpha^D\bigr),
 \qquad
 \alpha^D:D_Z C_\alpha\longrightarrow D_ZL(V)[1]
                      \simeq L(D_UV).
\]
The first arrow is the boundary of the dual of
$C_\alpha\to W\to L(V)$.  The equivalence on the right is canonical.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The restrictions of $D_CF$ are $D_Uj^*F$ and $D_Zi^!F$, giving the two
stated objects.  Moreover actual Verdier exchange and localization give
\[
 D_ZL(V)=D_Zi^*Rj_*V
 \simeq i^!j_!D_UV\simeq L(D_UV)[-1].
\]
Dualize the actual localization triangle of $F$.  Its restriction to
$Z$ identifies the resulting unit with the displayed boundary, which
therefore is the attaching map of $D_CF$.  Biduality and the unit/counit
identities give the coherence of this operation on morphisms.
\end{proof}

For $F=Q_{\mathbf A^1}$ the triple has $V=Q_U$, $W=Q$ and
$\alpha:Q\to B_0$ the actual degree-zero unit.  Its fibre is
$Q(-1)[-2]$, recovering purity; setting $\alpha=0$ would give a
different costalk.  This is a direct check that attaching data cannot
be dropped from the comparison.

On the open stratum the cochain algebra and its supported partner are
\[
 A_U=R\Gamma(U,Q),\qquad
 P_U=R\Gamma_c(U,Q)\simeq D_Q A_U(-1)[-2].
\]
For a unipotent lisse $V$, geometric smooth-curve duality gives
\[
 R\Gamma_c(U,V)\simeq D_Q R\Gamma(U,V^\vee)(-1)[-2].
\]
The comparison uses the actual evaluation and compact-support trace.
Cup-product associativity makes evaluation balanced for the two
$A_U$-actions; dualizing the regular actions gives the corresponding
base-linear dualizing bimodule.  The adjunction identities in
Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-triple} show how this partner enters the
localization maps.  These are statements about the algebraic pair;
identification with analytic and relative-root supported realizations
across the modification remains a separate geometric comparison.
In particular we assert neither $A_U\simeq P_U$ nor a Calabi--Yau
identification of the full algebra $A_C$ with its linear dual.

\subsection{Two intrinsically described support categories}
\begin{proposition}[The star and shriek pieces of the recollement]
\label{prop:clean-pieces}
Inside the proved ambient category define by geometric vanishing
\[
 \mathcal C_* =\{F:i^!F=0\},\qquad
 \mathcal C_! =\{F:i^*F=0\}.
\]
Then $Rj_*:\mathcal U^b(U)\simeq\mathcal C_*$ and
$j_!:\mathcal U^b(U)\simeq\mathcal C_!$ are equivalences, with inverse
$j^*$.  Verdier duality exchanges these categories.  The canonical map
$j_!V\to Rj_*V$ has cone $i_*L(V)$ and retains the nonzero link.
For $C=E$ proper, their integrated values are respectively
$R\Gamma(U,V)$ and $R\Gamma_c(U,V)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the reconstruction theorem $i^!F=0$ says exactly that
$\alpha:W\to L(V)$ is an equivalence, which identifies the triple
with $(V,L(V),\mathrm{id})$.  The condition $i^*F=0$ says $W=0$
and gives $(V,0,0)$.  These arguments give both essential surjectivity
and the functors on maps; alternatively full faithfulness is the
open-immersion adjunction.  The Verdier exchanges and the displayed
localization triangle were proved above.  Properness of $E$ and
composition of direct images give the integrated identities.
\end{proof}
These are intrinsic categories on a fixed algebraic pair, and each has
$K_0=\mathbf Z$.  They are useful candidates for the star and supported
pieces of a nearby realization.  Membership of the actual nearby image requires a separate geometric
calculation of its endpoint attaching maps.  This calculation is carried
out in Theorem~\ref{thm:actual-nearby-image}, and the actual supported
partner in Theorem~\ref{thm:actual-support-image}.  The present
proposition itself is not a definition of nearby cycles.

Nor is $\mathcal C_*$ a tensor subcategory for the ordinary ambient
tensor product.  For $F=Rj_*Q_U$, its endpoint stalk is $B_a$.  The
attaching map of $F\otimes F$ is, after the canonical identifications,
$B_a\otimes_Q B_a\to B_a$, the multiplication induced by restriction.
Its source has nonzero $H^2=Q(-2)$ whereas its target has zero $H^2$.
Thus it is not an equivalence and $F\otimes F\notin\mathcal C_*$.
If tensor operations on a star realization are represented using the
stratum tensor and $Rj_*$, their relation to geometric tensor operations
must be separately proved; the ambient tensor cannot be substituted.

\section{A minimal geometric kernel action}
Here take $C=E=\mathbf P^1_k$.  Let $\Delta:C\to C\times C$ be the
diagonal and $p_1,p_2$ the projections.  In the actual constructible
category on the product consider just the kernels
\[
 K_{\rm id}=\Delta_*Q_C,\qquad
 K_U=\Delta_*j_!Q_U,\qquad K_Z=\Delta_*i_*Q_Z.
\]
They are allowed to be constructible along the diagonal, rather than
lisse on the product.  Set
$\Phi_K(F)=Rp_{2,!}(p_1^*F\otimes^L K)$.

\begin{proposition}[Unit and normal-pair projectors]\label{prop:kernels}
These kernels act on $\mathcal C(C,Z)$ by
\[
 \Phi_{K_{\rm id}}=\mathrm{id},\qquad
 \Phi_{K_U}=j_!j^*,\qquad \Phi_{K_Z}=i_*i^*.
\]
Their kernel localization triangle induces the actual localization
triangle of these functors.  Under the Morita equivalence of
Theorem~\ref{thm:gluing} these actions are tensor with the bimodules
$\RHom(G_C,\Phi_K G_C)$; the unit is the regular bimodule $A_C$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a constructible $H$ on $C$, the diagonal projection formula gives
$p_1^*F\otimes\Delta_*H=\Delta_*(F\otimes H)$.  Since
$p_2\Delta=\mathrm{id}$ and $\Delta$ is proper,
$\Phi_{\Delta_*H}(F)=F\otimes H$ with its actual natural map.
Take $H=Q_C,j_!Q_U,i_*Q_Z$ and use the open and closed projection
formulas.  Proposition~\ref{prop:operations} proves preservation of
the specified source.  Applying the exact diagonal and kernel action
to $j_!Q_U\to Q_C\to i_*Q_Z$ proves the triangle assertion, including
its boundary map.

For any of these exact functors $T$, its action on endomorphisms gives
a left $A_C$-action on $\RHom(G_C,TG_C)$, commuting with the right action
by precomposition.  Evaluation constructs the natural tensor comparison;
it is an equivalence on the generator and hence on all perfect modules.
For $T=\mathrm{id}$ this is the regular bimodule and its identity action.
\end{proof}
This proves a minimal kernel interface on the fixed algebraic pair.
It does not prove compatibility of these kernels with refinement, nor
closure or associativity for all relative-product convolution kernels.

\section{Why the full exceptional category cannot be invariant}
\begin{proposition}[An obstruction to unrestricted refinement descent]
\label{prop:K0-obstruction}
For the exceptional curve with its three strata,
\[
 K_0\bigl(\mathcal C(\mathbf P^1,\{0,\infty\})\bigr)=\mathbf Z^3.
\]
The bounded unipotent local coefficient category of one connected
node, with constant simple coefficient, has $K_0=\mathbf Z$; in
particular this holds for unipotent continuous $\mathbf Z_\ell(1)$
representations in their geometric derived category.  These two
categories are not Morita equivalent.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $F$ take the Euler ranks of its restrictions to $U$, $0$, and
$\infty$.  They define a homomorphism to $\mathbf Z^3$.  The classes
of $j_!Q_U,i_{0,*}Q,i_{\infty,*}Q$ map to its standard basis.
Localization expresses $[F]$ as the sum of the open and closed
contributions.  On the open, finite unipotent filtrations and the
Postnikov tower express every class as its Euler rank times $[Q_U]$;
the point contributions have the same elementary property.  Thus the
three classes generate with no relations.  The identical filtration
argument at the old node, together with Euler rank one of its constant
coefficient, gives $\mathbf Z$.  Morita equivalence of these small
idempotent-complete stable categories would preserve $K_0$.
\end{proof}
Even proper integration forgets the problem: $R\Gamma(E,i_{0,*}Q)$ and
$R\Gamma(E,i_{\infty,*}Q)$ are both $Q$, but the mapping spectrum between
the two disjoint skyscrapers on $E$ is zero.  Integration cannot be
fully faithful on the constructed category.

Consequently $\mathcal C(C,Z)$ is a proved ambient recollement category,
not yet the desired refinement-invariant source.  Target~\ref{target:refinement}
must use a \emph{geometrically characterized nearby-admissible subdatum}
and prove its reconstruction.  Its characterization must retain the
actual specialization maps and the required extension data.  Defining
it to be the image of the old category would prove neither an intrinsic
criterion nor independence of the old model.

There is a further constraint: requiring that a refinement-invariant
full stable subcategory of $\mathcal C(E,Z)$ contain the constant object on all of $E$ and be closed
under the ambient projectors $j_!j^*$ and each $i_{a,*}i_a^*$ forces it
to contain all three generators above.  Thus the required supported
operations must be typed with their own support categories; they cannot
all be endofunctors of a single category with the old node's $K_0$.
This is an obstruction to that formulation, not to a correctly typed
multi-support Morita datum.  The formulation of support transport is
therefore part of the remaining theorem.

The costalks used in the recollement proof are those of the endpoints
inside $E$.  They are not the exceptional restriction along
$E\hookrightarrow X'_s$.  The following section constructs that
additional comparison from the actual nearby-duality theorem and
verifies admissibility by the actual Kummer attaching maps.

\section{Actual nearby coefficients on the exceptional curve}
Fix an algebraic closure $\bar K\subset\Omega$, and use geometric
nearby cycles, with the base Galois action forgotten in this section.
Identify $X'_{\bar\eta}=X_{\bar\eta}=\mathbf G_{m,\bar K}$ by $x$.
The source is the actual category
$\mathcal V=\mathcal U^b(\mathbf G_{m,\bar K})$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:unit-generation}.  Write
\[
 \kappa:E\hookrightarrow X'_s,\qquad
 N(F)=\kappa^*R\Psi_{X'}F,\qquad
 \mathcal S(F)=j^*N(F),\qquad
 \alpha(F):i^*N(F)\longrightarrow L(\mathcal S(F)).
\]
Here $\alpha$ is the restriction of the actual open-immersion unit
$N(F)\to Rj_*j^*N(F)$.  These functors and this map are defined before
any equivalence is asserted.  On $E^\circ$ use coordinate $u=x/t$;
thus $q_\infty$ is $u=0$ and $q_0$ is $v=u^{-1}=0$.

\begin{lemma}[The constant nearby calculation and its maps]
\label{lem:constant-nearby}
On the smooth locus of $X'_s$, $R\Psi_{X'}Q=Q$.  At a node with chart
$zw=t$, geometric nearby cohomology is $Q$ in degree zero and
$Q(-1)$ in degree one, generated by the Kummer class of $z$; there
is no other cohomology.  At the old point of $xy=t^2$, the same
statement holds with generator the Kummer class of $x$.
These identifications are compatible with Kummer classes of units on
smooth open neighbourhoods.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The chart for a new node is $\mathbf N\to\mathbf N^2$ with
$1\mapsto(1,1)$.  For the old node it is $\mathbf N\to P$ with
$1\mapsto c$.  Its group quotient is freely generated by $a$ modulo
$c$, and its underlying scheme is exactly the toric base change.
Thus the logarithmic chart criterion gives a vertical fs log smooth
morphism in both cases.  In particular the relation $a+b=2c$ does
not introduce torsion in $P^{\rm gp}/\mathbf Zc$.

Apply Nakayama's constant log-local-acyclicity theorem and the
classical comparison, in \cite[Theorem 8.3, Corollary 8.4]{IllusieOverview}.
The Kummer calculation of the resulting geometric log fibre gives
exterior powers of the relative rank-one characteristic group, with
its Tate twist (\cite[\S5]{IllusieOverview}).  The degree-one map is the actual
Kummer boundary of the indicated monomial.  At a smooth point the
relative group is zero and smooth local acyclicity gives the degree-zero
unit.  These assertions may first be made with $\mathbf Z/\ell^r$;
the displayed cohomology groups are free and compatible with reduction,
so the adic constant computation gives the asserted rational result.
The Kummer maps themselves are functorial, rather than chosen
isomorphisms of the resulting vector spaces.  For a unit $h$ on a
smooth neighbourhood its finite torsors $z^{\ell^r}=h$ are etale
there; smooth specialization identifies their Kummer boundary with
that of the specialized unit.  This verifies the compatibility used
below.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic admissibility of the actual nearby image]
\label{thm:actual-nearby-image}
The functor $\mathcal S$ is an equivalence
$\mathcal V\simeq\mathcal U^b(E^\circ)$, strong monoidal on this
source.  For every $F\in\mathcal V$ the actual attaching map $\alpha(F)$
is an equivalence, and the actual unit gives
\[
 N(F)\xrightarrow{\sim}Rj_*\mathcal S(F).
\]
Consequently $N$ identifies $\mathcal V$ with the intrinsically defined
category $\mathcal C_*=\{H\in\mathcal C(E,Z):i^!H=0\}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First take $F=Q$.  The smooth neighbourhood of $E^\circ$ is
$\Spec R[u,u^{-1}]$, so $\mathcal S(Q)=Q_{E^\circ}$ by the actual
smooth specialization unit.  At $q_0$, the source of $\alpha_a(Q)$
has its degree-one generator represented by $v=t/x$ on the geometric
generic fibre.  This class restricts, under the same specialization
construction, to the Kummer class of the unit $v$ on the punctured
strict neighbourhood of $q_0$ in $E$.  The latter generates $H^1(B_a)$.
At $q_\infty$ the identical argument uses $u=x/t$.

More explicitly, apply the exact nearby functor to the Kummer morphism
$Q\to Q(1)[1]$ on the generic fibre, and precompose with its constant
specialization unit.  Naturality of the open unit defining $\alpha$
identifies the image with the Kummer morphism on the smooth open
stratum.  The finite unit torsors there extend etale over
$R[u,u^{-1}]$ or $R[v,v^{-1}]$, so the last assertion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:constant-nearby} identifies this morphism before taking
cohomology.  The residue calculation of Proposition~\ref{prop:link}
then shows that its degree-one map is an isomorphism.  In the common
coordinate $x$, the signs are negative at $q_0$ and positive at
$q_\infty$: $[x]=-[v]$ and $[x]=[u]$ after the geometric base class
$[t]$ vanishes.  Degree zero is the identity by the same unit, and
there are no other degrees.  Thus $\alpha(Q)$ is an equivalence.

Now use the already defined natural transformation $N\to Rj_*\mathcal S$.
Its equivalence locus is thick.  Both its values and those of
$\mathcal S$ on $Q$ are bounded in the required categories by the
constant computation; exactness and retracts give the same property
on $\mathcal V=\thick(Q)$.  This proves the comparison for every
coefficient without assuming a lisse comparison theorem for those
coefficients.  Membership in $\mathcal C_*$ follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:gluing}.

It remains to check that $\mathcal S$ is an equivalence, rather than
just a functor into that stratum category.  Its map on the endomorphism
algebra of $Q$ is the actual specialization map
\[
 R\Gamma(\mathbf G_{m,\bar K},Q)\longrightarrow
 R\Gamma(E^\circ,Q).
\]
It is the identity in degree zero and sends the Kummer class of $x$
to that of $u$.  Indeed $x=tu$ and compatible roots of $t$ in $\bar K$
identify the generic torsors with those of $u$; the latter extend over
the smooth neighbourhood.  Both algebras have cohomology only in
degrees zero and one, so this actual algebra map is an equivalence.
Unit generation on both sides proves full faithfulness and essential
surjectivity.  Finally nearby cycles have their natural lax monoidal
map; on $E^\circ$ its unit map is an equivalence by smooth local
acyclicity.  The tensor comparison is an equivalence on $(Q,Q)$,
then on all pairs in the two thick closures by exactness in each
variable.  This proves the stated strong monoidal property of
$\mathcal S$, not of $N$ for the ambient tensor on $E$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The tensor product appropriate to the star image]
\label{prop:relative-star-tensor}
Let $H=Rj_*Q_U$ with its actual commutative algebra structure on $E$.
The forgetful functor from the thick subcategory generated by $H$ in
$H$-module sheaves to ordinary sheaf complexes is an equivalence onto
$\mathcal C_*$.  Thus the latter has a canonical tensor product
$\otimes_H$ realized by module sheaves, with unit $H$, and
\[
 N(F)\otimes_H N(G)\xrightarrow{\sim}N(F\otimes_Q G).
\]
For a supported coefficient one likewise has
\[
 N(F)\otimes_H j_!\mathcal S(G)
       \simeq j_!\bigl(\mathcal S(F)\otimes_Q\mathcal S(G)\bigr).
\]
These statements use a relative tensor, not the non-closed ordinary
$Q_E$-tensor product exhibited earlier.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The algebra $H$ is defined by the lax monoidal right adjoint $Rj_*$,
and $j^*H=Q_U$.  For the module generator,
\[
 \RHom_{H\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}(E)}(H,H)=R\Gamma(E,H)
      \simeq\RHom_E(H,H).
\]
The last equivalence is induced by $Q_E\to H$.  Since its open
restriction is the identity, its cone is
\[
 i_*\operatorname{cofib}\bigl(Q_Z\longrightarrow L(Q_U)\bigr)
       \simeq \bigoplus_{a\in Z}i_{a,*}Q(-1)[-1].
\]
Indeed applying $i^*$ gives exactly the displayed endpoint cofibres.
For any closed coefficient $C$, adjunction gives
$\RHom_E(i_*C,H)=\RHom_Z(C,i^!H)=0$.  Applying this to the actual
cone proves the required equivalence.  The larger object $i_*L(Q_U)$
is instead the cone of $j_!Q_U\to Rj_*Q_U$.
The forgetful map on generator endomorphisms is exactly this
equivalence, since evaluation of an $H$-linear endomorphism at its
unit gives the same section.  The two-variable thick-generation
argument proves full faithfulness on the thick module subcategory.
Its image is $\thick_E(H)=\mathcal C_*$, by the already proved
open reconstruction.  This supplies the claimed module realization
without merely transporting an abstract tensor across an equivalence.

The module tensor is exact in each variable and takes $(H,H)$ to
$H$, so it preserves this thick subcategory.  The actual lax tensor
comparison for $Rj_*$ factors over $H$ and is an equivalence on
$(Q_U,Q_U)$.  Generation in each variable proves it for all unipotent
coefficients.  The comparison $N\to Rj_*\mathcal S$ is the
open-adjunction unit and respects these module actions; together with
the proved monoidality of $\mathcal S$ this gives the first formula.
Finally the relative open projection formula, with $j^*H=Q_U$, gives
the second formula and its actual $H$-action on the supported object.
No unrestricted ordinary tensor closure has been added as an axiom.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The actual complete-support partner}
\begin{theorem}[Exceptional support on the modified model]
\label{thm:actual-support-image}
For $F\in\mathcal V$, the actual exceptional restriction satisfies
\[
 \kappa^!R\Psi_{X'}F\xrightarrow{\sim}j_!\mathcal S(F).
\]
This identifies the supported functor with $\mathcal C_!$ and exchanges
it with the star functor of Theorem~\ref{thm:actual-nearby-image} under
geometric Verdier duality.  The comparisons are those induced by
nearby duality and the actual open-immersion adjunctions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the geometric nearby-duality theorem of Gabber in the formulation
of \cite[Corollary 3.2, Remark 3.3]{LuZhengNearby}, with dualizing
complexes relative to the geometric generic and special points.
It gives $D_{X'_s}R\Psi F=R\Psi D_{X'_{\bar\eta}}F$.  The model is
separated of finite type over the complete excellent trait, and the
source consists of bounded constructible lisse perfect complexes.
The cited construction is induced by evaluation and the six-operation
trace, so it supplies the comparison of pairings as well as of objects.
We use its geometric version; no assertion about taking wild-inertia
invariants or about a full sliced vanishing-cycle functor is needed.
For the present rational source it suffices to establish this
canonical comparison on $Q$ and its thick closure.  On $Q$, the
semistable constant computations have uniformly bounded finite free
cohomology at every coefficient precision.  The finite-coefficient
nearby-duality maps are compatible with coefficient reduction and
evaluation, giving the adic and then rational comparison in the
constructible formalism.  The equivalence locus of the two exact
contravariant functors is closed under finite cones and retracts,
so Proposition~\ref{prop:unit-generation} extends it to $\mathcal V$.
This argument does not exchange duality with an arbitrary ambient
inverse limit of sheaves.

Write $D_\eta F=F^\vee(1)[2]$ on the smooth generic curve.  Proper
closed-immersion Verdier exchange and the preceding theorem give
\[
 \kappa^!R\Psi F
 \simeq D_E\kappa^*R\Psi D_\eta F
 \simeq D_E Rj_*\mathcal S(D_\eta F)
 \simeq j_!\mathcal S(F).
\]
For the last step, strong monoidality preserves lisse duals and
their evaluation, while exactness preserves shifts.  On the smooth
neighbourhood $\Spec R[u,u^{-1}]$ of $E^\circ$, the finite etale
sheaves $\mu_{\ell^r}$ specialize by their actual smooth pullbacks.
These identifications commute with $\mu_{\ell^{r+1}}\to\mu_{\ell^r}$
and the coefficient evaluation maps.  The selected adic passage and
rationalization therefore give $\mathcal S(Q(1))\simeq Q(1)$
canonically, without choosing a basis of the Tate line.  Together
these identifications give $\mathcal S(D_\eta F)\simeq
D_{E^\circ}\mathcal S(F)$, compatibly with evaluation.
All arrows come from these adjunctions and their Verdier mates, and
therefore retain the evaluation and localization compatibilities.
The support on the left is $E\hookrightarrow X'_s$, not the endpoint
support $Z\hookrightarrow E$ used to prove the intrinsic gluing theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[One modification: star modules and supported values]
\label{thm:one-modification}
Let $i_Y:Y\hookrightarrow X_s$ and put
\[
 B(F)=i_Y^*R\Psi_XF,\qquad A_Y=B(Q),\qquad
 B_!(F)=i_Y^!R\Psi_XF.
\]
The actual cochain algebra map
$A_\eta=R\Gamma(\mathbf G_{m,\bar K},Q)\to A_Y$ is an equivalence.
The functor $B$, with its natural $A_Y$-module structure, identifies
$\mathcal V$ with $\Perf(A_Y)$.  For this actual modification the
proper nearby and proper base-change maps give
\[
 B(F)\xrightarrow{\sim}R\Gamma(E,N(F)),\qquad
 B_!(F)\xrightarrow{\sim}R\Gamma(E,\kappa^!R\Psi_{X'}F)
                  \simeq R\Gamma_c(E^\circ,\mathcal S(F)).
\]
Together with Theorems~\ref{thm:actual-nearby-image} and
\ref{thm:actual-support-image}, these give a Morita equivalence
$\Perf(A_Y)\simeq\mathcal C_*$ with its actual supported partner.
They do not give an equivalence with the full $\mathcal C(E,Z)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The restriction map $R\Gamma(X_{\bar\eta},F)\to B(F)$ is constructed
by geometric nearby specialization.  On $Q$ it preserves the unit
and sends $[x]$ to the primitive relative Kummer class at the old
node, by Lemma~\ref{lem:constant-nearby}.  It is therefore an
algebra equivalence; no factor of two occurs since $a$ generates
$P^{\rm gp}/\mathbf Zc$.  Exactness and unit generation extend this
actual comparison to $F$, with its cup-product module action.  The
usual Morita identification for $\mathcal V$ then proves the first
assertion.  Forgetting this module structure would not give a fully
faithful functor into $D(Q)$.

The map $\mu$ is proper and $\mu_{\bar\eta}$ is the identity.
Classical proper nearby base change gives
$R\Psi_XF\simeq R\mu_{s,*}R\Psi_{X'}F$.  Proper base change along
$i_Y$ identifies its ordinary restriction with the first displayed
integral on the complete inverse image $E$.  Taking its Verdier mate
gives the exceptional proper base-change comparison and hence the
second integral.  Inserting Theorem~\ref{thm:actual-support-image}
and using properness of $E$ gives the compact-support expression.
Naturality of nearby restriction, cup products, the proper trace and
their adjunctions identifies these with the same coefficient maps
already used in the Morita comparison.

On $\mathcal C_*$, $R\Gamma(E,-)$ with its cochain action is the
Morita functor for the generator $Rj_*Q$: the map
$Q_E\to Rj_*Q$ induces
$\RHom_E(Rj_*Q,H)\simeq\RHom_E(Q_E,H)$ because $i^!H=0$.
Thus the integral comparison here retains a full module-valued
reconstruction on the proved admissible subcategory.  This explains
why it avoids the disjoint-skyscraper obstruction rather than
contradicting it.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Supplied compatible finite descent]
\label{cor:nearby-finite-descent}
Suppose a finite group $\Delta$ is supplied with compatible
$R$-automorphisms of $X$ and $X'$, preserving $Y$ and $E$, commuting
with $\mu$, and preserving the chosen geometric base.  The comparisons
in Theorems~\ref{thm:actual-nearby-image}--\ref{thm:one-modification}
then induce equivalences with this actual finite descent retained.
The Morita generators are induced generators as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-descent}, rather than only equivariant
trivial coefficients.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Generic restriction, geometric nearby cycles, the immersions of the
invariant strata, and proper direct image form equivariant functors
with their geometric composition cells.  All comparison maps used
above are their units, base-change maps or Verdier mates.  They
therefore define equivariant natural equivalences, whose limits over
$B\Delta$ are equivalences of the actual descent categories.  The
source and target geometric conditions are invariant under pullback,
so Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-descent} supplies the stated generators.
This argument neither exchanges a root-level colimit with $B\Delta$
nor constructs a lift of an arbitrary group action to the modification.
\end{proof}

The supported value is equivalently

\section{Cellular refinement for a fixed thickness}
\subsection{The local input for every integer thickness}
\begin{proposition}[All-thickness nearby algebras and branch maps]
\label{prop:all-thickness-nearby}
For every $d\geq1$, give $T_d=\Spec R[z,w]/(zw-t^d)$ the chart
$P_d=\langle a,b,c:a+b=dc\rangle$, with $a\mapsto z$, $b\mapsto w$
and $c\mapsto t$.  Over the standard log trait this is a vertical fs
log smooth scheme.  For $\Lambda_r=\mathbf Z/\ell^r$, its geometric
constant nearby stalk at the node has cohomology $\Lambda_r$ and
$\Lambda_r(-1)$ in degrees zero and one, and zero otherwise.  The
class $[z]$ is primitive.  The actual open-adjunction map to the link
on the branch with punctured coordinate $z$ sends it to $[z]$; on
the other branch $[w]=-[z]$ maps to its primitive coordinate class.
These are equivalences of the actual cochain algebras.  The same
statements hold adically and over $Q$, including when $p$ divides $d$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In coordinates $a=(1,0)$, $c=(0,1)$, $b=(-1,d)$, the monoid is
exactly the integral cone $n\geq0$, $dh+n\geq0$: for $h<0$ write
$(h,n)=(-h)b+(n+dh)c$, and for $h\geq0$ use $ha+nc$.
It is fine and saturated, its group is $\mathbf Za\oplus\mathbf Zc$,
and the group map $\mathbf Z\to P_d^{\rm gp}$ has zero kernel and
free cokernel $\mathbf Za$.  The scheme map to the chart base change
$\Spec R\times_{\Spec\mathbf Z[\mathbf N]}\Spec\mathbf Z[P_d]$
is an isomorphism.  These verify the log smooth chart criterion,
without an invertibility condition on $d$.  Inverting $c$ makes both
$a$ and $b$ invertible, so the structure is vertical; the generic
fibre has trivial log structure.

Apply \cite[Theorem 8.3 and Corollary 8.4]{IllusieOverview} with
invertible finite coefficients.  On the geometric log fibre the
relative characteristic group is $P_d^{\rm gp}/\mathbf Zc=\mathbf Za$.
Its prime-to-$p$ Kummer inertia has the rank-one exterior cohomology
computed in \cite[\S5]{IllusieOverview}.  The resulting degree-one
class is the Kummer boundary of $z$, not $d[z]$.  This proves the
stalk assertion; on either smooth branch open the relative group is
zero and smooth local acyclicity gives $\Lambda_r$.

To identify the map, apply nearby cycles to the generic Kummer
morphism $\Lambda_r\to\Lambda_r(1)[1]$ of $z$ and precompose with
the specialization unit.  On $D(z)=\Spec R[z,z^{-1}]$ the torsor
$h^{\ell^r}=z$ is etale.  Smooth specialization and naturality of the
open-adjunction unit identify its boundary on the punctured strict
branch with $[z]$.  This computes the actual map in degree one; in
degree zero it preserves the unit.  The identical argument on $D(w)$
uses $w$.  On the geometric generic fibre $[t]=0$, hence
$[w]=d[t]-[z]=-[z]$.  Both source and target have only the two stated
cohomology groups, so the algebra maps are equivalences.  No
formality of either algebra is used.

This computation is functorial in the monomial maps of the models.
In particular $x=t^a z=t^b/w$ gives $[x]=[z]=-[w]$ after passage to
the geometric generic fibre.  A change by a smooth unit is handled
by the same etale torsor naturality, with its actual specialized unit
class retained.  Pullbacks under the cut contractions below are
these monomial maps; their proper direct-image units will be compared
in Lemma~\ref{lem:cell-algebra-unit}.  We do not infer a shriek
comparison from this stalk computation.  Finally $p\mid d$ introduces
no relative group torsion and changes none of these arguments: only
$\ell$ is required to be invertible.

For the coefficient passage, the finite-level Kummer maps commute
with reduction.  At nodes their cohomology is finite free in the
fixed range $[0,1]$, and on smooth strata in degree zero.  Thus the
actual systems have derived reduction equal to the lower-precision
system; their stalk cohomology towers are Mittag--Leffler.  In the
constructible adic formalism their reductions detect equivalences.
The compatible finite-level algebra comparisons consequently give
an adic equivalence and then its rationalization.  This passage uses
the constructed constant system, not inverse limits of arbitrary
constructible sheaves.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Naturality of the selected branch maps]
\label{lem:branch-map-naturality}
The branch maps in Proposition~\ref{prop:all-thickness-nearby} are
maps of cochain algebras obtained by open adjunction.  Their
coefficient versions are module maps.  For the monomial model maps
of Proposition~\ref{prop:cut-models}, they give the open-unit squares
and the two-step attachment comparison of
Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre-cartesian-ambient}, with both branch signs retained.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At precision $r$, define every map by applying the open-adjunction
unit to the nearby complex, then restricting to the chosen branch.
For a refinement $u$, let $F_u$ denote module pullback and let
$\chi_u:F_uRj_*\to Rj'_*F_U$ be adjoint to the open counit.
Thus the actual square is
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
 F_uN_I(F)&\longrightarrow&N_J(F)\\
 \downarrow&&\downarrow\\
 F_uRj_*j^*N_I(F)&\xrightarrow{\chi_u}&Rj'_*j'^*N_J(F).
\end{array}
\]
The upper map is the nearby mate constructed in
Lemma~\ref{lem:cell-algebra-unit}.  The bottom arrow includes $Rj'_*$ of the open
restriction of that upper comparison, after $\chi_u$.  To check the square, apply open
restriction and take the adjoint: both composites become the same
open counit followed by the same nearby pullback.  Restricting this
square to a closed fibre gives the attachment square, without
assuming the external link is cartesian on that fibre.

On a gap $[a,b]$, the coordinate equalities
$x=t^az=t^b/w$ give, on the geometric generic fibre,
\[
 [x]=[z],\qquad [w]=-[x].
\]
For a refined gap $[a',b']\subset[a,b]$, pullback sends
$z$ to $t^{a'-a}z'$ and $w$ to $t^{b-b'}w'$.  Naturality of the
Kummer connecting morphism therefore gives the same two classes,
with the same signs.  This checks both branches of the actual square,
not just the rank of its degree-one groups.  Multiplication by a
smooth unit contributes its own specialized Kummer class; it is not
silently discarded.  Base constants disappear here only after
geometric generic base change.

For $I\subset J\subset K$, the exponents add and the mate of
$\chi_vF_v\chi_u$ is the composite open counit, which is the mate
of $\chi_{uv}$.  Their canonical adjunction comparison therefore
gives $\chi_{uv}=\chi_vF_v\chi_u$ with the specified composition cell.
The closed restriction gives the two-step formula for the branch
attachments, including the two branches at an internal node.
All maps were defined before the Kummer computation.  They are
algebra maps at the unit by the finite-arity counit calculation in
Lemma~\ref{lem:cell-algebra-unit}, and module maps on coefficients.
The loci of equivalence for these exact natural transformations
are thick.  Unit generation therefore proves the assertions for
nontrivial rational unipotent coefficients as well, without
splitting their extensions.  The coefficient passage is
Lemma~\ref{lem:selected-adic-comparisons}.  This verifies these
specified maps and their two-step composition, not the simultaneous
higher coherence of all supported and kernel operations.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An actual family closed under common refinement}
Fix an integer $m\geq2$ and put $X_m=\Spec R[x,y]/(xy-t^m)$.
The coefficient source remains
$\mathcal V=\mathcal U^b(\mathbf G_{m,\bar K})$; geometric nearby
cycles in this section forget the base Galois action.  For a set
$I\subset\{1,\ldots,m-1\}$ write
\[
 0=a_0<a_1<\cdots<a_r<a_{r+1}=m,\qquad
 d_i=a_{i+1}-a_i.
\]
Define $X_I$ by gluing the charts
\[
 \Spec R[z_i,w_i]/(z_iw_i-t^{d_i}),\qquad
 z_i=x/t^{a_i},\quad w_i=t^{a_{i+1}}/x,
\]
with $w_{i-1}=z_i^{-1}$ on the consecutive principal-open overlaps.
The induced nonconsecutive overlaps lie in the common generic fibre.  Let
$E_I=X_I\times_{X_m}Y$, where $Y=V(x,y,t)$.
For $I=\varnothing$, this means $X_I=X_m$ and $E_I=Y$.

\begin{proposition}[Models, complete support, and their maps]
\label{prop:cut-models}
These are actual proper toroidal modifications of $X_m$, with identical
geometric generic fibres.  For $I\ne\varnothing$, $E_I$ is a reduced
chain of $r$ projective lines.  Its strata are $r$ copies $U_i$ of
$\mathbf G_m$ and the $r+1$ nodes $q_i$ of the ambient special fibre;
the two outside nodes are smooth points of the chain itself.
For $I\subset J$ there is a canonical proper map
$\mu_{JI}:X_J\to X_I$, and
$\mu_{KI}=\mu_{JI}\mu_{KJ}$ for $I\subset J\subset K$.
The complete inverse image of $E_I$ is $E_J$.
Common refinements are supplied by $I\cup J$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On a gap of thickness $d$, inserting a cut at $e$, $0<e<d$, is the
blow-up of $(z,t^e)$ in $A=R[z,w]/(zw-t^d)$.  Its Rees algebra
is $A[zs,t^es]\subset A[s]$; the two standard opens have rings
$A[t^e/z]$ and $A[z/t^e]$ inside the fraction field.  With
$v=t^e/z$ and $u=z/t^e$, respectively, their fraction-ring charts are
\[
 R[z,v]/(zv-t^e),\quad w=t^{d-e}v,
 \qquad R[u,w]/(uw-t^{d-e}),\quad z=t^eu.
\]
There is no extra component from an uncancelled equation.  The monoid
$P_d=\langle a,b,c:a+b=dc\rangle$ is the saturated cone
$\{(h,n):n\geq0,dh+n\geq0\}$, with $a=(1,0)$, $c=(0,1)$,
$b=(-1,d)$.  The two blow-up monoids are already saturated copies
of $P_e$ and $P_{d-e}$.  Thus this is also the actual fs log blow-up;
no root extension of the base is required.

Iterating gives the displayed gap charts.  If $[a',b']\subset[a,b]$
is a refined gap, the map to the old chart is
$z\mapsto t^{a'-a}z'$ and $w\mapsto t^{b-b'}w'$.
These formulas agree on overlaps and compose identically.  Each
insertion is a blow-up and hence proper; the resulting glued models
are independent of insertion order by these formulas.  The chart formulas construct the identifications for different
insertion orders.  These identifications are unique once fixed on the
dense generic fibre, since the sources are reduced and the targets
are separated.  The common generic fibre alone does not construct them.

On an interior gap the ideal of $Y$ pulls back to $(t)$; on the first
and last gaps it pulls back to $(t,z_0)$ and $(t,w_r)$, respectively.
The reduced special fibres of the gap charts and their inverse
coordinate overlaps therefore give precisely the stated chain.
The empty cut set gives the point instead.  Equality of complete
inverse-image supports follows from composition of these ideal
pullbacks.  Finally the displayed formulas construct all maps to the
model for $I\cup J$, proving the common-refinement assertion as an
actual scheme statement.
\end{proof}

The family is deliberately restricted to integer cuts of this fixed
thickness.  Gaps of length one are regular semistable charts; larger
gaps are toroidal nodes of the stated thickness.  This theorem neither
inserts another regular node into a thickness-one chart over the same
base nor treats arbitrary ramified base extensions.

\subsection{Intrinsic cellular coefficients with branchwise admissibility}
Put $\kappa_I:E_I\to(X_I)_s$ and define the actual commutative
nearby algebra and complete-support module
\[
 H_I=\kappa_I^*R\Psi_{X_I}Q,\qquad
 W_I=\kappa_I^!R\Psi_{X_I}Q.
\]
On $U_i$ the algebra $H_I$ is $Q$.  At a node write $A_q=i_q^*H_I$.
For an incident branch $U_i$ write $B_{qi}$ for its punctured-strict-
neighbourhood cochain algebra.  The actual open-adjunction unit gives
$\lambda_{qi}:A_q\to B_{qi}$.

\begin{lemma}[The branch algebras and their coefficient functors]
\label{lem:branch-algebras}
Every $A_q$ has cohomology $Q$ and $Q(-1)$ in degrees zero and one.
Both actual algebra maps
\[
 A_q\xrightarrow{\lambda_{qi}}B_{qi}
 \xleftarrow{\kappa_{qi}}A_i=R\Gamma(U_i,Q)
\]
are equivalences.  The coefficient link is consequently an equivalence
$\mathcal U^b(U_i)\to\Perf(A_q)$, with the $A_q$-action obtained by
restriction along $\lambda_{qi}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For every gap, $P_d^{\rm gp}/\mathbf Zc$ is generated by $a$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:all-thickness-nearby} therefore supplies the
all-gap calculation, with its primitive Kummer class and no factor of $d$.
The branch coordinates are $x/t^{a_i}$ and their inverses.  The actual
Kummer-morphism argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:actual-nearby-image}
computes each $\lambda_{qi}$, independently on the two branches at
an internal node.  On the other side, $\kappa_{qi}$ is the actual
restriction from $\mathbf G_m$ to a punctured strict neighbourhood,
as in Proposition~\ref{prop:link}.  Their naturality as actual maps,
including coefficient extensions and the two branch signs, is
Lemma~\ref{lem:branch-map-naturality}.  Both preserve the unit and take
the primitive degree-one class to a generator, with the sign of the
local coordinate.  They are therefore algebra equivalences.
The coefficient link, as a module-valued functor, is extension of
scalars along the second map followed by restriction along the first;
the specified evaluation map is
\[
 R\Gamma(U_i,V_i)\otimes^L_{A_i}B_{qi}\longrightarrow L_{qi}(V_i).
\]
It is $B_{qi}$-linear, and then $A_q$-linear by restriction along
$A_q\to B_{qi}$.  It is an equivalence at the unit and hence on
its thick closure by exactness.
Thus it is an equivalence of the stated categories, not just a
comparison of underlying cohomology complexes.
\end{proof}

Let $S\subset E_I$ be a locally closed union of the stated strata.
Write $H_S=H_I|_S$ and use actual $H_S$-module sheaves.  Empty supports
are assigned the zero category.  The incidence graph of a nonempty $S$
has a vertex for each included $U_i$ and $q$, and an edge for each
included branch incidence; it is a finite forest.

\begin{definition}[The cellular coefficient category]\label{def:cell-category}
Let $\mathcal C_I(S)$ consist of bounded constructible $H_S$-modules
with the following geometric conditions.  Their restriction $V_i$ to
$U_i$ has unipotent lisse cohomology.  Their node restriction $M_q$
is perfect over $A_q$.  For every included branch the component
\[
 M_q\longrightarrow L_{qi}(V_i)
\]
of the actual attaching map is an equivalence of $A_q$-modules.
The module actions and these arrows are part of the actual sheaf
module structure; no condition refers to an image from the old model.
\end{definition}

At an internal node with two branches it is the two individual maps
that are invertible.  The combined map
$M_q\to L_{q,i}(V_i)\oplus L_{q,i+1}(V_{i+1})$
is diagonal up to those identifications, and need not be invertible.
Thus the one-component criterion $i^!F=0$ must not be imposed on the
whole chain.  Nor do we forget the $A_q$-actions and demand full
faithfulness in the category of ordinary node complexes.  Branchwise
admissibility is a geometric condition to be checked on the actual maps;
perfectness of an integrated cochain module alone does not imply it.

\begin{theorem}[Cellular reconstruction before invariance]
\label{thm:cell-reconstruction}
The category $\mathcal C_I(S)$ is the homotopy limit of its forest of
stratum categories and the actual branch link equivalences of
Lemma~\ref{lem:branch-algebras}.  It is the thick subcategory generated
by $H_S$ in $H_S$-module sheaves.  Hence
\[
 \mathcal C_I(S)\simeq\Perf(R\Gamma(S,H_S)).
\]
For $S=E_I$, the actual functor
$N_I(F)=\kappa_I^*R\Psi_{X_I}F$, with its $H_I$-action, is an
equivalence $\mathcal V\simeq\mathcal C_I(E_I)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the open union of the included $U_i$ and its finite closed
complement of nodes in $S$.  Recollement in module sheaves reconstructs
an object as
\[
 Rj_*V\mathop{\times}^{h}_{i_*L(V)}i_*M.
\]
Here $j:U\to S$ is the included open-stratum union, $i:Z\to S$
its closed node complement and $L=i^*Rj_*$.  The $H_S$-action on
$Rj_*V$ uses $H_S\to Rj_*j^*H_S$, and the closed module uses
$H_S\to i_*i^*H_S$.  Forgetting the module action creates limits
and detects equivalences, so the underlying pullback can be checked
on sheaf complexes.  Its mapping spectra, however, are module mapping
spectra, not arbitrary $Q$-linear mapping spectra.
The selected open coefficients have bounded constructible links by
unit generation and Lemma~\ref{lem:branch-algebras}.  Each perfect
$A_q$-module has bounded finite-dimensional underlying $Q$-cohomology,
since $A_q$ does and finite cones and retracts preserve that property.
The finite pullback therefore stays in the stated bounded
constructible category.  No unbounded-module assertion is used.
The generator endomorphism identification has type
\[
 \RHom_{H_S\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}(S)}(H_S,H_S)
        \simeq R\Gamma(S,H_S).
\]  At a node the
punctured strict neighbourhood is the disjoint union of its included
branches, so $L(V)$ is their finite product.  The module mapping
spectrum is the fibre of the same difference map as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:gluing}, now with $A_q$-linear maps at each node.
These statements follow from the actual adjunctions and prove enhanced
reconstruction, including the homotopies of attaching squares.

Imposing the individual equivalences in Definition~\ref{def:cell-category}
therefore gives exactly the asserted homotopy limit of categories.
Removing a leaf of a tree forgets an object determined, with a
contractible space of choices, by an equivalence across its single
incident edge.  Evaluation at any vertex of a connected tree is
consequently an equivalence of stable categories.  For a forest the
result is a finite product of these categories.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:branch-algebras}, $H_S$ evaluates to a generator at
each vertex.  Its finite product components split by the component
idempotents, so it generates the entire forest category.  Conversely
$H_S$ satisfies all the geometric conditions, and those conditions
are stable under finite cones and retracts.  This proves the thick
identification and then the Morita assertion.  The empty and isolated-
node cases have the same statement with zero or one vertex.

For actual $N_I(F)$, each branch admissibility map is a natural
transformation of exact functors on $\mathcal V$.  Its value on $Q$
is $\lambda_{qi}$, already proved invertible, and its node module on
$Q$ is $A_q$.  Unit generation proves the conditions for every $F$.
At any smooth component stratum the actual specialization functor is
an equivalence by the coordinate calculation $[x]\mapsto[x/t^{a_i}]$
of Theorem~\ref{thm:actual-nearby-image}.  Evaluation on that vertex
and the tree reconstruction prove the last assertion.  With no cuts
use the old-node module calculation with $P_m$ instead.  Thus the
geometric admissibility criterion was verified before identifying
the image; the image was not used as its definition.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[An internal node is not a terminal node]
\label{ex:internal-cell}
Let $q$ be an internal node of a chain with at least two components.
After identifying both branch algebras with $A_q$ by their actual
Kummer maps, the attaching map of $H_I$ is
\[
 A_q\xrightarrow{(1,1)}A_q\oplus A_q.
\]
The localization triangle on the chain therefore gives
\[
 i_q^!H_I\simeq A_q[-1],
\]
where an ordering of the branches identifies the quotient map with
$(b_1,b_2)\mapsto b_2-b_1$.  At an outside node only one branch is
present, so the same costalk is zero.  These assertions follow from
the actual link and localization triangle; the internal boundary is
nonzero, including for nonconstant coefficient modules.

In particular the complete-support module is not generally extension
by zero from the disjoint smooth strata.  Nearby Verdier duality gives
at an internal node
\[
 i_q^*W_I\simeq D_Q(A_q[-1])(-1)[-2]
                  =D_QA_q(-1)[-1],
\]
which has nonzero cohomology in degrees zero and one.  At an outside
node its stalk is zero.  Thus the one-component formula
$W=j_!Q$ cannot be copied onto a longer chain; the cellular supported
module retains the internal gluing.
\end{example}

\subsection{Support categories and the data to be compared}
We distinguish internal module duality from geometric Verdier duality:
\[
 M^{\vee_{H_S}}=R\mathcal Hom_{H_S}(M,H_S),\qquad
 D_SM=R\mathcal Hom_Q(M,\omega_S),\qquad
 \widetilde D_SM=D_SM(-1)[-2].
\]
Here $\mathcal Hom$ denotes the sheaf internal Hom; its global sections
are taken only when explicitly written.  Module duality is an
anti-equivalence on the finite-cell category $\mathcal C_I(S)$.
Verdier duality uses the geometric dualizing complex $\omega_S$, not
$H_S$, and gives the anti-equivalence
$\widetilde D_S:\mathcal C_I(S)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal C_I^!(S)$,
with the inverse obtained by the same normalized duality.  The same
convention applies to the two ambient types.  The action on a dual
is the contragredient action, identifying the opposite algebra through
commutativity.  The normalization is involutive because duality reverses
both Tate twists and shifts.  The covariant expression
$\widetilde D_S(M^{\vee_{H_S}})$ composes two contravariant functors;
it does not identify these two dualities.

For $k_S:S\hookrightarrow E_I$ define
\[
 W_S=k_S^!W_I=(\kappa_Ik_S)^!R\Psi_{X_I}Q.
\]
This is an actual support object.  Geometric nearby duality identifies
it, with its $H_S$-action, as
\[
 W_S\simeq D_S H_S(-1)[-2].
\]
Let $\widetilde D_S=D_S(-1)[-2]$; this is an involutive duality on
constructible $H_S$-modules, using the commutativity of $H_S$ to
identify the opposite action.  Let $\mathcal C_I^!(S)$ be its image
on $\mathcal C_I(S)$, equivalently the modules whose normalized
Verdier dual satisfies Definition~\ref{def:cell-category}.

\begin{proposition}[Actual supported partners and typed operations]
\label{prop:cell-support}
Both categories are realized in actual module sheaves, and
\[
 M\longmapsto W_S\otimes^L_{H_S}M
 \simeq \widetilde D_S(M^{\vee_{H_S}})
\]
is an equivalence $\mathcal C_I(S)\simeq\mathcal C_I^!(S)$.
For $S=E_I$ and $M=N_I(F)$ its value is
$\kappa_I^!R\Psi_{X_I}F$.
For an inclusion of allowed supports, ordinary restriction preserves
$\mathcal C_I$, and exceptional restriction preserves
$\mathcal C_I^!$.  Tensor over $H_S$, its action on the supported
partner, and their evaluation maps are preserved on these categories.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The formula for $W_S$ is Gabber nearby duality followed by actual
Verdier exchange for $\kappa_Ik_S$, with the generic curve shift and
Tate twist.  It applies to every gap just as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:actual-support-image}.  Thus it is a geometric
identification of the already defined costalk.
For a finite-cell $H_S$-module, the evaluation comparison from
$W_S\otimes_{H_S}M$ to $\widetilde D_S(M^{\vee_{H_S}})$ is an
equivalence on $H_S$ and hence on its thick closure.  The right side
is a composite of two dualities, so gives the asserted equivalence.
For actual nearby coefficients the natural mixed projection and
nearby cup-product map gives
$W_I\otimes_{H_I}N_I(F)\to\kappa_I^!R\Psi F$.
It is balanced for the actual $H_I$-action, is the identity at $F=Q$,
and hence is an equivalence throughout the geometrically generated
source.  No unrestricted exceptional projection formula is assumed
to be invertible on arbitrary constructible coefficients.

Restriction takes $H_T$ to $H_S$, so the proved generation theorem
shows it preserves the star categories.  Verdier exchange gives the
exceptional assertion for their supported partners.  Relative tensor
and evaluation preserve finite-cell modules by exactness and the unit
calculation.  The same argument proves the mixed action on $W_S$.
\end{proof}

We now specify the \emph{cellular datum} $\mathfrak C_I$.  It consists
of the categories $\mathcal C_I(S),\mathcal C_I^!(S)$ for the allowed
supports, their actual algebras $H_S$, supported modules $W_S$ and
Morita presentations, restriction and exceptional restriction with
their composition cells, and the actual extension/localization diagrams
in the ambient module categories.  In particular, for an open/closed
decomposition $T=U\sqcup Z$, it retains
\[
 j_!j^*M\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow i_*i^*M\longrightarrow,
 \qquad
 i_*i^!P\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow Rj_*j^*P\longrightarrow,
\]
where $M\in\mathcal C_I(T)$ and $P\in\mathcal C_I^!(T)$.
Extension terms are not asserted to belong to $\mathcal C_I(T)$:
they have their own support type and live in the displayed ambient
module category.  The datum includes their mapping objects through
recollement, the branch inertia algebras and maps, and the Verdier
exchange of these triangles.  Thus an invariance assertion for this
datum includes the restriction/link functors and boundary maps, not
only the value of $R\Gamma(E_I,-)$.

The ambient category needed here can itself be specified without using
all constructible modules.  Let $\mathcal A_I(S)$ have the same stratum
conditions as Definition~\ref{def:cell-category}, but allow arbitrary
$A_q$-linear attaching maps.  Recollement proves that it is generated
by $j_{i,!}Q_{U_i}$ and $i_{q,*}A_q$.  It contains the displayed star
localization terms; its normalized Verdier dual contains the dual
terms.  All their mapping spectra are the actual module mapping
spectra.  The following fibrewise statement ensures that these maps,
including off-diagonal extension maps, are also compared under
refinement.
The roles can be summarized as
\[
 \mathcal C_I(S)\subset\mathcal A_I(S),\qquad
 \mathcal C_J(S')\subset\mathcal A_{J|I}(S')\subset\mathcal A_J(S').
\]
The first category requires every included branch map to be invertible;
the middle fine category requires this only within coarse fibres;
the ambient categories permit arbitrary attachments.  The cartesian
categories carry the coefficient equivalence, while the middle
ambient category retains the coarse localization maps.

\subsection{The proper cellular comparison}
Let $I\subset J$, and put $u:E_J\to E_I$.  For an allowed support
$S\subset E_I$ use its \emph{complete} inverse image $S'=u^{-1}S$,
with the pulled-back scheme structure.  Its strata have the preceding
forest description; nilpotent structure, if present, does not change
the etale module-sheaf category.  The map $u_S:S'\to S$ is proper.

\begin{lemma}[The actual algebra unit under contraction]
\label{lem:cell-algebra-unit}
The nearby pullback map and its adjoint give
\[
 u_S^*H_S\longrightarrow H_{S'},\qquad
 H_S\xrightarrow{\sim}Ru_{S,*}H_{S'}.
\]
The second is an equivalence of actual algebras, natural for
restrictions of supports and for composition of the model maps.
There is also a natural equivalence
$W_S\simeq Ru_{S,*}W_{S'}$ with its $H_S$-action.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We construct the map at each finite precision, then apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:selected-adic-comparisons}.  Write $f:X_J\to X_I$,
and let $j_I,j_J$ denote the geometric generic maps in the nearby
construction.  The special pullback of the mate
\[
 f^*Rj_{I,*}F\longrightarrow Rj_{J,*}f_\eta^*F
\]
is the nearby pullback map.  The mate itself is adjoint to
\[
 j_J^*f^*Rj_{I,*}F
       \simeq f_\eta^*j_I^*Rj_{I,*}F
       \xrightarrow{f_\eta^*\epsilon}f_\eta^*F.
\]
These formulas can be made before geometric generic base change and
are compatible with it.  For any finite tuple of coefficients,
tensoring these counits and then multiplying is the counit for the
tensor of the tuple.  Thus the mate commutes with every finite-arity
lax tensor operation, including the nullary unit and substitution.
At $F=\Lambda_r$ it is a map in commutative algebras, and for other
$F$ it is a map in modules over that algebra.  This constructs the
lift; it is not inferred from a cohomology isomorphism.

The adjoint $\theta:H_S\to Ru_{S,*}H_{S'}$ is the classical proper
nearby comparison.  Indeed composition of direct images gives
$Rf_*Rj_{J,*}=Rj_{I,*}Rf_{\eta,*}$, and ordinary proper base change
moves the special restriction past $Rf_*$.  Here $f_\eta$ is the
identity.  This derives the required proper nearby theorem from
ordinary proper base change (SGA~4, Expose XII, Theorem 5.1), without
an additional stack-nearby assertion.  Base changing again to the
complete inverse image of $S$ gives $\theta$, hence its underlying
equivalence and its equivalence in commutative algebras.

Write $F_u=u_H^*$ and $G_u=Ru_*$.  Their module projection map is
specified by the adjoint of the evaluation map
\[
 F_u(M\otimes_{H_S}G_uP)\simeq
 F_uM\otimes_{H_{S'}}F_uG_uP
 \xrightarrow{1\otimes\epsilon}F_uM\otimes_{H_{S'}}P.
\]
Its adjoint is the $H_S$-linear projection map
\[
 \pi_{M,P}:M\otimes_{H_S}G_uP\longrightarrow
                 G_u(F_uM\otimes_{H_{S'}}P).
\]
Fix $P$ in the domain of these operations.  For $M=H_S$ this map is
\[
 G_uP\xrightarrow{\eta_{G_uP}}G_uF_uG_uP
       \xrightarrow{G_u\epsilon_P}G_uP=G_uP,
\]
whose composite is the identity by the adjunction triangle identity.
Thus $\pi_{M,P}$ is invertible on $\thick(H_S)$ by exactness; the
counit $\epsilon_P$ itself need not be invertible on an arbitrary
ambient object.  Taking $P=H_{S'}$ gives the comparison for $G_uF_uM$;
taking $P=W_{S'}$ gives the selected supported-action comparison.

For the supported map first use the finite-level nearby duality
pairing to identify $W_S=\widetilde D_SH_S$.  The proper duality
map is adjoint to the pairing
\[
 G_uH_{S'}\otimes G_u\widetilde D_{S'}H_{S'}
 \longrightarrow G_u(H_{S'}\otimes\widetilde D_{S'}H_{S'})
 \longrightarrow G_u\omega_{S'}(-1)[-2]
 \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Tr}_u}\omega_S(-1)[-2].
\]
It gives $G_uW_{S'}\simeq\widetilde D_SG_uH_{S'}\simeq W_S$.
Moving an element of $H_S$ from one input of this pairing to the
other gives the same map: this is associativity of the algebra
map $u^*H_S\to H_{S'}$ and the evaluation action.  Equivalently the
two composites from $H_S\otimes G_uW_{S'}$ to $W_S$ agree.  This
proves the $H_S$-linear mate and its compatibility with evaluation.
Reduction and rationalization are those of
Lemma~\ref{lem:selected-adic-comparisons}, including the pairings.

For an open or closed support restriction the maps are formed from
the same counits after base change.  Applying its restriction to
the displayed projection and pairing formulas gives their support
squares.  In particular the open-unit square, whose closed
restriction is the attaching map, is a square of modules; its
explicit mate is Equation~\eqref{eq:actual-pullback-attachment}.
This proves the individual module and support comparisons used below.
It does not by itself construct a single coherent diagram containing
all six-operation and kernel variances at once.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Comparison of the ambient gluing maps]
\label{lem:fibre-cartesian-ambient}
Let $\mathcal A_{J|I}(S')$ be the full subcategory of
$\mathcal A_J(S')$ whose restriction to the complete inverse image
of each coarse stratum belongs to its intrinsic cartesian category
of Definition~\ref{def:cell-category}.  Then actual module pullback
and direct image induce mutually inverse equivalences
\[
 \mathcal A_I(S)\simeq\mathcal A_{J|I}(S').
\]
In particular this compares the full mapping spectra of the extension
and localization terms for coarse supports, not only their restrictions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Adjunction and the fibre category.}
Work first in the ambient derived categories of module sheaves.  The
algebra map $u_S^*H_S\to H_{S'}$ gives the genuine adjunction
\[
 F=u_H^*=H_{S'}\otimes^L_{u_S^*H_S}u_S^*(-)
 \quad\dashv\quad
 G=Ru_{S,*},
\]
where $G$ restricts the action along $H_S\to Ru_{S,*}H_{S'}$.
The unit is the sheaf pullback/direct-image unit followed by the
algebra map; the counit is induced by the direct-image evaluation
$u_S^*Ru_{S,*}P\to P$ and the $H_{S'}$-action.

Over a coarse open stratum $u$ is an isomorphism.  Over a coarse node
$q$ write $u_q:Z_q\to q$ for its complete fibre.  The restricted left
adjoint is
\[
 F_q(M)=H_{Z_q}\otimes^L_{A_q}M.
\]
The actual algebra unit gives $A_q\simeq Ru_{q,*}H_{Z_q}$.
For $M\in\Perf(A_q)$, projection on the free module and then finite
cones and retracts proves that $M\to Ru_{q,*}F_q(M)$ is an
equivalence.  Hence $F_q$ is fully faithful.  Its thick image
contains $H_{Z_q}$, which generates the intrinsic cartesian fibre
category by Theorem~\ref{thm:cell-reconstruction}.  Thus $F_q$ is
an equivalence onto that category, with counit invertible there.
This proves the fibre statement without any arbitrary attaching maps.

\emph{Transport of arbitrary attachments.}
Let $j:U\to S$ be the union of coarse open strata and $i:Z\to S$
the closed node union.  Use $j',i'$ for their complete inverse images.
The map over $U$ is the identity.  Put
\[
 L(V)=i^*Rj_*V,\qquad L'(V)=i'^*Rj'_*V,\qquad
 F_Z=\prod_q F_q,\quad G_Z=Ru_{Z,*}.
\]
Ordinary proper base change and composition of right adjoints give
an actual natural equivalence
\[
 \lambda_V:G_ZL'(V)\xrightarrow{\sim}i^*G Rj'_*V
                    \xrightarrow{\sim}i^*Rj_*V=L(V).
\]
It uses the actual algebra map of Lemma~\ref{lem:cell-algebra-unit}.
The object $L'(V)$ need not be cartesian inside the exceptional
fibres: an external branch can contribute only at a terminal point.
The adjunction $F_Z\dashv G_Z$ below is therefore used on the full
ambient module categories, even though $F_Z$ is an equivalence only
on the specified fibre subcategories.

We verify which attachment is induced by actual pullback.
Let $\nu_E:E\to Rj_*j^*E$ be the localization unit, and define
\[
 \chi_V:F Rj_*V\longrightarrow Rj'_*V
\]
to be adjoint, under $j'^*\dashv Rj'_*$, to
$j'^*F Rj_*V\simeq j^*Rj_*V\to V$.
The last arrow is the open counit.  This defines $\chi$ on all
objects and morphisms, independently of generation.  Base change
for module pullback identifies $i'^*F=F_Zi^*$; set
$\kappa_V=i'^*\chi_V:F_ZL(V)\to L'(V)$.
The two maps
\[
 F E\xrightarrow{F\nu_E}F Rj_*V\xrightarrow{\chi_V}Rj'_*V,
 \qquad
 F E\xrightarrow{\nu'_{FE}}Rj'_*j'^*FE=Rj'_*V
\]
are the same: under the open adjunction both correspond to the
identity of $V$.  This is an equality of natural transformations
via the adjunction equivalence of mapping spaces.  Hence the
actual pullback of $(V,M,\alpha)$ has attachment
\[
 \beta_{\mathrm{geom}}=\kappa_V\circ F_Z\alpha.
\]

We next identify $\kappa$, rather than assuming it is the required
mate.  Denote the ambient units by $\eta$ and $\eta_Z$.  Consider
\[
 Rj_*V\xrightarrow{\eta}GF Rj_*V
          \xrightarrow{G\chi_V}G Rj'_*V\simeq Rj_*V.
\]
The right-adjoint composition $G Rj'_*\simeq Rj_*$ is the one
from the common open immersion; over $U$, both $F$ and $G$ are
the identity.  Thus the composite restricts to the identity of $V$.
Full faithfulness of $Rj_*$ identifies its mapping space with that
of $V$, so the composite is the identity, with its canonical homotopy.
Applying $i^*$, and the proper base-change square that carries
$\eta$ to $\eta_Z$, gives
\[
 \lambda_V\circ G_Z\kappa_V\circ\eta_{Z,L(V)}
                         =\operatorname{id}_{L(V)}.
\]
The adjoint of $\kappa_V$ is consequently $\lambda_V^{-1}$.
Writing the adjunction counit as $\epsilon_Z$ proves the explicit
identity
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_V=\epsilon_{Z,L'(V)}\circ F_Z(\lambda_V^{-1}),\qquad
 \beta_{\mathrm{geom}}
 =\epsilon_{Z,L'(V)}\circ F_Z(\lambda_V^{-1}\alpha).
 \label{eq:actual-pullback-attachment}
\end{equation}
This construction does not require $L'(V)$ to lie in the cartesian
fibre category.  Conversely, the adjoint of a fine attachment,
followed by $\lambda$, recovers $\alpha$.  The already proved fibre
equivalence identifies its closed object with $F_ZM$.  The triangle
identities therefore give inverse assignments on attachments and
on their full mapping spaces.

For clarity, the mapping spectrum between coarse triples is
\[
 \operatorname{fib}\left(
  \operatorname{Map}(V,V')\oplus\operatorname{Map}(M,M')
  \xrightarrow{\ (f,g)\mapsto L(f)\alpha-\alpha'g\ }
  \operatorname{Map}(M,L(V'))\right).
\]
The fine expression has $F_ZM,F_ZM'$ and $L'(V')$ in place of the
closed objects and link.  Fibre full faithfulness identifies the
second summand, and the ambient adjunction identifies
\[
 \operatorname{Map}(F_ZM,L'(V'))
   \simeq\operatorname{Map}(M,G_ZL'(V'))
   \simeq\operatorname{Map}(M,L(V')).
\]
Naturality of the mates identifies the difference maps.  Thus the
whole mapping spectra agree, including off-diagonal terms, and every
fine triple in the stated category is reconstructed from a coarse
one.  This proves full faithfulness and essential surjectivity of
actual $F$, not just of an abstract generator correspondence.
Equivalently its actual unit can be checked on all coarse strata by
proper base change: it is the identity on $U$ and the already proved
unit $M_q\to Ru_{q,*}F_qM_q$ on each $q$.  The strata are jointly
conservative on the underlying sheaf complexes.

\emph{Two refinements and a nontrivial fibre.}
For $S''\xrightarrow{v}S'\xrightarrow{u}S$, use the same coarse
open $U$ and its complete inverse images.  Write $F_u,F_v$ for
module pullback and keep the analogous closed restrictions.
The definition by the open counit gives
\[
 \chi_{uv,V}=\chi_{v,V}\circ F_v\chi_{u,V},\qquad
 \kappa_{uv,V}=\kappa_{v,V}\circ F_{v,Z}\kappa_{u,V}
\]
under $F_{uv}=F_vF_u$.  Indeed both composites are adjoint to the
identity over $U$.  Likewise the right-adjoint composition gives
$\lambda_{uv}=\lambda_u\circ G_{u,Z}\lambda_v$.
Consequently an arbitrary attachment satisfies
\[
 \beta_{uv}(\alpha)=\kappa_{v,V}F_{v,Z}(\kappa_{u,V}F_{u,Z}\alpha)
                  =\beta_v(\beta_u(\alpha)).
\]
These comparison homotopies are obtained in the functor adjunctions,
so for three refinements their associativity is that of composition
and its mates.  They are not choices made after an objectwise test.

For a concrete fibre with an internal node take thickness $6$,
$I=\{1,5\}$, $J=\{1,2,4,5\}$ and $K=\{1,2,3,4,5\}$.
The coarse node between cuts $1$ and $5$ has in the $J$ model a
complete fibre containing the components at $2$ and $4$ and their
internal node.  Choose arbitrary open coefficients $V$ on the two
coarse branches, $M\in\Perf(A_q)$, and an arbitrary
$\alpha:M\to L(V)$; no invertibility or diagonal condition is imposed.
Its fine closed coefficient is $H_{Z_q}\otimes_{A_q}^LM$, while
$L'(V)$ is supported at the two external attachment points and
vanishes on the fibre's interior, including the internal node.
Equation~\eqref{eq:actual-pullback-attachment} therefore transports
both arbitrary external attachments without making this link
cartesian.  Adding cut $3$ refines the internal node; the displayed
composition identity proves that its attachment is exactly that
of the direct $I\to K$ pullback.  This tests the off-diagonal
extension map, not just the coefficient $H$ or its cohomology.

\emph{Extension terms and boundary cells.}
The standard objects have triples $(V,0,0)$ and $(0,M,0)$, namely
$j_!V$ and $i_*M$.  Their pullbacks are $j'_!V$ and $i'_*F_ZM$.
These generate the fibrewise-cartesian ambient category by the actual
open/closed localization triangle and the fibre generation theorem;
they are not replaced by the single global generator $H_S$.
Their units are the open identity and the fibre unit, and their
counits are the corresponding open identity and fibre counit.
In particular
\[
 \operatorname{Map}(i_*M,j_!V[1])
       \simeq\operatorname{Map}(M,L(V))
       \simeq\operatorname{Map}(F_ZM,L'(V))
       \simeq\operatorname{Map}(i'_*F_ZM,j'_!V[1]).
\]
Using the same localization-triangle convention on both sides, its
extension class $\alpha$ maps to $\beta$.  Thus the actual boundary
and not merely the two terms is compared.  General coarse-support
localization follows by restriction of these squares; Verdier mates
give the dual triangles.  No equivalence with all of
$\mathcal A_J(S')$ is asserted.
\end{proof}

Define the cellular direct image on the profile over $I$ by actual
$Ru_{S,*}$ on every complete inverse-image support $S'$, followed by
its stratum restrictions and attaching data.  Module actions are
restricted along the algebra unit in Lemma~\ref{lem:cell-algebra-unit}.
This gives a concrete meaning to $\mu_*^{\mathrm{cell}}$; it is not a
Kan extension on an undecorated interval.

\begin{theorem}[Pairwise supported Morita comparison for integer cuts]
\label{thm:cellular-refinement}
For $I\subset J$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:cut-models} and every allowed
coarse support $S$ with its complete inverse image $S'$, actual direct
image induces equivalences
\[
 \mathcal C_J(S')\xrightarrow[Ru_{S,*}]{\sim}\mathcal C_I(S),
 \qquad
 \mathcal C_J^!(S')\xrightarrow[Ru_{S,*}]{\sim}\mathcal C_I^!(S).
\]
They identify relative tensor and the supported action by the projection
maps of Lemma~\ref{lem:cell-algebra-unit}, and Verdier partners by its
pairing.  Each coarse support-inclusion square and localization triangle
is compared by its actual proper base-change map and mate.  The retained
ambient mapping spectra are compared by
Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre-cartesian-ambient}.  The specified two-step
refinement and attachment comparisons agree with actual composition.
These functors identify the selected actual nearby and support
realizations.  This is a pairwise theorem with the displayed naturality
maps; it does not assert an equivalence in an as-yet-unconstructed
category of fully coherent six-operation/kernel profiles.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the module pullback
\[
 u_H^*M=H_{S'}\otimes^L_{u_S^*H_S}u_S^*M.
\]
It is an actual extension-of-scalars functor, not ordinary sheaf
pullback alone.  It sends $H_S$ to $H_{S'}$.  On finite-cell modules
the natural projection map identifies its adjunction unit as
\[
 M\longrightarrow Ru_{S,*}u_H^*M
 \simeq M\otimes_{H_S}Ru_{S,*}H_{S'}\simeq M.
\]
The projection map is an equivalence on the unit and hence on finite
cones and retracts; no projection assertion on all unbounded modules
is needed.  The algebra unit of Lemma~\ref{lem:cell-algebra-unit}
makes this a fully faithful functor.  Its image is thick and contains
$H_{S'}$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:cell-reconstruction}, which proved
intrinsic geometric generation on both supports, therefore gives
essential surjectivity.  Its inverse is the stated actual direct image.
The same argument, or the corresponding generator endomorphism
comparison, proves the Morita equivalence rather than just equality
of integrated complexes.

Proper Verdier duality commutes with $Ru_{S,*}$, with the actions
induced by the algebra unit.  It therefore gives the equivalence on
$\mathcal C^!$, and carries $W_{S'}$ to $W_S$ with its evaluation.
Equivalently one may use the mixed projection comparison on
$W_{S'}\otimes_{H_{S'}}u_H^*M$ and check it on $M=H_S$.
Extension of scalars is strong monoidal; its inverse on these proved
subcategories is consequently strong monoidal as well.  Thus tensor,
the mixed supported action and evaluation are intertwined.

For an inclusion of coarse supports, proper ordinary base change
identifies restriction before and after direct image; its Verdier
mate identifies costalk on the supported partners.  For a coarse
open/closed pair and its complete inverse image, composition of
shriek images and proper base change give, in the ambient categories,
\[
 Ru_*j'_!j'^*M'\simeq j_!j^*Ru_*M',\qquad
 Ru_*i'_*i'^*M'\simeq i_*i^*Ru_*M'.
\]
The dual identities hold for the second localization triangle.
They identify the units and counits themselves, since they are the
mates of the same geometric squares.  Applying the exact direct
image to their fibre/cofibre sequences therefore identifies the
boundary maps too.  Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre-cartesian-ambient} compares the mapping spectra
of these extension terms as well as their restrictions, so this compares the stated ambient gluing maps as well as the
object values.  A simultaneous higher profile statement is separated
below.
It does not assert an equivalence of the entire ambient constructible
categories, nor preservation of the star subcategory by every
extension term.

For $I\subset J\subset K$ the scheme maps compose as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:cut-models}.  Derived module pullback has its
canonical associativity cell, and the adjunction unit for a composite
is the composite unit.  The attachment identity and its two-step composition were proved
in Equation~\eqref{eq:actual-pullback-attachment} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre-cartesian-ambient}.  The above direct images and proper
base-change transformations were all formed from this one geometric
diagram, with the common generic fibre fixed.  These constructions
prove the stated two-step composition comparison.  The finite cut
poset supplies actual common refinements by union, and hence spans
of the pairwise equivalences.  We do not infer simultaneous higher
support/tensor/Verdier/kernel coherence from the two-step calculation.
In particular no unrestricted choice-independent profile equivalence
is claimed here.

Finally the natural map $u_H^*N_I(F)\to N_J(F)$ is an equivalence
on $F=Q$ and hence on the generated geometric source.  Its adjoint
is exactly proper nearby comparison; the supported version is its
proved Verdier mate.  This verifies the realization assertion for
the same functors used in the cellular equivalence.
\end{proof}

\begin{target}[The simultaneous enhanced profile comparison]
\label{target:enhanced-profile}
Construct the indexing correspondence category of cut models and
complete inverse-image supports, together with its tensor and kernel
operations, and a functor carrying it to the enhanced coefficient
categories.  Show that its structure maps are exactly the nearby,
projection, evaluation, base-change and attachment maps constructed
above.  Verify the mixed pasting cells for support inclusions and
refinements, their Verdier mates, all finite-arity tensor substitutions,
and the diagonal pushforward cells in the same construction.
\end{target}
The full target remains broader than the pairwise theorem.
Theorem~\ref{thm:anchored-coherent-descent} below proves the version with
a fixed coarse support index, its fibrewise-cartesian ambient layers,
and the specified minimal kernel diagrams.  It does not construct
an unrestricted correspondence category of all fine supports and kernels.
The target is not a hypothesis on the coefficient category.
The pairwise maps and their two-step identities provide its candidate
structure maps.  A list of commuting triangles is not a construction
of this entire enhanced diagram.

\subsection{The minimal kernel comparison, with support types retained}
For an allowed support $T$ let $\Delta_T:T\to T\times T$ be its
diagonal.  Kernels below carry the two actions of the pulled-back
algebra $H_T$.  The unit kernel is $\Delta_{T,*}H_T$ and its action is
\[
 M\longmapsto Rp_{2,!}
 \bigl(K\otimes^L_{p_1^*H_T}p_1^*M\bigr).
\]
For an open/closed pair $T=U\sqcup Z$ use also
$\Delta_{T,*}j_!H_U$ and $\Delta_{T,*}i_*H_Z$.

\begin{corollary}[Diagonal and normal-pair kernels descend]
\label{cor:cell-kernels}
The three kernels act respectively as the identity, $j_!j^*$ and
$i_*i^*$, with the actual localization boundary.  For complete
inverse-image supports their proper pushforwards along
$u_T\times u_T$ are the corresponding coarse kernels, with their
module actions and boundary maps.  Their actions and their Verdier
mates therefore intertwine the cellular equivalence in
Theorem~\ref{thm:cellular-refinement}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The diagonal projection formula over the actual algebra $H_T$ reduces
the three kernel actions to the indicated restriction/extension
functors.  Their triangle is the diagonal image of the ordinary
localization triangle of $H_T$.  Moreover
$(u_T\times u_T)\Delta_{T'}=\Delta_Tu_T$ and both $u_T$ and the
diagonals are proper.  Hence the pushed unit is
$\Delta_{T,*}Ru_{T,*}H_{T'}=\Delta_{T,*}H_T$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:cell-algebra-unit}.  The two other kernels follow
from the open/closed identities in the preceding proof.  Those
identities preserve the boundary because its arbitrary extension
class is carried by Equation~\eqref{eq:actual-pullback-attachment};
the two-refinement computation in Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre-cartesian-ambient}
identifies its composites.  Applying Verdier duality to this same
localization triangle gives the asserted mates.
Under the proved Morita presentations these are the associated
bimodules and their actual tensor actions.
\end{proof}
This is the promised minimal kernel interface.  It does not provide a
category of all relative-product kernels or prove general convolution
closure and associativity.  The projector terms have their recorded
support types; they are not all endofunctors of a single cartesian
coefficient category.

For example, when $m=4$ the incomparable models with cuts $\{1\}$
and $\{2,3\}$ have the actual common refinement $\{1,2,3\}$.
The theorem gives pairwise comparisons along these proper maps.
Supports on the incomparable models can be compared this way only
when their complete inverse images agree on the common refinement;
arbitrary different supports are not identified.  It is compatible with a supplied finite group action on the
whole geometric diagram by the equivariant-unit argument of
Corollary~\ref{cor:nearby-finite-descent}.  Constructing unsupplied
lifts, adding arbitrary ramified base changes, retaining the infinite
base Galois action, and identifying analytic/relative-root and integral
realizations remain outside this fixed-thickness rational theorem.

\section{Coherent descent of a fixed coarse support diagram}
\label{sec:anchored-descent}
Fix a cut set $B$ and let $\mathcal P_B$ be the finite poset of cut
sets $J\supset B$, with arrows towards finer cuts.  Let $\mathcal L_B$
be the poset of locally closed unions of strata of $E_B$.  Our actual
support index has objects $(J,S)$, $J\in\mathcal P_B$, $S\in\mathcal L_B$;
its scheme at this object is $S_J=u_{JB}^{-1}S$.  An inclusion
$S\subset T$ gives $S_J\subset T_J$, and a refinement gives the proper
map $S_K\to S_J$.  These squares are cartesian by complete inverse
image.  Intersections, complements in open/closed decompositions, and
all repeated inverse images are taken as schemes.  This index does
not add arbitrary new fine supports to the coarse support set.

At $(J,S)$ use four types of coefficients:
\[
 \mathcal A_{J|B}(S_J),\quad
 \mathcal A_{J|B}^{!}(S_J):=\widetilde D_{S_J}
                         \mathcal A_{J|B}(S_J),\quad
 \mathcal C_J(S_J),\quad\mathcal C_J^!(S_J).
\]
Here $\mathcal A_{B|B}=\mathcal A_B$.  The ambient category is the
intrinsic fibrewise-cartesian category of
Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre-cartesian-ambient}; arbitrary attachments between
coarse strata are retained.  A duality is typed as a functor from the
opposite category.  This avoids treating Verdier duality as covariant.

The support diagram consists of the following specified operations:
ordinary restriction on the star ambient categories; $j_!$
and $i_*$ for coarse open/closed decompositions; the two localization
triangle functors into the ambient categories; their normalized Verdier
conjugates on the supported ambient categories; relative tensor on
$\mathcal C$, its action on $\mathcal C^!$, and the typed evaluations
$M^{\vee_H}\otimes_HM\to H$ and
$M^{\vee_H}\otimes_H(W\otimes_HM)\to W$.
Include $R\Gamma$ and $R\Gamma_c$ with values in the fixed category
$D(Q)$, and the specified global evaluation/trace pairings.
Ambient Verdier duality includes its biduality and localization cells;
no tensor-dualizability of every ambient object is assumed.  Include
the embeddings of these smaller categories into the ambient ones.
Include the three diagonal/localization kernel actions of
Corollary~\ref{cor:cell-kernels}, with their displayed boundaries.
Include the attaching mapping bifunctor
$(M,V)\mapsto\operatorname{Map}(M,L(V))$ with values in spectra.
It is compared on the fine model by
$\operatorname{Map}(F_ZM,L'(V))$, not by requiring $L'(V)$ to be
cartesian inside the contracted fibre.  Star $Rj_*$ is not asserted
to preserve the fibrewise-cartesian ambient category.  Its supported
Verdier counterpart occurs in the dual localization triangle.
These are specified kernel diagrams and actions, not a category of all
kernels with arbitrary convolution.  At $B$ these operations and their
cells are the actual enhanced module-sheaf operations used above.

\begin{definition}[The operation diagram being transported]
\label{def:anchored-operation-diagram}
For the fixed $B$, take as types the four categories at each
$S\in\mathcal L_B$ displayed above and their opposite categories,
together with the fixed targets $D(Q)$ and spectra.  Include the
functor categories of arrows and cofiber sequences in each ambient
type, with their evaluation functors; these type the localization
triangle and its connecting arrow.  For any list of input types and
output type the operation object is the full infinity-category
\[
 \operatorname{Fun}(\mathcal D_{c_1}\times\cdots\times
                         \mathcal D_{c_n},\mathcal D_d).
\]
Substitution is functor composition.  These operation objects define
a $\mathrm{Cat}_\infty$-enriched operation structure.  They are not
replaced by their maximal subgroupoids: noninvertible natural
transformations, in particular localization arrows, are retained.

Fix its base subdiagram as follows.  Its operations are precisely the
support, localization, embedding, duality, relative tensor, global
section, evaluation and attaching bifunctors enumerated above, and
the three specified kernel actions.  Include their identities and
finite substitutions.  Its cells are the actual adjunction units and
counits, support composition/base-change cells, cofiber-sequence cells,
biduality, tensor substitution and evaluation cells from the fixed
base enhancement, with their simplices and substitutions.  Equivalently
one may take the subdiagram generated by this specified collection
inside the displayed functor infinity-categories.  This fixes an
operation diagram, not all operations on all coefficient categories.
The three kernel objects themselves retain the separately specified
proper-pushforward diagram, not an extra invertible kernel-category type.

At $J\supset B$ replace the four coefficient types by their stated
fibrewise-cartesian versions on $S_J$, take opposites and triangle
categories functorially, and keep $D(Q)$ and spectra fixed.  Refinement
acts by the module equivalences, their Verdier conjugates and the
specified comparison transformations in the following theorem.
Higher cells on the fine operation diagram will be transported from
this fixed base subdiagram; they are not arbitrary preassigned fine cells.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Transport of a typed operation diagram]
\label{lem:typed-operation-transport}
Let a fixed enhanced diagram have coefficient categories $\mathcal D_c$
indexed by types $c$, with specified functors of finite arity and
specified diagrams of natural transformations.  Suppose
$e_{j,c}:\mathcal D_c\simeq\mathcal D_{j,c}$ are equivalences forming
a diagram in $j$, and each operation at $j$ is equipped with a
specified equivalence to the conjugate of the operation at the base.
Then the conjugate diagram supplies coherent cells on these operations,
including simultaneous mixed cells and their higher relations.
This statement also applies to opposite categories and to operations
valued in categories of triangles.  It does not assert that arbitrary
previously chosen cells coincide with the transported ones.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each list of input types and one output type, composition with
$e$ and its inverse gives an equivalence of functor categories
\[
 \operatorname{Fun}(\mathcal D_{c_1}\times\cdots\times
 \mathcal D_{c_n},\mathcal D_d)
 \simeq
 \operatorname{Fun}(\mathcal D_{j,c_1}\times\cdots\times
 \mathcal D_{j,c_n},\mathcal D_{j,d}).
\]
Use the unit and counit of these equivalences in substitutions.
Their triangle identities identify successive substitutions, and the
associativity of functor composition supplies all iterated substitutions.
Equivalently these equivalences identify the colored operation
objects, which are the displayed full functor infinity-categories, with
composition given by substitution.  Noninvertible natural transformations
are arrows in these categories and remain present throughout.  Transport the whole base diagram through this
identification, then through the specified equivalences of operations.
This transports simplices, not just objects or edges.  The fibre of an
equivalence of mapping spaces over a specified simplex is contractible,
so this introduces no additional independent choices of higher cells.
The same construction in functor categories handles triangles, and
opposites handle the contravariant operations.  Functorial equivalences
$e_{j,c}$ make the construction simultaneous in $j$.  No claim about
a contractible space of all possible geometric enhancements is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Coherent refinement descent with a fixed coarse support index]
\label{thm:anchored-coherent-descent}
For the fixed-thickness integer-cut family, the preceding typed support
diagram admits a coherent enhancement under refinement in $\mathcal P_B$.
Its star transition is actual derived module pullback and its inverse
is actual proper direct image.  On supported types the transition is
its normalized Verdier conjugate, again inverse to proper direct image.
It retains the ambient localization terms, the specified support maps,
tensor and evaluation, and the three minimal kernel comparisons.
The higher mixed cells are those transported from the fixed base
operation diagram of Definition~\ref{def:anchored-operation-diagram}.
The listed elementary support/refinement squares are identified with
the actual base-change mates; no equality with every independently
prescribed fine enhancement is claimed.
All these refinement edges can be inverted coherently.  This proves
descent for this fixed coarse support index, not equivalence with all
fine ambient categories or with a profile of arbitrary fine supports.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $F_{JB}=H_J\otimes_{u_{JB}^*H_B}u_{JB}^*(-)$, restricted to
$S_J$ where appropriate.  The nearby algebra maps in
Lemma~\ref{lem:cell-algebra-unit} are obtained from the generic inverse-
image diagram and its adjunction counits.  Thus for a string of actual
model maps their composites are the mates of that same composite
counit.  Constructing mates on the functor categories of strings gives
a diagram of algebra pullbacks, rather than independently choosing
pairwise algebra maps.  Derived tensor associativity then defines
$F_{KB}\simeq F_{KJ}F_{JB}$ on all strings.  The threefold associativity
cell is the associativity cell for this single iterated tensor product;
the pentagon and its higher versions are those of that tensor product.
These are the requisite functorial equivalences $e_{J,c}$.

Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre-cartesian-ambient} proves that $F_{JB}$ is an
equivalence $\mathcal A_B(S)\simeq\mathcal A_{J|B}(S_J)$.
Its restriction gives the equivalence on $\mathcal C$.
On the supported types define the covariant functor by the typed
composition
\[
 \mathcal A_B^!(S)
 \xrightarrow{\ \widetilde D_S\ }\mathcal A_B(S)^{\mathrm{op}}
 \xrightarrow{\ F_{JB}^{\mathrm{op}}\ }
                  \mathcal A_{J|B}(S_J)^{\mathrm{op}}
 \xrightarrow{\ \widetilde D_{S_J}\ }\mathcal A_{J|B}^!(S_J).
\]
We denote it by $F_{JB}^{!}$; its restriction gives the same formula
on $\mathcal C^!$.  Thus the middle functor is explicitly the opposite
of the star functor.  Proper duality identifies its inverse with $Ru_{JB,*}$,
by the explicit evaluation/trace pairing of
Lemma~\ref{lem:cell-algebra-unit}.  This also defines its composition
cells; ordinary pullback is not substituted for this supported transition.

We specify the conjugation maps for the operation diagram.  For a
support inclusion $k:S\to T$ they are
\[
 k_J^*F_{JB}\simeq F_{JB}k^*,\qquad
 k_J^!F_{JB}^{!}\simeq F_{JB}^{!}k^!.
\]
The first is inverse-image/tensor associativity on the actual cartesian
square, and the second is its Verdier mate.  For a coarse open/closed
pair, $F_{JB}j_!\simeq j_{J,!}F_{JB}$ and
$F_{JB}i_*\simeq i_{J,*}F_{JB}$ follow from extension by zero and
closed base change.  The map
\[
 F_{JB}Rj_*\xrightarrow{\chi_{JB}}Rj_{J,*}F_U
\]
is the mate of the open counit, but need not be invertible.  Its
fine target can be supported at the ends of a contracted fibre and
need not belong to $\mathcal A_{J|B}$.  We therefore compare the
attaching mapping bifunctor instead: the actual proper comparison
$G_ZL'(V)\simeq L(V)$ and the module adjunction give
\[
 \operatorname{Map}(F_ZM,L'(V))\simeq
 \operatorname{Map}(M,G_ZL'(V))\simeq\operatorname{Map}(M,L(V)).
\]
These are equivalences of bifunctors with values in spectra.
Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre-cartesian-ambient} proves that their mate sends
an attachment to its actual pullback via the closed restriction of
$\chi_{JB}$.  Thus the required operation is conjugated even though
the external link itself is not.  The dual ambient localization
operations are obtained by Verdier conjugation of $j_!$ and $i_*$,
so no closure under star $Rj_*$ has been inserted.
The localization triangle is transported as the cofibering of its
specified unit/counit.  Tensor conjugation is the associativity map
of relative tensor; the mixed supported action is the projection mate
specified in Lemma~\ref{lem:cell-algebra-unit}.  Evaluation conjugation
is its displayed trace pairing.  The global-section operations are
conjugated by $R\Gamma(S_J,-)=R\Gamma(S,Ru_*(-))$ and
$R\Gamma_c(S_J,-)=R\Gamma_c(S,Ru_*(-))$, since $u$ is proper,
followed by the proved unit equivalence on the corresponding type.
Their scalar target has identity transition.  This also types the
global pairings without assuming an equivalence of unrestricted
ordinary sheaf categories.  Each minimal kernel is the diagonal
image of one of these localization terms.  The identity
$(u\times u)\Delta_J=\Delta_Bu$ and composition of direct images give
its specified pushforward conjugation, including the action and boundary.
More precisely these kernels are the finite diagram
$\Delta_*j_!H_U\to\Delta_*H_T\to\Delta_*i_*H_Z$.
At a finer model they are its actual module pullbacks before applying
that model's diagonal.  Proper pushforward of the diagonal diagram
uses $Ru_*F_u\simeq\mathrm{id}$ on the three ambient terms.  The
unit for a composite is the composite unit, so the resulting kernel
comparisons on every refinement string form a cartesian section of
the proper-pushforward diagram of kernel objects.  Their actions are
the already transported localization operations.  We do not apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:typed-operation-transport} to the entire kernel categories,
nor claim full faithfulness of forgetting a diagonal bimodule action.
No kernel pullback equivalence on an unrestricted product category is used.

These explicit conjugations verify the hypotheses of
Lemma~\ref{lem:typed-operation-transport}.  Apply it to the entire
base operation diagram at once.  This constructs the higher mixed cells,
not just checks of its separate triangles.  For example, pasting a
support square with a refinement square gives on star restriction the
same inverse-image/tensor associativity map in either order.  Taking
the counit mate gives exactly the mixed attachment square involving
$\chi$ (without declaring it invertible); dualizing gives
the costalk square; taking the cofibering gives its boundary square.
The projection and evaluation formulas identify their tensor and
Verdier variants with these transported cells.  Thus the elementary
geometric squares have their original arrows.  Higher mixed cells are
the transported cells just constructed; we do not claim uniqueness of
an unrelated enhancement of all six operations.

Finally the coefficient/operation diagram, whose refinement arrows
are equivalences, factors
through the localization of its index at these arrows.  This is the
universal property of categorical localization, applied to the entire
typed diagram.  Its inverse arrows are the actual $Ru_*$ with the
proved units and counits.  The poset $\mathcal P_B$ has initial object
$B$ and terminal full cut; unions give actual common refinements.
The resulting coefficient and operation identifications are therefore
independent of the path within this anchored index, with the constructed
higher cells.  The selected kernel objects have the compatible cartesian
section just described; no ambient kernel-category arrow is inverted.  Changing
the anchor to a finer model compares the old diagram on the corresponding
fibrewise-cartesian subcategories, not with the whole new ambient layer.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A loop is a new coefficient problem]
\label{rem:loop-monodromy-gate}
The forest argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:cell-reconstruction} is essential.
For a constant diagram of coefficient categories on a graph with one
cycle, take the unit at every vertex and identity attachments except
one scalar automorphism $\lambda\in Q^\times$, $\lambda\ne1$.
Every branch map is invertible, but the product around the cycle is
$\lambda$.  This object is not generated thickly by the global unit:
finite extensions, cones and retracts of the unit have only generalized
eigenvalue $1$ for the cycle monodromy, whereas this object has
monodromy $\lambda$.  This is an obstruction for the categorical graph
model, not a claimed geometric realization on every nodal curve.
For general skeleta one must retain the diagram and its global
monodromy, or prove a geometric global-unipotence restriction and
its operation closure.  The anchored theorem above makes neither claim.
\end{remark}
