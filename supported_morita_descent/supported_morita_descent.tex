\begin{abstract}
We study geometric rational nearby-cycle modules on proper nodal
curves over a fixed trait. A geometric constructibility profile
specifies marked components, unipotent chain blocks and a finite
system of supports. For actual integer-cut modifications we prove
that these conditions select equivalent coefficient categories in
every presentation, without choosing a coarse anchor. The comparisons
retain arbitrary exterior attachments, horizontal and loop monodromy,
localization boundaries and geometric Verdier pairings.
On a source generated by support graphs and coefficient rectangles,
an actual one-sided kernel counit gives units, strong convolution
and a multi-object Morita realization. The same method handles
finite etale support spans in existing presentations of a fixed
cover diagram. A triangular cubic supplies continuous loop holonomy,
a nonzero boundary, and both rectangular and coupled noninvertible
kernels. An off-diagonal calculation shows why arbitrary independent
contraction of the endpoints of a coupled kernel fails.
The results concern the specified nearby-module support system;
general log/root sources and higher-dimensional modifications are
outside their scope.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Fix a complete strictly henselian discrete valuation ring $R$ with
algebraically closed residue field $k$, uniformizer $t$, and
$\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k$.  Fix a completed algebraic closure of its
fraction field and compatible base roots.  We begin with proper nodal
models of a smooth generic curve, with local equations $xy=t^m$,
$m\geq1$.  The intended modifications are actual integer-cut toroidal
modifications over this fixed trait and their composites.  Within one connected integer-cut component,
Section~\ref{sec:profiles} constructs the common refinement and proves
descent; arbitrary rational subdivisions are not included.

A support profile specifies finite diagrams of actual locally closed
special-fibre supports and open/closed decompositions.  Presentations
are to be compared on complete inverse-image supports in common
refinements.  No coarse model is fixed for the entire theory.  A new
fine support need not represent the same profile as an old support.
The initial kernel products are over $k$; a relative kernel theory over
$R$ is a separate extension.

The invariant sought is a diagram of categories and operations,
\[
 \bigl(\mathcal C_M(S),\mathcal C_M^D(S),
 j^*,j_!,Rj_*,i^*,i^!,i_*,D,\mathrm{ev},\mathrm{Tr}\bigr),
\]
restricted to operations for which preservation and finiteness are
proved.  The notation does not assume that all six operations preserve
one small coefficient category.

\subsection{The two main results}
The coefficient algebra on a model $M$ is $H_M=R\Psi_M\Qell$.
Its restriction to a smooth stratum is the constant algebra; its
node stalk is the cochain algebra of the geometric vanishing
annulus. We retain sheaf modules and their attaching maps, rather
than replacing the whole curve by one cohomology algebra.

\paragraph{Theorem A: coefficients with supports.}
A profile marks geometric divisorial valuations on the canonical
finest model. Unmarked components form rational chains; their
coefficients are unipotent with invertible internal branch maps.
Marked open strata have arbitrary finite-rank lisse coefficients,
and all attachments between pieces are retained. Supports are
specified unions and differences of whole pieces.
Theorem~\ref{thm:profile-descent} compares the resulting full
coefficient categories for every presentation in one integer-cut
component, together with their support diagrams and Verdier partners.
The profile is defined geometrically before the descent theorem;
Lemma~\ref{lem:profile-images} proves that all presentations select
the same objects on the finest model.

The two coefficient types have distinct roles. Ordinary modules
are transported by
$F_u=H_{M'}\otimes_{u^*H_M}^Lu^*(-)$, with inverse $G_u=Ru_*$
on the selected image. Their supported partners are obtained by
geometric Verdier duality $D$ and transported by $F_u^D=DF_uD$.
Restriction and extension by zero use the ordinary type; their
Verdier mates use the supported type. The failed ordinary $Rj_*$
comparison in Example~\ref{ex:right-open-obstruction} explains
why this distinction is necessary.

\paragraph{Theorem B: geometric kernel comparisons.}
The initial kernel source is generated by support graphs and
rectangles of these coefficients, with all actual kernel maps.
For a model change, Theorem~\ref{thm:profile-kernel-descent}
proves the actual counit
\[
 U_b\star(V_b\star P\star U_a)\longrightarrow P\star U_a
\]
is invertible in the kernel category. Inserting this map proves
strong convolution, and the diagonal gives the regular unit.
Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel-morita-verdier} then gives the Morita
realization and its geometric Verdier mates. It does not infer
kernel equivalences from equivalences of their actions.

Section~\ref{sec:independent-curves} keeps the same curve setting
and distinguishes two extensions of B. Rectangles allow independently
chosen presentations of several curves. Finite etale support spans
allow coupled kernels, but the fixed generic cover diagram must
have actual finite etale model presentations. Normalization
uniqueness proves that their modification squares are Cartesian.
The source is independently specified by those geometric models;
existence on every possible coarse model is not a theorem here.

\subsection{Proof structure and inputs}
Sections~\ref{sec:local-source}--\ref{sec:supported-source}
identify the local cochain source, compare coordinate torsors, and
reconstruct actual modules by recollement, including mapping spectra.
Section~\ref{sec:curve-modification} proves one modification at a
time: the exceptional chain is controlled by its unit, while the
exterior attaching maps are compared by an ambient adjunction.
Section~\ref{sec:profiles} constructs the finest model and proves
the common image needed for A. Section~\ref{sec:profile-kernels}
proves boundedness and the actual counit before using kernel or
Verdier formalism. Section~\ref{sec:actual-loop} constructs the
cyclic test curve first, then its rectangular and finite-cover kernels.
The examples are applications, not inputs to the descent proofs.

The local node comparison and rational unipotent interface use
\cite{Local}; the integer-cut fibre reconstruction uses
\cite{Refinement}; the ambient enhanced kernel operations use
\cite{Kernels}. The results invoked from these papers are named
in the input table in Section~\ref{sec:inputs}, with archived source
versions and the hypotheses used here. The new common-image,
support-preservation and enlarged-source kernel-counit arguments
are proved here. All products of
special-fibre supports are over $k$.

\subsection{Related work}
Haine--Porta--Teyssier prove that coarsening an exodromic
stratification localizes its exit-path infinity-category at the
arrows made invertible by the coarsening
\cite[Theorem 0.3.1(3)]{HPT}. This is a general precedent for the
localization mechanism. We do not verify the hypotheses needed to
apply that theorem directly to our enhanced etale nearby-algebra
module categories. Instead, the work here identifies the actual
coefficient source, the image of each exceptional chain and its
compatibility with the specified support operations.
Curry's subdivision theorem identifies cellular sheaf cohomology
before and after pullback to a subdivision
\cite[Theorem 6.12]{Curry}. Relative to that particular invariance
statement, our comparison retains full module mapping spectra and
actual exterior attachments, as well as localization boundaries and
Verdier pairings. We do not claim subdivision invariance itself as a
new formal mechanism; these geometric and supported checks are the
content required in the present coefficient setting.

\section{Coordinate changes and actual local coefficients}
\label{sec:local-source}
Put $Q=\Qell$ and $\Lambda_r=\mathbf Z/\ell^r$.
All nearby cycles in this section are geometric: no extra base-inertia
representation is added.  Let $M/R$ be one of the actual nodal models
above, put $Y=M_s$, and let $i:Z\hookrightarrow Y$ be its finite node
locus and $j:U\hookrightarrow Y$ its smooth complement.  The algebra
\[
 H=R\Psi_M Q,\qquad j^*H\simeq Q_U,\qquad A_q=i_q^*H
 \quad(q\in Z)
\]
is defined by actual nearby cycles and their lax monoidal structure.
The smooth restriction uses the actual specialization unit.
These nearby algebras are used with their commutative enhanced
structures. Ordinary coefficients are right modules; their Verdier
partners are right modules over the opposite algebra, equivalently
left modules over the original algebra. A kernel from $a$ to $b$
is left $H_a$ and right $H_b$. When tensoring two ordinary
coefficients, or forming a rectangle, commutativity supplies the
required left action with the usual symmetry signs. Verdier duality
transposes actions; it does not assert a scalar tensor-dual formula
for an arbitrary constructible coefficient.
We use the stable categories of sheaf modules over these algebras,
with underlying bounded constructible $Q$-complexes when specified.
No model contraction enters these definitions.

\subsection{The unit torsor must be retained}
For an invertible function $f$ on a scheme or analytic domain on which
$\ell$ is invertible, write
\[
 T_n(f)=\{z:z^{\ell^n}=f\},\qquad G_n=\mu_{\ell^n}.
\]
This is the actual right $G_n$-torsor.  For a node coordinate $x$
these torsors are taken on the geometric generic locus, where $x$
is invertible; a unit torsor $T_n(a)$ extends etale over the chart.
Write
$T_n(f)\mathbin{\wedge^{G_n}}T_n(g)$ for the quotient of their
product by $(s,t)\mapsto(s\zeta,t\zeta^{-1})$.
Its residual $G_n$-action is multiplication in either factor.

\begin{lemma}[Unit-coordinate torsors and their descent cells]
\label{lem:unit-torsor-overlap}
On an overlap where $x'=a x$ with $a$ an invertible regular function,
there is a canonical torsor isomorphism
\[
 m_{a,x}:T_n(a)\mathbin{\wedge^{G_n}}T_n(x)
       \xrightarrow{\sim}T_n(x'),\qquad [s,z]\longmapsto sz.
\]
These maps commute with $T_{n+1}(f)\to T_n(f)$ and with restriction.
For $x''=b x'$, their two composites agree through the canonical
contracted-product associator and $m_{b,a}$.  In a node chart
$xy=t^m$, compatible base roots $t_n^{\ell^n}=t$ give
\[
 T_n(x)\longrightarrow T_n(y),\qquad z\longmapsto t_n^m/z,
\]
where the group action is inverted.  These comparisons, including
branch interchange and unit changes together, satisfy all the cells
induced by multiplication and inversion of the torsors.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Multiplication is invariant under the displayed antidiagonal action.
After an etale cover trivializing the two torsors, it is the identity
comparison between two $G_n$-torsors, hence an isomorphism before that
cover by descent.  On three factors both composites send
$[r,s,z]$ to $rsz$.  On any number of factors all parenthesizations
are induced by this same multiplication map; its associativity gives
the higher coherence as well as the three-factor identity.
Raising to the $\ell$th power commutes with these maps.  The formula
for interchange has $\ell^n$th power $t^m/x=y$, and sends $z\zeta$
to $(t_n^m/z)\zeta^{-1}$.  Its square is the identity.  Compatibility
with units is the identity $(az)^{-1}=a^{-1}z^{-1}$ on the contracted
products.  All these are identities of actual maps of torsors.
\end{proof}

For a representation $V$ of $G_n$, our associated sheaf convention is
$(z\zeta,v)\sim(z,\zeta v)$.  Choosing a local root $s$ of $a$
identifies $T_n(x)$ with $T_n(ax)$ by $z\mapsto sz$.
Changing $s$ to $s\zeta$ changes the associated-sheaf comparison by
$\rho(\zeta)$.  This is its descent datum, not an ambiguity to discard.
Equivalently the comparison twists the coefficient by $T_n(a)$.
In particular the two functors using $x$ and $ax$ are not assigned
an identity natural transformation on constant representation labels.
Explicitly, if $x_j=a_{ij}x_i$ and local roots $s_{ij}$ are chosen,
then on a triple overlap
\[
 \zeta_{ijk}=s_{jk}s_{ij}s_{ik}^{-1}\in G_n,
 \qquad \phi_{jk}\phi_{ij}=\rho(\zeta_{ijk})\phi_{ik}.
\]
The contracted-product comparison retains precisely this transition;
one cannot set $\zeta_{ijk}=1$ merely by citing associativity.
On four overlaps the corresponding cocycle identity follows from
multiplication of the $s_{ij}$.  Thus the construction descends the
torsor-twisted presentations, not constant labels with their
transition data erased.

\begin{proposition}[Coefficient passage and intrinsic vertical unipotence]
\label{prop:coordinate-coefficients}
The torsor comparisons induce comparisons of actual associated
coefficient sheaves, their tensors, duals, and evaluation maps, with
the preceding descent cells.  They extend to continuous adic local
systems with a stable finite lattice and to their bounded derived
unipotent extensions.  On a strict nodal germ, the category of local
coefficients with unipotent relative Kummer action is independent of
an ordered node coordinate.  This does not assert that its horizontal
descent datum is constant on a larger overlap.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At each finite precision a finite continuous representation of the
relative $\ell$-Kummer inertia factors through some $G_n$.  The associated-sheaf construction carries the
actual maps of Lemma~\ref{lem:unit-torsor-overlap} and their identities
to maps and identities of coefficient sheaves.  The same construction
on $V\otimes W$ and $V^\vee$ identifies the maps with tensor and dual
comparisons: the transitions on the dual are $\rho(\zeta)^{-\vee}$,
so evaluation descends.  There is no averaging argument here.

For a lattice $V_\bullet$, choose a factoring level separately at
each precision and use a common refinement for each comparison.
The torsor maps commute with all such refinements and with coefficient
reduction.  They therefore define morphisms of cartesian adic systems,
with their already defined cells.  Equivalences are tested componentwise
in that category.  Rationalize only afterwards.  Finite Postnikov
extensions are taken in the actual derived sheaf categories, where
these exact tensor comparisons already exist.  The finite-action
derived descent input of \cite{Local} permits finite coefficient
diagrams to be represented at common levels when required; a single
finite level for a whole integral system is not asserted.

The relative characteristic group of $xy=t^m$ is
$P_m^{\mathrm{gp}}/\mathbf Z[t]\simeq\mathbf Z$; its geometric
$\ell$-Kummer inertia is its hom into $\mathbf Z_\ell(1)$.
Multiplication of a log chart by a unit does not change this group.
An ordered branch chooses a generator; interchange negates it.
Unipotence means that a finite-dimensional representation admits a
finite filtration with trivial relative-inertia quotients.  This
condition is invariant under these group identifications and under
conjugation; it does not require a chosen logarithm or generator.

There is also a geometric verification that unit twists do not add
vertical monodromy on a strict germ.  A unit in a strictly henselian
local ring with $\ell$ invertible has an $\ell$th root: lift a root
in the separably closed residue field by Hensel's lemma.  Applying
this recursively constructs compatible roots of the unit on that
strict germ.  Thus its Kummer tower there is trivializable, although
not canonically trivialized.  On a larger overlap it need not be
trivial; its transition sheaves must be retained.  This argument
uses a strict germ, not a claim that one finite etale cover
trivializes every root level globally.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The actual node category and branch maps}
For a geometric node $q$, let $\mathcal U(T_q)$ denote the full
bounded derived category on its actual geometric vanishing annulus
whose cohomology local systems have a finite filtration with constant
quotients.  This is an intrinsic condition on actual local systems;
it is not defined by being in the image of a Morita functor.

\begin{proposition}[Node source and geometric Morita comparison]
\label{prop:node-source}
There is an equivalence induced by the actual cochain functor
\[
 \mathcal U(T_q)\xrightarrow{\ R\Gamma\ }\Perf(A_q).
\]
For each of the two strict punctured branches $b$ at $q$, the actual
nearby open-unit map
\[
 \beta_b:A_q\longrightarrow B_b:=R\Gamma(\eta_b,Q)
\]
is an equivalence of cochain algebras.  The two branch orientations
are opposite.  These statements and their comparison functors
respect unit-coordinate changes by the torsor descent above.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
 \cite{Local} supplies the fixed-node comparison;
 \cite[\texttt{prop:all-thickness-nearby}]{Refinement} supplies the
 all-thickness calculation.  Together they identify the actual
 constant-coefficient maps.  The node and branch
algebras have $Q$ in degree zero, $Q(-1)$ in degree one, and no other
cohomology.  On a branch with parameter $x$ the degree-one map is
its primitive Kummer class.  On the opposite branch $y$ it is its
negative, since $[x]+[y]=m[t]$ and the geometric base class vanishes.
There is no factor $m$ in the relative generator.  These are the
maps induced by the nearby open unit, not chosen cohomology
isomorphisms.  Consequently they are algebra equivalences.

The actual annulus cochain comparison has the same primitive Kummer
class and identifies its unit endomorphism algebra with $A_q$.
A unipotent local system is a finite extension of constant sheaves;
finite Postnikov towers put every object of $\mathcal U(T_q)$ in
the thick closure of $Q$.  Conversely cohomology of a finite cone
or retract of these objects still has unipotent monodromy.  Thus the
condition defining $\mathcal U(T_q)$ equals that thick closure.
Evaluation gives the usual Morita comparison on its generator;
exactness in both variables extends the mapping equivalence and
essential image to the two thick closures.  This proves the stated
equivalence with $\Perf(A_q)$, on full mapping spectra.

Under $x'=ax$, the Kummer class changes by $[a]$.  The torsors of
$a$ extend etale over the chart, so their specialization is the
actual torsor of the specialized unit.  On a strict branch this
unit has compatible roots and has zero residue.  This preserves the
primitive branch calculation, but does not set $[a]=0$ on a whole
horizontal overlap.  Lemma~\ref{lem:unit-torsor-overlap} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:coordinate-coefficients} give the associated
functor comparisons and their cells before cohomology is taken.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Naturality of links and pairings on overlaps]
\label{lem:overlap-mates}
The comparisons just constructed commute with actual nearby
open-unit maps, restrictions to a branch, and their Verdier mates.
Their compatibility on triple overlaps is the compatibility of
these actual natural transformations, including mapping homotopies.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An etale change of chart identifies nearby cycles by etale base
change.  This is the comparison obtained by restricting the generic
and special sheaf sites.  The square involving $F\to Rj_*j^*F$
commutes because, after the open adjunction, both paths are the
same open counit applied to the same coefficient map.  Closed
restriction gives the branch square.  For a composite of restrictions
this is the composite counit square.  It defines the cells on strings
of restrictions, so no new cells are chosen after computing stalks.
The tensor comparison of the torsor functors preserves evaluation.
On bounded constructible sheaves Verdier duality takes these squares
to their adjoint-mate squares and preserves their composition cells;
its trace is the actual trace for the geometric support map.
These assertions concern sheaves already compared by actual maps,
not a claim that arbitrary equivalences of cohomology lift to maps.
\end{proof}

\section{An intrinsic supported nearby source}
\label{sec:supported-source}
This section specifies what ``actual coefficients'' means for the
first result.  The source is a category of actual constructible
nearby-algebra modules on $Y$, with a geometric supported generation
statement below.  It is not claimed to be a full category of generic
sheaves before nearby cycles.  Retaining this distinction is essential:
adding arbitrary attachments changes the source, and nearby images
do not in general recover arbitrary generic-fibre maps.

\begin{definition}[Intrinsic stalk conditions]
\label{def:intrinsic-source}
Let $\mathcal A(Y)$ be the full subcategory of the actual stable
category of $H$-module sheaves consisting of bounded constructible
modules $F$ such that
\[
 j^*F\in D^b_{\mathrm{lisse}}(U,Q),\qquad
 i_q^*F\in\Perf(A_q)\quad(q\in Z).
\]
Here lisse cohomology has finite rank, with continuous geometric
$\ell$-adic monodromy; no global unipotence or unit-generation
condition is imposed on $U$.  All attachments are arbitrary actual
$H$-module attachments.  For a locally closed union $S$ of this
stratification use $H_S=H|_S$ and the same conditions on its retained
strata.  For a finer marked smooth-point stratification, its additional
closed stalk algebra is $Q$; that is a new support profile.
\end{definition}

This definition involves no modification, essential-image criterion,
anchor, or assertion that a counit is invertible.  Its node condition
is expressed through an actual geometric stalk algebra.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:node-source} it has an independently identified
local Kummer coefficient meaning.

\begin{lemma}[Finite branch links with arbitrary horizontal monodromy]
\label{lem:finite-horizontal-links}
For $V\in D^b_{\mathrm{lisse}}(U,Q)$, the actual link
\[
 L(V)=i^*Rj_*V
\]
has components $L_q(V)\in\Perf(A_q)$.  Its two branches use the
actual algebra maps $\beta_b$, and
$L_q(V)=\bigoplus_{b\ni q}R\Gamma(\eta_b,V)$ as an $A_q$-module.
If $V$ has cohomology in $[a,b]$, the link has cohomology in
$[a,b+1]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The strict punctured neighbourhood of a node is the disjoint union
of its two punctured strict branches.  Each is the spectrum of the
fraction field of a strictly henselian discrete valuation ring;
its etale coefficients are continuous representations of its
inertia group $I_b$.  The pro-$\ell$ tame quotient is
$\Gamma_b=\mathbf Z_\ell(1)$.  The kernel $K_b$ of
$I_b\to\Gamma_b$ is an extension of wild pro-$p$ inertia by the
prime-to-$\ell$ part of tame inertia.  In residue characteristic zero the wild subgroup here is trivial.
The kernel has $\ell$-cohomological dimension zero.  On a finite-dimensional continuous $Q$-representation
its image is finite of order prime to $\ell$: a stable lattice has
an open pro-$\ell$ congruence subgroup, which has trivial intersection
with this image.  Thus its invariants are exact, with the actual
finite averaging projector.  This reduction is local on a branch;
it does not discard the horizontal local system on $U$.

For a representation $V_b$ put $W=V_b^{K_b}$.  Derived invariants
reduce to $C^*_{\mathrm{cts}}(\Gamma_b,W)$.  Choose a generator only
for this calculation.  The generalized eigenvalue-one summand $W_1$
of its operator $T$ is unipotent; on the complementary rational
primary summand $W_{\ne1}$, $T-1$ is invertible.  The continuous
cochain calculation is the two-term complex $[W\xrightarrow{T-1}W]$,
so the latter summand is zero.  The summand $W_1$ is a finite extension
of trivial representations.  Its cochains therefore lie in the thick
closure of $C^*_{\mathrm{cts}}(\Gamma_b,Q)=B_b$, without replacing
its extensions by split cohomology.  They are perfect $B_b$-modules,
and hence perfect $A_q$-modules under $\beta_b$.
This also proves the cohomological interval.  Apply finite Postnikov
triangles to a bounded lisse complex.  The argument uses the actual
invariant and cochain maps, so identifies the module structure of the
geometric link.  It does not assume the horizontal monodromy itself
is unipotent or that its entire cochain functor is faithful.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Coordinate-independent supported source and reconstruction]
\label{thm:local-supported-source}
For the fixed actual nodal model $M$, Definition~\ref{def:intrinsic-source}
is coordinate independent and gives an equivalence with the category
of homotopy-coherent triples
\[
 (V,W,\alpha),\quad V\in D^b_{\mathrm{lisse}}(U,Q),\quad
 W\in\prod_{q\in Z}\Perf(A_q),\quad
 \alpha:W\longrightarrow L(V).
\]
The equivalence retains full mapping spectra, arbitrary attachments
and actual localization boundaries.  It is compatible with strict
etale changes of chart and with the torsor comparisons above.
Moreover $\mathcal A(Y)$ is exactly the finite stable,
idempotent-complete envelope in actual $H$-module sheaves generated by
\[
 j_!V\quad(V\text{ an actual finite-rank lisse sheaf on }U),
 \qquad
 i_{q,*}R\Gamma(T_q,E)\quad(E\in\mathcal U(T_q)).
\]
The node modules in the second formula are transported by the actual
nearby comparison to $A_q$-modules.  Thus this envelope is geometrically
specified before any model-descent theorem is used.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The nearby algebra $H$, its strata, and the stalk and lisse conditions
are intrinsic.  Etale base change identifies these data.  The only
chart description at a node is its local Kummer presentation, which
is coordinate independent by Propositions~\ref{prop:coordinate-coefficients}
and \ref{prop:node-source}.  No assertion that the unit torsor is
canonically trivial is needed.

Recollement holds in the ambient stable categories of module sheaves:
$j^*,i^*$ are jointly conservative; $j_!,i_*,Rj_*$ are their actual
sheaf-module operations.  Given a triple, form
\[
 F=Rj_*V\mathop{\times}^{h}_{i_*L(V)}i_*W.
\]
Its restrictions are $V$ and $W$.  The branch lemma makes $Rj_*V$
bounded constructible with perfect node stalks.  Thus $F$ satisfies
the defining conditions.  Conversely the open unit of a given $F$
produces $\alpha$ and the canonical map to this pullback; the two
restrictions show it is an equivalence.  For two triples the mapping
spectrum is the homotopy fibre of
\[
 \operatorname{Map}_U(V,V')\oplus\operatorname{Map}_{A_Z}(W,W')
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Map}_{A_Z}(W,L(V')),
 \qquad(f,g)\longmapsto L(f)\alpha-\alpha'g.
\]
Here $A_Z$ denotes the family $(A_q)$.  In particular the homotopy in
an attaching square is retained; a diagram of object values would
not give this formula.  The same construction gives
\[
 i^!F=\operatorname{fib}\bigl(W\xrightarrow{\alpha}L(V)\bigr),
 \qquad j_!V\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow i_*W
           \xrightarrow{\delta_F}j_!V[1].
\]
The boundary corresponds to $\alpha$ under
$\operatorname{Map}(i_*W,j_!V[1])\simeq
\operatorname{Map}_{A_Z}(W,L(V))$, with the boundary convention of
this localization triangle.

Each displayed geometric generator satisfies the intrinsic conditions.
They are preserved by finite cones and retracts.  Conversely every
$V$ is obtained by finite Postnikov triangles from its lisse cohomology
sheaves; every $W_q\in\Perf(A_q)$ is the actual cochain image of an
$E\in\mathcal U(T_q)$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:node-source}.
The localization triangle therefore places every $F$ in the stated
finite envelope.  This proves both inclusions without defining that
envelope by a descent or a counit condition.

On smooth strata the geometric input is the actual lisse sheaf, not
just its global cohomology.  At finite precision its finite etale
local trivializations lift to the smooth formal neighbourhood by
formal etale lifting.  Applying smooth nearby specialization to
these lifts recovers that sheaf.  These constructions commute with
coefficient reduction; they give the usual geometric local
realizations of the horizontal generators.  This supplies local
geometric provenance, not a claim of a single global algebraic
lifting for every supported object or for every morphism between them.

Finally the reconstruction is made from actual adjunction units and
pullbacks.  Etale restriction commutes with these operations here;
Lemma~\ref{lem:overlap-mates} identifies the resulting chart and
branch comparisons and their cells.  This proves the asserted
compatibility rather than merely an abstract equivalence of categories.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Effective chart descent at a fixed model]
\label{cor:fixed-model-etale-descent}
The categories and reconstruction of
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-supported-source} satisfy effective descent
for finite jointly surjective strict-etale chart covers of $M$,
with the induced finite support profile.  This is descent of
presentations at one model, not descent along a proper modification.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Actual sheaf-module objects and maps descend in the stable category
of modules over the sheaf algebra $H$; on the chart nerve the algebra
is the pullback of $H$ by etale nearby base change.  This is sheaf
and module descent before restricting the coefficient category.
The lisse, finite-rank and constructibility conditions are etale local.
At a geometric node, a chart cover induces copies of the same strict
geometric germ, so perfectness of its $A_q$-module can be checked
on that cover.  Boundedness is uniform on a finite cover by taking
the maximum and minimum of its finitely many bounds.  Thus the
ambient descent object lies in $\mathcal A(Y)$ whenever its chart
objects do, and conversely.  The descent assertion on mapping spectra
is the ambient sheaf-module descent assertion, restricted to a full
subcategory.  The explicit reconstruction and its link functor
commute with these restrictions by Lemma~\ref{lem:overlap-mates}.
The same argument applies to a finite diagram of geometric supports.
It retains the overlap transition maps and does not replace them
by identities; in particular it does not eliminate loop holonomy.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Typed Verdier partner and a nonzero boundary]
\label{cor:local-verdier-boundary}
Let $D_Y=R\mathcal Hom_Q(-,\omega_Y)$ be actual geometric Verdier
duality, giving the contragredient $H$-action.  Define
$\mathcal A^D(Y)$ independently by the conditions
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 N\in D_c^b(Y,H\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}),\qquad
 j^*N\in D^b_{\mathrm{lisse}}(U,Q),\\
 D_Qi_q^!N\in\Perf(A_q),
 \quad D_Q=R\operatorname{Hom}_Q(-,Q).
 \end{gathered}
\]
Then $D_Y:\mathcal A(Y)^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\mathcal A^D(Y)$
is the actual geometric duality, with its evaluation, trace and
localization mates.  These statements hold on the typed support
restrictions above.  At any node the localization boundary for
$F=H$ is nonzero.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For bounded constructible sheaves on the finite-type curve,
Verdier biduality is valid and the ordinary/exceptional exchange is
\[
 i_q^!D_YF\simeq D_Q i_q^*F,\qquad
 j^*D_YF\simeq D_Uj^*F.
\]
The smooth curve duality $D_U$ preserves bounded finite-rank lisse
complexes, with its genuine dimension shift and twist.  Exact
finite-dimensional biduality at $q$ proves that the two displayed
stalk/costalk conditions are exchanged, in both directions.  Actions
are the ones obtained by transposing the actual $H$-action under
evaluation.  Thus the dual category is characterized by actual
costalks, not defined as a formal opposite.  Applying this same
geometric duality to localization gives its mate triangle.  Evaluation
and trace are the ambient geometric maps; the overlap comparisons
respect them by Lemma~\ref{lem:overlap-mates}.  No assertion that
$\omega_Y$ is a line bundle or scalar twist is used.

For $F=H$, at a node the attaching map is
$A_q\to B_{b_1}\oplus B_{b_2}$.  Each component is $\beta_b$,
an equivalence preserving the unit; in degree zero it is
$1\mapsto(1,1)$.  It is not zero.  The mapping identification in
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-supported-source} therefore proves that
$\delta_H$ is a nonzero actual module-sheaf morphism.  This test is
local and does not yet construct the global loop example.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What this source theorem does and does not identify]
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-supported-source} closes the local source
question for the explicitly specified nearby-enhanced supported
envelope.  It permits arbitrary horizontal local systems and genuine
attachments, without global unit generation or a coarse anchor.
It does not identify this envelope with a full category of all
algebraic generic-fibre coefficients; its maps are actual
nearby-module maps.  Such an identification would require a new
source theorem and can fail if generic supports have been forgotten.
It also does not identify all fine objects with pullbacks from a
coarser model.  The next section proves the essential-image theorem for actual
curve modifications; Section~\ref{sec:profiles} then proves the
anchor-free profile comparison in the specified modification component.
\end{remark}

\section{Actual curve modifications and typed supported descent}
\label{sec:curve-modification}
An allowed map in this section is an actual proper morphism
$u:M'\to M$ over the fixed trait, an isomorphism on the generic fibre
and away from finitely many nodes, whose strict-etale local models
are finite integer-cut modifications of $xy=t^m$.
This is a geometric condition on an existing morphism; existence of
such a morphism for an arbitrary proposed subdivision is not assumed.
Write $u:Y'\to Y$ also for the special-fibre map, and set
$F_u=u_H^*=H'\otimes^L_{u^*H}u^*(-)$ and $G_u=Ru_*$.
The right adjoint uses the actual algebra map $H\to Ru_*H'$.

\begin{definition}[The geometric fibre condition]
\label{def:fibre-condition}
Let $C_q=u^{-1}(q)$ be the complete fibre over a modified node.
Define $\mathcal A_u(Y')$ as the full subcategory of
$\mathcal A(Y')$ with the following additional conditions on each
$C_q$.  On every open exceptional component $\mathbf G_m$, the
cohomology local systems are unipotent.  At every incidence within
$C_q$, the individual module attachment to its punctured branch
link is invertible, using the actual branch algebra map.
There is no invertibility requirement on attachments from $C_q$
to the exterior open strata of $Y'$.  These conditions are imposed
on the restriction to the complete closed fibre, not on its costalk.
For a support $S\subseteq Y$ use $S'=u^{-1}S$ and the same conditions
on fibres contained in $S'$.
\end{definition}

The fibre is a chain, including its terminal nodes.  This definition
uses its genuine strata, unipotent monodromy and branch maps; it is
not a definition as the image of $F_u$.  No condition is imposed on
the global dual graph of $Y$ or on horizontal monodromy outside the
exceptional chains.

\begin{lemma}[Actual algebra unit and the chain coefficient category]
\label{lem:curve-unit}
The actual unit $H\to Ru_*H'$ is an equivalence of nearby algebras.
For every modified node $q$,
\[
 F_q:\Perf(A_q)\longrightarrow\mathcal C(C_q),\qquad
 W\longmapsto H_{C_q}\otimes^L_{A_q}W
\]
is an equivalence onto the category specified by the fibre conditions
in Definition~\ref{def:fibre-condition}.  Its inverse is $R\Gamma(C_q,-)$,
with the $A_q$-action given by the actual algebra unit.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Proper nearby base change identifies the unit with the map below.
At torsion level it follows from ordinary proper base change
\cite[Expose XII, Theorem 5.1]{SGA4}, applied to the proper scheme
map $u$ and the special-fibre immersion, together with composition
of generic direct images in the definition of geometric nearby
cycles. We use its constructible adic comparison and rationalization
\cite{LZEnhanced,LZAdic} for these finite-type trait models with
$\ell$ invertible. Thus the unit is
$R\Psi_MQ\to Ru_*R\Psi_{M'}u_\eta^*Q$.
The generic map is an isomorphism, so this is an equivalence.
The comparison is the mate of that generic pullback identity;
its multiplicative structure is therefore that of the actual nearby
algebra map.  Proper base change restricts it to
$A_q\simeq R\Gamma(C_q,H_{C_q})$.

For $W=A_q$, the coefficient unit
$W\to R\Gamma(C_q,F_qW)$ is just this map.  The projection map
extends the assertion to finite cones and retracts, hence to every
perfect $W$.  This proves full faithfulness on perfect modules.
The local chain reconstruction of
\cite[\texttt{thm:cell-reconstruction}]{Refinement} identifies the
independently specified unipotent, branchwise-invertible fibre
category with $\operatorname{thick}(H_{C_q})$.  Its hypotheses hold
on each actual integer-cut fibre; its argument concerns that chain,
not the global curve.  The image of the fully faithful $F_q$ is
thick and contains $H_{C_q}$, hence is exactly this category.
The torsor and branch comparisons of Section~\ref{sec:local-source}
identify the conditions under changes of these local charts.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pairwise curve descent with arbitrary exterior attachments]
\label{thm:curve-pairwise-descent}
For every actual modification just specified and every locally closed
coarse support $S$ that is a union of the strata of
Definition~\ref{def:intrinsic-source}, with its complete inverse image
$S'$, there is an
actual adjoint equivalence
\[
 F_u:\mathcal A(S)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal A_u(S'),
 \qquad G_u=Ru_{S,*}.
\]
Its unit is the actual module-adjunction unit.  It preserves all
mapping spectra, arbitrary exterior attachments and their localization
boundaries.  No global unit-generation hypothesis or forest condition
on the whole curve is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
It suffices to give the argument on $Y$; restriction to a complete
inverse-image support uses the identical proper base-change squares.
Let $U$ be the complement of the coarse nodes, $Z$ their union,
$Z'=u^{-1}Z$, and write $j,i,j',i'$ for the immersions.  Unmodified
nodes have point fibres and are included with identity transitions.
Over $U$ the map is the identity.  Put
\[
 L(V)=i^*Rj_*V,\qquad L'(V)=i'^*Rj'_*V,\qquad
 F_Z=\prod_q F_q,\qquad G_Z=Ru_{Z,*}.
\]
Proper base change and composition of right adjoints give the actual
comparison $\lambda_V:G_ZL'(V)\xrightarrow{\sim}L(V)$.
It applies to arbitrary lisse $V$ on the exterior $U$.
The link $L'(V)$ is an ambient module object; it need not satisfy
the fibre conditions and is not replaced by a cartesian object.

Define $\chi_V:F_uRj_*V\to Rj'_*V$ to be adjoint to the identity
of $V$ on $U$.  Its closed restriction is
$\kappa_V:F_ZL(V)\to L'(V)$.
The adjoint of $\kappa_V$ under the ambient adjunction
$F_Z\dashv G_Z$ is $\lambda_V^{-1}$: indeed the composite
\[
 Rj_*V\longrightarrow G_uF_uRj_*V
       \xrightarrow{G_u\chi_V}G_uRj'_*V\simeq Rj_*V
\]
restricts to the identity on $U$.  Full faithfulness of $Rj_*$
identifies it with the identity, with its adjunction homotopy.
Proper base change carries this unit identity to the closed square.
Thus, with $\epsilon_Z$ the actual ambient counit,
\[
 \kappa_V=\epsilon_{Z,L'(V)}F_Z(\lambda_V^{-1}).
\]
The actual pullback of a coarse triple $(V,W,\alpha)$ therefore has
triple
\[
 (V,F_ZW,\ \epsilon_{Z,L'(V)}F_Z(\lambda_V^{-1}\alpha)).
\]
This formula follows from the open unit defining the attaching map;
it is not a freely chosen gluing equivalence.

Lemma~\ref{lem:curve-unit} identifies the possible closed objects
with the fibre category.  The ambient adjunction identifies the
attaching mapping spectra by
\[
 \operatorname{Map}(F_ZW,L'(V))
  \simeq\operatorname{Map}(W,G_ZL'(V))
  \simeq\operatorname{Map}(W,L(V)).
\]
These identifications preserve the difference map in the mapping-fibre
formula of Theorem~\ref{thm:local-supported-source}; hence they
identify the full mapping spectra of triples, including their
extension homotopies.  They also give an inverse on all fine triples
satisfying the geometric fibre condition.  This proves the asserted
equivalence with actual $F_u$ and $G_u$.

Alternatively its unit is checked on the jointly conservative coarse
strata: the identity over $U$ and the perfect-module unit of
Lemma~\ref{lem:curve-unit} at each $q$.  Bounded constructibility of
the selected pullback and pushforward follows from the finite chain
calculation and the same recollement: all exterior objects and links
are bounded constructible, with finitely many fibres.  This argument
does not require a global generator and leaves the entire exterior
local system, including its monodromy, unchanged.
Finally $F_uj_!V\simeq j'_!V$ and $F_ui_*W\simeq i'_*F_ZW$.
The mapping comparison identifies the actual connecting morphism
between these objects.  It therefore compares localization triangles,
not only their three object values.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The operation diagram has two coefficient types]
\label{prop:typed-modification}
Let $\mathcal A_u^D(S')$ be the bounded constructible modules $N$
whose actual $D_{S'}N$ satisfies the intrinsic fibre conditions above.
Equivalently the fibre test is applied to $D_{C_q}i_{C_q}^!N$;
this uses actual costalks.  The supported transition
\[
 F_u^D=D_{S'}F_u^{\mathrm{op}}D_S
 :\mathcal A^D(S)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal A_u^D(S')
\]
has inverse actual proper direct image.  Ordinary pullback compares
support restrictions, extension by zero and closed pushforward on
the ordinary types.  The dual transition compares their Verdier
mates on the supported types.  The pairings and trace comparisons
are the actual geometric ones.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The costalk characterization follows from
$i_{C_q}^*D_{S'}N\simeq D_{C_q}i_{C_q}^!N$.
Proper Verdier duality gives $D_SG_u\simeq G_uD_{S'}$,
with the action through the same nearby-algebra map.  Conjugating
the proved adjunction equivalence therefore gives the stated
supported equivalence and its inverse, identified by this actual
proper-duality map.

Restriction and extension-by-zero squares for $F_u$ are actual
module pullback squares on complete inverse-image supports.
The closed-pushforward square is the corresponding closed support
base-change square.  Applying actual Verdier duality gives the
$Rj_*$ and exceptional-restriction squares with transition $F_u^D$,
with all types changed accordingly.  Naturality of evaluation and
proper trace identifies their pairing squares; the identities of
adjunction and biduality identify the localization mates.
No identification $F_u=F_u^D$ in the fine ambient category is asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Ordinary pullback does not preserve the raw right open image]
\label{ex:right-open-obstruction}
Take the thickness-two node and its one-cut modification, with
exceptional fibre $C$, and let $j:U=Y\setminus\{q\}\hookrightarrow Y$.
For $V=Q_U$, its coarse link is $A_q\oplus A_q$.  Thus on the open
exceptional component
\[
 (F_uRj_*Q_U)|_{C^\circ}=Q_{C^\circ}^{\oplus2}.
\]
But $Rj'_*Q_U$ restricts to zero on $C^\circ$: this open subset of
$Y'$ is disjoint from $U$.  Hence the actual comparison
$F_uRj_*Q_U\to Rj'_*Q_U$ is not an equivalence.  The latter link
must remain in the ambient category in the preceding proof.
The supported-type comparison of Proposition~\ref{prop:typed-modification}
is unaffected.  An untyped assertion that ordinary pullback
intertwines every raw six-operation term is false.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Coherence for strings of actual modifications]
\label{prop:actual-string-coherence}
For a composable string of allowed actual modifications, the
comparisons above have the composition cells of the actual module
pullback/direct-image adjunctions, with their support and Verdier
mates.  The image for a composite is the iterated image; it is also
characterized directly by the fibre condition for the composite.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The algebra maps $u^*H\to H'$ come from the same generic pullback
identities and nearby adjunctions.  Thus on strings they form the
actual coherent diagram of algebra maps.  Extension of scalars
and derived tensor associativity give $F_{uv}\simeq F_vF_u$,
with the associativity cells of a single iterated tensor product.
For the external link comparison both
$\chi_{uv}$ and $\chi_vF_v\chi_u$ are adjoint to the same open
identity.  Their specified adjunction identification gives its
composition homotopy and, on longer strings, its higher cells.
The same applies to $\lambda$ by right-adjoint composition.
The mapping-fibre proof consequently transports each attachment
with these cells.  Applying the pairwise theorem to the composite
identifies its direct geometric fibre condition with the iterated
image.  Verdier conjugation supplies the supported cells.
This proves coherence for actual strings without transporting a
preselected anchor's entire operation diagram.
\end{proof}

\section{Geometric profiles and anchor-free descent}
\label{sec:profiles}
We now fix a connected component $\mathscr M$ of the category of
actual nodal models and integer-cut modifications considered above.
All models have the specified identification of their generic curve,
and all morphisms induce its identity.  Connected means connected by
a finite zigzag of these actual maps.  It does not mean that all models
of a given generic curve are admitted.  We order by refinement, so
an arrow in the indexing category $M\to M'$ denotes a geometric map
$M'\to M$.

\begin{lemma}[A canonical finest model in the integer-cut component]
\label{lem:finest-model}
The component $\mathscr M$ has a terminal refinement $\widetilde M$,
unique up to the unique isomorphism inducing the generic identity.
Its node thicknesses are all one.  It can be constructed by repeatedly
blowing up the reduced nonregular locus of the total space of any
model in $\mathscr M$.  Every actual integer-cut modification has
the same terminal refinement.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At a node $xy=t^m$ the total space is regular exactly when $m=1$.
For $m\geq2$ blow up the ideal $(x,y,t)$.  On the $x$-chart put
$t=xw$, $y=xv$.  The strict-transform equation is
$v=x^{m-2}w^m$, so this chart is $\operatorname{Spec}R[x,w]/(xw-t)$
with $v$ eliminated.  The $y$-chart is identical.  On the $t$-chart
put $x=tu$, $y=tv$; its equation is $uv=t^{m-2}$.
For $m=2$ this last chart is $uv=1$; for $m=3$ it has thickness one.
These equations follow in the blow-up charts by removing the torsion
in the Rees presentation; the original node is integral, so they
are the charts of the blow-up itself.  Thus for $m>2$ the outer cuts
are $1,m-1$ and the remaining middle thickness is $m-2$; for $m=2$
there is the single cut $1$.  Iteration gives exactly all integer cuts
$1,\ldots,m-1$ and terminates.

The nonregular locus is a finite reduced closed subscheme, and its
blow-up is an actual globally defined projective modification.
The chart computation is etale local and is unchanged by multiplication
of coordinates by units or by interchange of branches.  It therefore
constructs the full resolution globally, without gluing hypothetical
modifications or invoking a contraction theorem.

For a partial cut set, resolving each remaining gap inserts precisely
the missing integer cuts.  The full-cut comparison in
\cite[\texttt{prop:cut-models}]{Refinement} hence identifies its
resolution with that of the original node, compatibly with the map
to the original model.  These local identifications glue: two maps
between the separated models agreeing on the dense generic fibre
agree everywhere.  The models here are reduced and flat, and the
generic fibre is schematically dense.  The same uniqueness removes
any dependence on a zigzag connecting models in $\mathscr M$.
Consequently the resolution of any one member resolves every member
and gives its unique refinement arrow to the resulting
$\widetilde M$.  Resolving again does nothing, so this is terminal.
\end{proof}

Write $\widetilde Y=\widetilde M_s$ and let $\widetilde G$ be its
finite dual graph, retaining loop edges and the genera of normalized
components.  Each component defines its normalized divisorial
valuation on the function field of the fixed generic curve.
These valuations identify components across all presentations.

\begin{definition}[A marked geometric constructibility profile]
\label{def:marked-profile}
A profile $\mathfrak p$ in $\mathscr M$ consists of the following
geometric data, chosen before any coefficient category.
Choose a subset $P$ of these divisorial valuations.  Every unmarked
component of $\widetilde Y$ must have normalization $\mathbf P^1_k$
and exactly two incident branches.  Require each connected component
of the subgraph induced by unmarked vertices to be a path, not a cycle.
In particular an unmarked isolated self-loop is excluded.
All vertices of positive genus or of valence different from two
must therefore be marked.

The profile pieces on $\widetilde Y$ are the smooth open strata
$U_v$ of marked components, and the closed blocks $C_e$ consisting
of a maximal unmarked path together with all its incident nodes.
A node directly between marked components is itself a block.
A path may return to the same marked component; it still has two
terminal branches and is allowed.  These pieces partition
$\widetilde Y$.

Choose finitely many closed subsets that are unions of whole profile
pieces, include $\varnothing$ and $\widetilde Y$, and take their finite
closure under unions and intersections. The support diagram contains
the differences of these closed subsets, with the induced locally
closed inclusions and open/closed decompositions. This is still a
finite diagram: the finitely many profile pieces give only finitely
many subsets. In particular the diagram contains support
intersections used in kernel composition. A presentation is a model
$M\in\mathscr M$ in which every marked valuation remains a component.
\end{definition}

This definition uses a canonically constructed fine model, not a
chosen coarse model.  Equivalently its markings are specified
geometric valuations and residual branches.  Their coefficients
and descent properties are not used to choose them.  Every profile
has a presentation, namely $\widetilde M$ itself.  We do not assume
that contracting all its unmarked paths produces another model.

For a presentation let $r_M:\widetilde M\to M$ be the canonical
map.  Its exceptional fibres contain no marked component and lie
inside single blocks.  Let $U_{v,M}$ and $C_{e,M}$ be the images of
the profile pieces.  The former are identified with $U_v$, and the
latter are closed chains, possibly just a node.  The full inverse
image of $C_{e,M}$ is $C_e$.  The specified supports are likewise
complete inverse images: the marked closed subsets are saturated
for $r_M$, so properness takes them to closed subsets and their
differences to the required locally closed supports.  This proves
existence of all these geometric support presentations.

\begin{definition}[Profile coefficients]
\label{def:profile-coefficients}
Let $\mathcal A_{\mathfrak p}(M,S)$ be the full subcategory of
$\mathcal A(S_M)$ whose restriction to every block $C_{e,M}$ retained
in the support has unipotent cohomology on its open rational
components and invertible individual internal branch attachments.
At a block consisting of a point this is the existing perfect-stalk
condition.  The marked open strata retain arbitrary horizontal
lisse coefficients, and attachments between a block and marked
open strata are arbitrary.
Define the supported type by actual Verdier costalk conditions:
$N$ belongs to $\mathcal A_{\mathfrak p}^D(M,S)$ when its actual
$D_{S_M}N$ satisfies these geometric ordinary-type conditions.
On a closed block the test is on $D_{C_{e,M}}i_{e}^!N$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Support operations preserve the geometric profile]
\label{lem:profile-support-closure}
Let $e:S\hookrightarrow T$ be an inclusion in the finite support
diagram. The actual operations $e^*$ and $e_!$ preserve the ordinary
profile categories. In particular closed pushforward does so, and
an open/closed decomposition gives its actual localization triangle
inside these categories. Their Verdier mates $e^!$ and $Re_*$
preserve the supported profile categories.
These operations commute with the corresponding typed modification
comparisons on complete inverse-image supports.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every support is a union of whole profile pieces. Thus its
intersection with a marked open stratum is either that entire
stratum or empty; its intersection with a closed block is either
that entire block or empty. Restriction preserves the conditions
on each retained piece. For extension by zero, the ordinary
restriction to a piece outside $S$ is zero, by base change for an
immersion; on a piece in $S$ it is the original restriction.
Consequently an unmarked block has either its original unipotent,
branchwise-invertible coefficient or the zero coefficient, and
marked open strata have lisse restrictions. Node stalks remain
perfect. Attachments at the boundary of $S$ are exterior
attachments between pieces, on which the definition imposes no
invertibility requirement. No internal branch of a block is cut.
The underlying operations are bounded constructible on this finite
stratification. This proves preservation, including zero blocks.

For a closed $i$ and complementary open $j$, the actual triangle
$j_!j^*F\to F\to i_*i^*F$ has all its terms in the ordinary profile
category by the preceding argument; its connecting map is unchanged.
Actual geometric Verdier exchange gives the supported operations
and the mate triangle. Finally Proposition~\ref{prop:typed-modification}
compares these ambient operations by actual maps on full inverse-image
supports. The preservation just proved allows those same maps to
restrict to the profile categories. This last step cannot be replaced
by preservation of the larger ambient category alone.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The same profile selects the same fine objects]
\label{lem:profile-images}
For every presentation $M$, actual pullback to $\widetilde M$
induces equivalences
\[
 F_{r_M}:\mathcal A_{\mathfrak p}(M,S)
       \xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal A_{\mathfrak p}(\widetilde M,S),
\]
and similarly for the supported types using $F_{r_M}^D$.
These are statements about independently specified geometric
conditions on both sides.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each block is a chain with two terminal branches, not a circular
fibre.  Its unipotent, branchwise-invertible module category is
$\operatorname{thick}(H_{C_{e,M}})$.  To see that the chain result
used earlier applies without assuming a contracted coarse model,
use its actual recollement presentation: on each open rational
component, continuous Kummer cochains identify the unipotent category
with perfect modules over its unit cochains; each puncture restriction
is the primitive Kummer equivalence.  At a node use
Proposition~\ref{prop:node-source}.  Requiring each branch map to be
invertible therefore joins these perfect-module categories by
equivalences along a finite incidence tree.  Successively removing
terminal vertices identifies the resulting category with any one
of these perfect-module categories.  The actual unit restricts to
the unit at every vertex, hence generates the result.  This is an
argument on a single closed block, never on the possibly cyclic
global graph.  Actual torsor twists are retained in its equivalences.

The pairwise theorem applies to $r_M$.  On a block its pullback is
fully faithful and carries $H_{C_{e,M}}$ to $H_{C_e}$ by actual
extension of scalars.  Thus it identifies their two thick unit
categories.  Every fine profile object satisfies the fibre condition
for $r_M$, because each fibre is a subchain of one block.
The pairwise theorem first descends this object to $M$; the just
proved block comparison shows that its descended restriction has
the required block condition.  Conversely a coarse profile object
pulls back to the fine block category.  Outside the blocks the map
is the identity.  The external-attachment mapping equivalence of
Theorem~\ref{thm:curve-pairwise-descent} retains all maps joining
these blocks to the marked open strata.  This proves both image
inclusions and full faithfulness, not just equality of object
restrictions.  Actual proper duality proves the supported assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A: anchor-free typed supported Morita descent]
\label{thm:profile-descent}
Fix an integer-cut component $\mathscr M$ and a marked geometric
profile $\mathfrak p$ as above.  The systems
\[
 \bigl\{\mathcal A_{\mathfrak p}(M,S),
          \mathcal A_{\mathfrak p}^D(M,S)\bigr\}_S
\]
for all presentations admit canonical coherent comparisons independent
of a coarse anchor and of the choice of common refinement.  The
comparisons retain full mapping spectra, actual localization
boundaries, arbitrary horizontal and loop monodromy, and the typed
geometric Verdier pairings and trace maps.  The ordinary support
operations and their supported Verdier mates are those of
Proposition~\ref{prop:typed-modification}; no untyped six-operation
claim is made.  These comparisons in particular give a multi-object
Morita-invariant supported system, without a global unit generator.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The terminal refinement of Lemma~\ref{lem:finest-model} is a
presentation of every profile.  It is therefore a common refinement
of every finite family of presentations; the same holds for any
finite family of common refinements.  Lemma~\ref{lem:profile-images}
proves, by the geometric block condition, that the images of all
presentations in it are exactly the same category.  This is the
essential step that equality of support labels alone did not prove.

For presentations $M,N$ put $F_M=F_{r_M}$ and $G_M=Rr_{M,*}$
on the indicated ordinary types.  The comparison is
$T_{N\leftarrow M}=G_NF_M$.  Its composite with
$T_{L\leftarrow N}$ is compared to $T_{L\leftarrow M}$ by the actual
counit $F_NG_N\to\mathrm{id}$ on
$\mathcal A_{\mathfrak p}(\widetilde M,S)$.
The counit is invertible on that geometrically identified category.
The units and triangle identities give the identity comparisons;
composition of these same adjunctions on strings gives associativity
and all higher cells, as in Proposition~\ref{prop:actual-string-coherence}.
Thus the result is not obtained by choosing unrelated equivalences.

If another actual common refinement $L$ is used, it refines further
to $\widetilde M$.  The string comparisons and the same counit identify
the comparison through $L$ with the displayed one.  For any finite
diagram of such choices these identifications are the restrictions
of the single coherent adjunction diagram through the terminal
refinement.  This proves independence of common refinement with
its higher comparisons.  The uniqueness of the generic-identity
isomorphism of terminal refinements makes the construction independent
of the model used to construct $\widetilde M$.

For each specified support, $r_M$ uses its complete inverse image.
Lemma~\ref{lem:profile-support-closure} proves preservation of the
profile categories by the support operations themselves.
Actual support squares and their localization connecting maps were
compared in the pairwise theorem.  Applying the argument simultaneously
to the finite support diagram gives its ordinary operation comparisons.
Actual Verdier conjugation and proper trace give the supported
comparisons and mixed pairing cells, with their types retained.
An equivalence of these small idempotent-complete stable categories
is a Morita equivalence in the multi-object sense.  Nothing replaces
a marked stratum's full coefficient category by its cohomology algebra,
and no step removes the gluing around a cycle.  This proves all the
assertions in the stated domain.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The precise model-independence obtained]
Theorem~\ref{thm:profile-descent} settles the coefficient-and-support
presentation problem for these geometric profiles inside a connected
component of actual integer-cut modifications.  It neither claims
that all models of one generic curve belong to that component nor
allows arbitrary rational cuts over a fixed trait.  The canonical
fine model is available because this restricted modification class
has a finest refinement.  In a larger model category that fact
requires a new theorem and may fail.  Changing the marked valuation
set changes the constructibility profile; new fine objects are not
silently admitted into an old profile.  The source remains the
actual nearby-enhanced supported envelope, not all generic sheaves
with all their original maps.
\end{remark}

\section{Geometric kernels with horizontal coefficients}
\label{sec:profile-kernels}
Fix the component and profile of Theorem~\ref{thm:profile-descent}.
Close its finite support list under the intersections used below;
these are again differences of closed saturated unions.  Products
of supports are over $k$.  Write $\mathcal C_a=\mathcal
A_{\mathfrak p}(M,S)$ for $a=(M,S)$, with actual algebra $H_a$.
The public enhanced six-operation and adic formalism is that of
\cite{LZEnhanced,LZAdic}, used here on finite-type scheme supports
with $\ell$ invertible and geometric rational coefficients.
The ambient extension
$\mathscr D(S)=\operatorname{Ind}(D_c^b(S,\Qell))$, its module
operations and its comparison with actual bounded module objects
are the constructions of \cite{Kernels},
\path{lem:bounded-ambient} and \path{prop:ambient-kernel-types}.
Neither input asserts boundedness of arbitrary relative tensors
over a nearby algebra. Profile preservation and boundedness of the
specific convolution integrands are proved below; all generated
expressions are proved bounded before Verdier duality is applied.

\begin{definition}[An independently specified geometric kernel source]
\label{def:profile-kernel-source}
For $a=(M,S)$ and $b=(N,T)$ with $N$ refining $M$, kernels are actual
$(p_S^*H_a,p_T^*H_b)$-bimodule sheaves on $S_M\times_kT_N$.
Our generators are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the diagonal $\Delta_*H_a$;
\item graphs and transposed graphs of the specified support
inclusions within one model, with their regular coefficients, and
the actual diagonal localization triangle and its arrows;
\item for each actual refinement $u:N\to M$, the transposed
proper graph $U_u$ with coefficient $H_N$, acting by $F_u$;
\item within each model, every rectangle $C\boxtimes_Q E$, where
$C\in\mathcal C_a$, $E\in\mathcal C_b$ are independently defined
profile coefficients on the two supports.
\end{enumerate}
Take finite composable convolutions, finite cones, shifts and retracts,
and retain all actual ambient kernel mapping spectra between the
resulting objects.  There is no restriction by equality of actions,
preservation of coefficients or invertibility of a counit.
Before localization, model changes in a word are all refinements.
Reverse proper graphs will be used for comparison, not declared to
be generators or inverse kernels in this ambient source.
In particular no boundedness of the bare projection kernel
$U_u\star V_u$ is assumed.  The composites used below are evaluated
with their specified coefficients and proved bounded by the controlling
formulas; only those bounded composites are subjected to Verdier duality.
\end{definition}

The action and convolution are the geometric operations
\[
 \Phi_P(M)=Rp_{T,!}(p_S^*M\otimes^L_{p_S^*H_a}P),\qquad
 L\star P=Rp_{13,!}
   (p_{12}^*P\otimes^L_{p_2^*H_b}p_{23}^*L).
\]
The regular diagonal is their unit.  Tensor products and maps here
are derived module operations, not tensors of cohomology groups.

\begin{lemma}[Coefficient tensors and the required projection map]
\label{lem:profile-tensor-projection}
For $C,D\in\mathcal C_a$, the complex $C\otimes^L_{H_a}D$ is
bounded constructible and belongs to $\mathcal C_a$.  Its compactly
supported cohomology is perfect over $Q$.
For an actual refinement $u:N\to M$, profile coefficients $C$ on $M$
and $D$ on $N$ satisfy the actual projection equivalence
\[
 C\otimes^L_{H_M}G_uD\xrightarrow{\sim}
 G_u(F_uC\otimes^L_{H_N}D).
\]
These statements also hold on the specified complete support restrictions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On a smooth stratum $H=Q$, and the complexes are lisse of bounded
finite rank.  At a node both stalks are perfect over $A_q$.
Their relative tensor is therefore bounded with finite-dimensional
cohomology.  There are finitely many strata and a uniform bound
on each lisse stratum, so this is a bounded constructible tensor
on the whole support.  On an unmarked block, the actual chain
comparison with perfect modules is monoidal: restriction, regular
module tensor, and the branch equivalences are the actual tensor
maps.  Thus the block condition is preserved.  This proves membership
in the profile category.  Finiteness of compactly supported cohomology
for a finite-type curve and bounded constructible rational coefficients
then gives a perfect $Q$-complex.

Check the displayed projection map on geometric stalks using proper
base change. Off exceptional fibres it is the identity. At a
contracted node put $E_q=u^{-1}(q)$ and $C_q=i_q^*C$. The map is
\[
 C_q\otimes^L_{A_q}R\Gamma(E_q,D|_{E_q})
 \longrightarrow R\Gamma(E_q,
             H_{E_q}\otimes^L_{A_q}C_q
                    \otimes^L_{H_{E_q}}D|_{E_q}).
\]
The coefficient stalk $C_q$ is perfect over $A_q$.  The map is an equivalence on $A_q$, then on
finite cones and retracts.  This proves it on all stalks, with the
actual projection map and its coefficient actions.  We have not
assumed that an arbitrarily attached sheaf is globally tensor-dualizable.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Control of generated kernels and their actions]
\label{prop:profile-kernel-control}
Every generated kernel is bounded constructible; its action preserves
the profile category and has bounded constructible integrand.
For every composable pair of generated kernels, the relative tensor
integrand defining their convolution is also bounded constructible.
Geometric convolution is defined on this source with its usual
associativity and unit cells.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Here is a controlling family of actual kernels.  For $M\preceq N$
and a support $W\subseteq S\cap T$, let
\[
 G_{M,N}^{S,T}(W)=\gamma_{W,!}H_{W_N},\qquad
 \gamma_W:W_N\longrightarrow S_M\times T_N,
 \quad w\longmapsto(u(w),w).
\]
Also use rectangles $R_{M,N}(C,E)=C\boxtimes_Q E$ with
$C\in\mathcal C_{(M,S)}$, $E\in\mathcal C_{(N,T)}$.
This controlling family may include objects beyond the initial
rectangle list; it is specified geometrically, not by their actions.
All generators lie in its finite stable envelope. The support
intersections are in the finite diagram by construction, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:profile-support-closure} supplies the required
preservation by support restriction and extension by zero.

We first check relative tensor integrands, before applying direct
image. Pull the two kernels to the threefold product. For two
graphs their supports meet along the composite graph, and the
regular intermediate algebra cancels in the relative tensor.
The resulting coefficient is the final regular algebra, hence
bounded. For a graph followed by a rectangle the same cancellation
leaves the restriction of its first coefficient to the intermediate
graph and the external second coefficient. For a rectangle followed
by a graph it leaves the external first coefficient and the
extension-of-scalars pullback of the middle coefficient; this
pullback is a bounded selected coefficient by Theorem A.
For two rectangles the middle factor is
$E\otimes^L_{H_{T_N}}C'$, bounded constructible by
Lemma~\ref{lem:profile-tensor-projection}. All remaining factors
are external bounded constructible $Q$-complexes. Extensions by
zero along the graph support immersions preserve this boundedness.
These identifications are the actual support tensor maps: on stalks,
factors outside the intersecting supports are zero, and on the
intersection they are precisely the regular cancellations just
described. Stalks detect equivalences and commute with relative
tensor. Each coefficient family has finitely many strata, so the
bounds are uniform for each such integrand. This does not assert
a common bound for all objects of the category or finite Tor-amplitude
for every ambient bimodule.

Graph--graph convolution is the graph on the support intersection,
with the composite actual refinement.  Regular factors cancel in
the relative tensor.  This is the actual support base-change map;
the support tensor identity can be checked on scalar stalks, as
in \cite[\texttt{lem:support-intersection}]{Kernels}.
For the other three cases the formulas, with well-typed supports,
are as follows:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_{N,L}(C,E)\star G_{M,N}^{S,T}(W)
  &\simeq R_{M,L}
       (e_{W_M,S_M,!}G_{u_W}(C|_{W_N}),E),\\
 G_{N,L}^{T,V}(Z)\star R_{M,N}(C,E)
  &\simeq R_{M,L}
       (C,e_{Z_L,V_L,!}F_{u_Z}(E|_{Z_N})),\\
 R_{N,L}(C',E')\star R_{M,N}(C,E)
  &\simeq R\Gamma_c(T_N,E\otimes^L_{H_{T_N}}C')
                         \otimes_Q R_{M,L}(C,E').
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first is proper graph base change followed by direct image of
the first coefficient; the second is graph pullback and regular
cancellation on the target coefficient.  The third is compact-support
base change and projection along the middle factor.  Thus these are
kernel equivalences with both outer actions, not conclusions inferred
from actions.  Lemma~\ref{lem:profile-tensor-projection} and Theorem A
show that every coefficient on their right sides remains in the
profile category and that the scalar complex in the last line is
perfect.  Support restriction and extension by zero are the proved
ordinary typed operations.  The same scalar projection maps, or their
relative bar resolutions, show naturality with both module actions.

The graph action is
$e_{W_N,T_N,!}F_{u_W}e_{W_M,S_M}^*$, and a rectangle acts by
\[
 \Phi_{R_{M,N}(C,E)}(M_0)
   =R\Gamma_c(S_M,M_0\otimes^L_{H_{S_M}}C)\otimes_Q E.
\]
These preserve the profile source by the same lemma.  Their integrands
are respectively the actual graph pushforward of a bounded selected
pullback and the external product
$(M_0\otimes_{H_{S_M}}^LC)\boxtimes_Q E$, hence bounded constructible.
The three convolution formulas and the graph formula put every
finite word in the finite stable envelope of the controlling family.
For two fixed objects in this envelope the integrand is obtained by
finite cones and retracts from the pairwise integrands checked above:
relative tensor is exact in each variable. Thus boundedness holds
before direct image for all generated pairs, not merely for the
values of their convolution or their actions. Scalar finite-type
shriek direct image then has bounded constructible values here.
The same exactness extends the action and all comparisons to finite
cones and retracts, retaining all actual ambient maps.
Fubini and tensor associativity are the actual maps before restriction
to this source, so supply its coherent composition.  No boundedness
assertion on arbitrary ambient bimodules is needed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Actual kernel descent for one model change}
For one actual modification $u:M'\to M$, use its restrictions on
both endpoint supports.  Write $U_a,U_b$ for the pullback graphs and
$V_a,V_b$ for their reverse proper graphs, acting by $G_u$.
For a generated kernel $P$ within $M'$ define
\[
 T_u(P)=V_b\star P\star U_a
       \simeq R(u_{S}\times u_T)_*P.
\]
The algebra actions on the last expression are restricted through
the actual nearby algebra maps.  This is simultaneous coarsening
of a kernel on one model; arbitrary independent contractions of
its two endpoints are not part of this assertion.

\paragraph{Relation to the anchored kernel theorem.}
The one-sided counit mechanism below, and the insertion of that
counit to obtain strong convolution, already appear in
\cite{Kernels}, \path{thm:one-sided-descent}. That result treats a
fixed local anchored four-model system and its restricted
coefficient rectangles. Here the source is selected by geometric
profiles, with arbitrary horizontal lisse coefficients on the
marked strata. The preceding bounded-link, relative-tensor and
convolution-integrand arguments verify the enlarged domain; the
proof below verifies the actual counit on its generators.
The further increments are independent endpoint changes on the
rectangular source (Theorem~\ref{thm:independent-curve-descent})
and finite etale spans in Cartesian presentations
(Theorem~\ref{thm:etale-span-descent}), with a separate span-counit
calculation. Neither follows merely by citing the anchored theorem,
and neither asserts descent for arbitrary coupled kernels.

\begin{theorem}[B: one-sided kernel descent and strong convolution]
\label{thm:profile-kernel-descent}
For the geometric generated source within $M'$, $T_u$ takes values
in the source within $M$.  The actual target counit induces an
equivalence of kernels
\[
 \alpha_P:U_b\star T_u(P)
       =U_b\star V_b\star P\star U_a
       \xrightarrow{\epsilon_b\star1}P\star U_a.
\]
Consequently $T_u$ has invertible unit and composition maps
\[
 \Delta_*H_{S_M}\xrightarrow{\sim}T_u(\Delta_*H_{S_{M'}}),
 \qquad T_u(L)\star T_u(P)\xrightarrow{\sim}T_u(L\star P).
\]
These statements are natural on full actual kernel mapping spectra
and compatible with localization and strings of actual modifications.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the controlling graph $G_{M',M'}(W)$, proper product direct
image is $G_{M,M}(W)$: its coefficient comparison is exactly
$H_{W_M}\xrightarrow{\sim}Ru_{W,*}H_{W_{M'}}$.
For a rectangle it is
\[
 T_u(C'\boxtimes E')\simeq G_uC'\boxtimes G_uE'
\]
by proper product base change and the scalar projection formula.
Theorem A puts both resulting coefficients in their coarse profile
categories.  These comparisons imply preservation of the generators.
Preservation of their convolutions will also follow from the counit
calculation below, so is not being assumed at this step.

For a graph, both sides of $\alpha_P$ identify with the same graph
on $W_{M'}$ over $S_M\times T_{M'}$.  On that graph $\alpha_P$ is
the coefficient counit $F_{u_W}G_{u_W}H_{W_{M'}}\to H_{W_{M'}}$.
The actual algebra unit and the adjunction triangle make this the
inverse pulled-back unit, hence an equivalence.

For a rectangle $P=C'\boxtimes E'$, the actual graph projection
and base-change comparisons give the vertical equivalences in
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
 U_b\star T_u(P) & \xrightarrow{\alpha_P} & P\star U_a\\
 {\scriptstyle\sim}\big\downarrow &&
 {\scriptstyle\sim}\big\downarrow\\
 G_uC'\boxtimes F_uG_uE' &
 \xrightarrow{\;1\boxtimes\epsilon_{E'}\;} & G_uC'\boxtimes E'.
\end{array}
\]
The square commutes by naturality of these projection and base-change
comparisons with respect to the target adjunction counit, using the
same enhanced adjunction and convolution associators as in the
definition of $\alpha_P$.
The right vertical comparison is proper graph projection on the source
factor, using the coefficient projection map of
Lemma~\ref{lem:profile-tensor-projection}; it is a kernel calculation
on the product, not only a calculation of its global action.
Under these specified comparisons the map $\alpha_P$ is
$1\boxtimes\epsilon_{E'}$.  Theorem A identifies $E'$ with the
pullback of its actual proper direct image, by the independently
proved block condition, so this counit is invertible.  This proves
both the map and its invertibility on the controlling family.

Every finite convolution in the one-model source is in its finite
stable controlling envelope by Proposition~\ref{prop:profile-kernel-control}.
The two sides of $\alpha$ are exact functors on actual kernel
categories and the comparison is their natural counit transformation.
Its equivalence locus is therefore closed under finite cones and
retracts; the calculation proves it for every generated kernel.
Insert this same middle counit to obtain
\[
 V_c\star L\star U_b\star V_b\star P\star U_a
 \longrightarrow V_c\star L\star P\star U_a.
\]
It is an equivalence by $\alpha_P$ and is precisely the strong
composition comparison for $T_u$.  This now proves convolution
preservation in the generated source.  The unit comparison is the
actual algebra unit on the diagonal, already calculated above.
The triangle identities and composition of the same counits give
its unital and associative cells, including higher cells.  Proper
pushforward and its support base-change mates identify these maps
under composed modifications.  Since all are natural transformations
on actual kernel categories, this includes their maps and
localization connecting morphisms, not merely their isomorphism classes.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Morita realization and actual Verdier mates}
\begin{theorem}[Model-independent realization of the generated source]
\label{thm:kernel-morita-verdier}
The global source of Definition~\ref{def:profile-kernel-source} has
an enhanced multi-object Morita realization preserving units and
convolution, with actual geometric Verdier mates.  It sends each
refinement graph to an equivalence and therefore factors through
localization at these graphs.  After the canonical identifications
of Theorem A, its coefficient target and its simultaneous-coarsening
comparisons are independent of model presentation and of common
refinement.  No equivalence or full faithfulness of the whole kernel
categories is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a kernel $P:a\to b$ its Morita bimodule, without choosing any
single generator, is the enriched Yoneda module
\[
 \mathbb M_P(X,Y)=\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal C_b}(Y,\Phi_P(X)),
 \qquad X\in\mathcal C_a,\ Y\in\mathcal C_b.
\]
Each target module is representable and hence perfect in the
multi-object Morita sense.  This follows from the proved preservation
of $\mathcal C_b$, not from a stipulated perfectness condition on
kernels.  The actual geometric action on all kernel mapping spectra
makes this an enhanced functor.  Enriched Yoneda evaluation and
geometric Fubini give
$\mathbb M_P\otimes_{\mathcal C_b}\mathbb M_L\simeq
\mathbb M_{L\star P}$.  The diagonal acts by the identity, so its
module is the regular identity bimodule.  These are the evaluation
maps themselves and have their associative and unit cells.

The same construction applies to the mixed-model generated words:
the graph and rectangle control formulas in
Proposition~\ref{prop:profile-kernel-control} were stated for
$M\preceq N\preceq L$, not only for one model.  Thus all their
actual kernel maps are retained before localization.  A refinement
graph acts by $F_u$, an equivalence on the profile categories by
Theorem A.  The universal localization at these specified arrows
therefore admits the Morita functor.  This is localization at actual
refinement arrows, not a quotient of kernels by equality of actions.
To view the target on the canonical finest model, replace the action
of $P:(M,S)\to(N,T)$ by $F_{r_N}\Phi_PG_{r_M}$.
The actual adjunction units and counits identify compositions and
send each refinement graph to the identity equivalence.  Theorem A
supplies independence of common refinement.  The specified natural
transformation induced by $\alpha_P$ in Theorem B identifies the
one-model coarsening squares at the level of actual kernels first,
and hence at this Morita realization; it is not deduced backwards
from an equivalence of actions.

For completeness the supported action is an actual geometric formula.
Let $D$ denote geometric Verdier duality and label the corresponding
supported kernel by $P^D=D_{S_M\times T_N}P$, retaining its
contragredient actions. It is left $H_a^{\mathrm{op}}$ and right
$H_b^{\mathrm{op}}$, in the symmetry convention fixed above; the
geometric endpoints have not been exchanged. For a supported
coefficient $N_0$, view $p_S^!N_0$ as a left $p_S^*H_a$-module
using the actual map
$p_S^*H_a\otimes p_S^!N_0\to p_S^!(H_a\otimes N_0)\to p_S^!N_0$.
The Hom below is Hom of left modules. Precomposition with the
right $H_b$-action on $P$ supplies the left $H_b$-action on its
value, equivalently the required right $H_b^{\mathrm{op}}$-action.
Set
\[
 \Psi_{P^D}(N_0)=Rp_{T,*}
    R\mathcal Hom_{p_S^*H_a}(P,p_S^!N_0).
\]
If $N_0=D_SM_0$, the bounded integrand established above and actual
support duality give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_T\Phi_P(M_0)
 &\simeq Rp_{T,*}D_{S_M\times T_N}
                 (p_S^*M_0\otimes^L_{p_S^*H_a}P)\\
 &\simeq Rp_{T,*}R\mathcal Hom_{p_S^*H_a}
                 (P,p_S^!D_SM_0).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Put $\omega=\omega_{S_M\times T_N}$. The middle identification
is the actual balanced tensor--Hom adjunction
$R\mathcal Hom_Q(p_S^*M_0\otimes_{p_S^*H_a}^LP,\omega)
\simeq R\mathcal Hom_{p_S^*H_a}(P,
R\mathcal Hom_Q(p_S^*M_0,\omega))$.
It holds in the ambient module category by the relative bar/Hom
adjunction; no interchange of an infinite limit with a colimit is
used. The bounded integrand identifies its left side with geometric
Verdier duality, and actual exchange for $p_S$ identifies the inner
Hom on the right with $p_S^!D_SM_0$. This proves the boundedness of
this particular Hom integrand as well as the displayed comparison;
it does not assert boundedness of arbitrary relative internal Hom.
No tensor-dual formula for an arbitrary attached coefficient is used.  The pairing
is evaluation followed by the geometric support trace.  It specifies
the comparison, shows it is natural in coefficients and kernels,
and proves that this already defined Hom expression is bounded and
lies in the supported profile type.  Biduality gives every supported
input in this form.  Applying the same evaluation on a threefold
product and transitivity of trace identifies its convolution mate
with $D(L\star P)$, and dualizes the actual localization boundary.
The coefficient refinements on this side are $F_u^D$, as required.
This proves Verdier compatibility of the model comparisons with
actual geometric mates, rather than only a formal opposite action.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Endpoint scope of the kernel theorem]
The one-sided counit theorem uses the same actual model change on
both endpoints of a one-model kernel.  Mixed-model generated words
are realized geometrically and refinement graphs are then localized.
Section~\ref{sec:independent-curves} extends the rectangular part to
independent presentations and gives a counterexample for unrestricted
coupled kernels. We do not assert that all constructible kernels
belong to the generated source.  A kernel category need not be
unchanged as a category of actual kernels even when its Morita
realization is compatible with changes of presentation.
\end{remark}

\section{Kernels between curve presentations}
\label{sec:independent-curves}
\subsection{Rectangles and independent model changes}
The two endpoints need not belong to the same curve for rectangular
kernels. The distinction between these kernels and geometrically
coupled kernels is essential, even when their actions on selected
coefficients agree.

Fix a finite labelled family $(\mathscr M_i,\mathfrak p_i)_{i\in I}$
of integer-cut components and geometric profiles over the same trait
and residue field. Choose one presentation $M_i$ for each label.
Objects are the pairs $(i,S)$ in their finite support diagrams.
Define a geometric kernel source $\mathcal K(\mathbf M)$ by the
following generators: within each label, all same-model generators
of Definition~\ref{def:profile-kernel-source}; between any two labels,
all actual rectangles $C\boxtimes_Q E$ with coefficients in their
independently defined profile categories. Take finite convolutions,
cones, shifts and retracts, with full actual kernel mapping spectra.
No comparison between the generic curves, no common coarse model,
and no invertibility condition on a kernel counit enters this
definition. Cross-label isomorphism graphs and general correspondences
are not additional generators, even if two labels happen to denote
isomorphic curves.

\begin{theorem}[Independent model changes for the rectangular extension]
\label{thm:independent-curve-descent}
For independently chosen actual modifications
$u_i:M_i'\to M_i$ of these presentations, the actual kernel functors
\[
 T_{ij}(P)=V_j\star P\star U_i
         \simeq R(u_{i,S}\times u_{j,T})_*P
\]
define a unital, strongly convolution-compatible functor
$\mathcal K(\mathbf M')\to\mathcal K(\mathbf M)$.
For every generated $P:(i,S)\to(j,T)$ the actual target counit
\[
 U_j\star T_{ij}(P)\longrightarrow P\star U_i
\]
is an equivalence of kernels. All generated kernels, action
integrands and convolution integrands are bounded constructible.
The multi-object Morita realization and its actual Verdier mates
are compatible with these comparisons and coherent strings of
independent model changes. At the level of this realization the
result is independent of the chosen presentations in each component.
It does not assert an equivalence of the actual kernel categories.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Within one label the controlling kernels are the support graphs
$G_i(W)$ and rectangles of Proposition~\ref{prop:profile-kernel-control}.
Add all cross-label rectangles $R_{ij}(C,E)$ to that controlling
family. A pair of graph kernels can compose only within the same
label, where its product is the graph of the support intersection.
Composing a graph with a rectangle, on either side, restricts and
extends by zero the corresponding coefficient. For two rectangles,
possibly joining three different curves, the actual product formula is
\[
 R_{jk}(C',E')\star R_{ij}(C,E)
 \simeq R\Gamma_c(T_{M_j},E\otimes^L_{H_j}C')
                          \otimes_Q R_{ik}(C,E').
\]
These are the same support and projection maps on the threefold
product over $k$; they do not require the outer factors to be the
same curve. Lemmas~\ref{lem:profile-support-closure} and
\ref{lem:profile-tensor-projection} prove preservation and finiteness.
The four integrand checks in
Proposition~\ref{prop:profile-kernel-control} likewise use only the
intermediate algebra and the corresponding coefficients, so apply
on these products of different curves. Finite stable closure gives
boundedness and action preservation for the full generated source.
In particular any word that crosses labels reduces to the finite
stable envelope of rectangles between its two endpoints, even if it
returns to its starting label. It creates no unexamined coupled graph.

For a rectangle on the fine presentations, proper product base
change and the actual graph projection formulas give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 T_{ij}(C'\boxtimes E')&\simeq G_iC'\boxtimes G_jE',\\
 U_j\star T_{ij}(C'\boxtimes E')
   &\simeq G_iC'\boxtimes F_jG_jE',\\
 (C'\boxtimes E')\star U_i&\simeq G_iC'\boxtimes E'.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Under these specified kernel comparisons the actual map is
$1\boxtimes\epsilon^j_{E'}$. Theorem A for the $j$th curve makes
it invertible. The modifications at $i$ and $j$ have not been
identified. For a support graph, both endpoints have the same label,
hence use the same $u_i$; Theorem B gives exactly the required actual
counit. The controlling family and exactness in finite cones and
retracts prove the counit for every generated kernel, naturally on
all its actual maps. They also show that $T_{ij}$ lands in the
specified coarse source.

For $P:i\to j$ and $L:j\to k$, the composition comparison inserts
this same counit:
\[
 V_k\star L\star U_j\star V_j\star P\star U_i
       \longrightarrow V_k\star L\star P\star U_i.
\]
It is invertible by the assertion just proved for $P$. A diagonal
unit has one label at both ends, so its unit comparison is the
actual algebra unit for $u_i$, not an assertion about two unrelated
contractions. Adjunction triangles give the unit and associative
cells; the actual graph maps and product pushforwards give their
compatibility with strings of modifications.

The action of each generated kernel preserves the appropriate
profile category. Enriched Yoneda therefore gives a right-target-
perfect multi-object Morita bimodule, and its tensor composition
is the actual action composition. The geometric tensor--Hom and
Verdier argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel-morita-verdier} applies
to $S_{M_i}\times_k T_{M_j}$ without change; the integrands needed
there have just been proved bounded. Evaluation and trace specify
the supported mates of the same comparisons. Finally each
$\mathscr M_i$ has its own canonical finest presentation. Apply
Theorem A independently in each component and conjugate the kernel
actions by these coefficient equivalences. The actual counit squares
just proved compare coarsening with that conjugation. Common-refinement
independence and higher cells follow componentwise from A; no claim
that all the curves have a common geometric model is made.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The obstruction for unrestricted coupled kernels}
\begin{example}[An obstruction to arbitrary independent endpoint descent]
\label{ex:independent-endpoint-obstruction}
Let $u:M'\to M$ be a one-cut modification of a thickness-two node
$q$, and write $Y',Y$ for the special fibres. Choose a profile in
which the exceptional component is unmarked, so that both $M$ and
$M'$ are presentations. Take two labelled copies of $Y'$ with this
profile. Leave the source presentation
unchanged and contract the target by $u$. Consider the actual
identity graph kernel $P=\Delta_*H'$ between the two copies.
If one admitted this cross-label graph with arbitrary independent
endpoint changes, its proposed comparison would be
\[
 U_u\star V_u\longrightarrow\Delta_*H'.
\]
This map is not an equivalence of actual kernels.

Indeed choose two distinct geometric points $x,y$ in the open
exceptional component $C^\circ$. Both map to $q$ and have nearby
algebra $Q$. In the graph convolution the intersecting support is
$Y'\times_Y Y'$. Its projection to $Y'\times_kY'$ is a closed
immersion, since $Y$ is separated, and the middle coordinate is
uniquely determined. Pushforward on this support is therefore
extension along that closed immersion. Taking stalks of the
relative tensor in the ambient module category gives
\[
 (U_u\star V_u)_{(x,y)}\simeq Q\otimes^L_{A_q}Q,
 \qquad (\Delta_*H')_{(x,y)}=0.
\]
The restrictions of $u^{-1}i_q^*H$ and $H'$ to $C^\circ$ are
the constant algebra families $A_q$ and $Q$. Choose an etale path
between $x$ and $y$ on this connected stratum. Naturality of the
actual algebra map along that path identifies the two stalk
augmentations up to homotopy. Only existence of this identification,
not a canonical choice or a claim that its space is contractible,
is needed below.
The balanced multiplication $Q\otimes^L_{A_q}Q\to Q$, together with
its unit from $Q$, has composite $\mathrm{id}_Q$. Consequently this
stalk is nonzero. No boundedness or finite-dimensionality of the bare
projection kernel is needed for the contradiction; failure of those
properties would be a further obstruction to enlarging the source.

The failure persists despite equality of the induced actions on the
selected profile category: Theorem A makes $F_uG_u$ the identity
there. Thus coefficient descent cannot prove this missing kernel
comparison. The example does not contradict Theorem B, which
contracts both endpoints by the same map, nor
Theorem~\ref{thm:independent-curve-descent}, whose cross-label
source has independently specified rectangular generators.
\end{example}

\subsection{Finite etale support spans}
\label{sec:etale-spans}
We next enlarge only the basic geometric kernels of Theorem B.
The trait, geometric coefficients, integer-cut models, profiles and
products over $k$ are unchanged. The additional kernels are finite
etale support spans. Their model changes must be Cartesian; this is
an independently checkable geometric requirement, not a condition
on their coefficient actions or their counits.

Fix a finite generic diagram $\mathcal D$ of finite etale covers and spans over
the fixed generic base curve. A presentation consists of a base
model $X$ of a fixed profile $\mathfrak p$ and actual finite etale
model covers $\pi_i:X_i\to X$, together with actual finite etale
model spans for the specified generic spans, all with their generic
identifications. This is a condition on actual schemes and maps,
not on a coefficient descent functor. Finite disjoint unions of
nodal models are allowed. Include identity spans and their support
restrictions. Give each $X_i$ the marking pulled back from
$\mathfrak p$ on the canonical finest models. For each base support
$S$, retain its full inverse image $S_i$ in $X_i$. We use these
finite inverse-image support diagrams, not arbitrary additional
supports on the covers. Existence of such a presentation is not
asserted for every proposed generic diagram on every model.

\begin{lemma}[Finite etale maps preserve the lifted profile]
\label{lem:etale-profile}
The lifted markings define geometric profiles. Finite etale maps
between these covers preserve the profile categories by actual
pullback and finite direct image, with their supported Verdier mates.
The same holds on their full inverse-image support restrictions.
For an integer-cut modification $u:X'\to X$, the pullbacks
$X_i'=X_i\times_X X'$ are actual integer-cut modifications of $X_i$,
and the finest-model and coefficient comparisons are the actual
etale and proper base-change comparisons.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Formation of the reduced nonregular locus commutes with etale base
change. The successive blow-ups in Lemma~\ref{lem:finest-model}
therefore do so as well; their local node thicknesses and cuts are
unchanged. Hence the finest model of $X_i$ is the pullback of that
of $X$. The generic-identity comparison is the unique one described
in that lemma.

A finite etale cover of a closed unmarked block is a disjoint union
of copies of that block. For a point this is immediate. For a rational
chain, pull the cover back to its normalization. Each connected cover
of a $\mathbf P^1$ component has degree one: the unramified
Riemann--Hurwitz formula gives $2g-2=-2d$, so $d=1$.
At nodes an etale cover glues the same number of sheets of the two
branches. The incidence graph is a tree, so successive choices at
its leaves trivialize these sheet permutations without a cycle
condition. Thus the whole block cover splits. In particular the
unmarked pieces upstairs are again rational paths; no circular
unmarked block is introduced. Marked components remain marked and
may retain arbitrary horizontal monodromy.

Actual etale nearby base change identifies $H_{X_i}$ with
$\pi_i^*H_X$, including algebra actions and branch maps. Pullback
preserves the stalk and lisse conditions. Finite etale direct image
has stalks that are finite sums over sheets. It preserves lisse
coefficients on marked strata, perfect modules at nodes, and the
internal branch isomorphisms on each split block. There is no new
condition on exterior attachments. These operations are bounded;
no invariants or division by the degree is used. The support
assertions follow by their Cartesian support squares. Finally
$f^!=f^*$ for an etale map and $Rf_!=Rf_*$ for a finite map give
the actual Verdier exchanges, evaluation and finite trace. Applying
the same geometric base-change maps to $u$ proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

For supports $S,T$ and a support $W\subseteq S\cap T$ in the base
diagram, represent a specified span of $\mathcal D$ by a finite
etale $X$-scheme $Z$ with $X$-maps
\[
 X_i\xleftarrow{p}Z\xrightarrow{q}X_j.
\]
Both maps are finite etale: etale locally on $X$ they are maps of
finite sets of sheets. Write $Z_W$ for the full special-fibre
inverse image of $W$, and form the actual kernel
\[
 K_{ij}(Z,W)=(p,q)_!H_{Z_W}\quad\hbox{on }S_i\times_k T_j.
\]
The map factors as a finite map to the product over $W$, followed
by the support immersion into the product over $k$. Both actions
come from the actual etale identifications of nearby algebras.
Include all these span kernels, and all rectangles of independently
defined profile coefficients on the $S_i$, then take finite
convolutions, cones, shifts and retracts with full actual kernel
mapping spectra. Denote this source by $\mathcal K_{{\rm et},\mathcal D}(X)$.
The identity spans and their support restrictions include the old
units and support graphs; include their actual localization arrows.
This definition does not refer to invertibility of any descent map.

\begin{lemma}[Existing finite etale presentations form Cartesian squares]
\label{lem:etale-cartesian-presentations}
Let $u:X'\to X$ be an allowed base modification and suppose both
models carry actual finite etale presentations of the same generic
diagram $\mathcal D$. Their cover and span squares are canonically
Cartesian, compatibly with composition. Conversely an existing
presentation on $X$ pulls back to one on $X'$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Nodal total spaces $xy=t^m$ are
normal, as follows from the hypersurface $S_2$ property and
regularity in codimension one. Finite etale covers are normal as
well. The pullback $X_i\times_X X'$ and the independently given
$X_i'$ are finite normal covers of $X'$ with the same identified
generic cover; both are its normalization in that cover. Their
unique isomorphism identifies the generic maps, hence the whole
diagram, by separatedness and generic density. The same applies
to every span model $Z$. Thus Cartesian compatibility is a proved
consequence of these geometric presentations. Conversely any
existing presentation pulls back to one on $X'$.

The uniqueness of these generic-identity comparisons also identifies
their composites on any string of modifications.
\end{proof}

The fine kernel source is defined by its actual finite etale model
generators and its selected coefficients, not as the image of a
coarse kernel source. The comparison just proved permits us to
write $Z'=Z\times_X X'$ in the proof. We do not assume that every
arbitrary cover first given on $X'$ has a presentation on every
coarser model. The common base is part of the geometric cover
diagram; its presentation is allowed to change, and is not a
distinguished coarse anchor for descent.

\begin{theorem}[Theorem B with finite etale support spans]
\label{thm:etale-span-descent}
Fix the generic finite etale cover/span diagram $\mathcal D$ and
its prescribed profiles and support diagram. Let $u:X'\to X$ be
an allowed actual base modification, with both $X$ and $X'$ carrying
existing finite etale presentations of that same generic diagram.
Their cover and span squares are canonically Cartesian by
Lemma~\ref{lem:etale-cartesian-presentations}.
At each such actual presentation, every generated kernel in
$\mathcal K_{{\rm et},\mathcal D}(X)$ and each action or convolution
integrand is bounded constructible. The resulting actions preserve
the selected profile categories.
For this $u$ and every generated fine kernel $P:i\to j$, actual
coarsening has values in the specified coarse source and satisfies
\[
 T(P)=V_j\star P\star U_i\simeq R(u_i\times u_j)_*P,
 \qquad U_j\star T(P)\xrightarrow{\sim}P\star U_i,
\]
where the displayed map is the actual target counit. It preserves
units and convolution strongly, and gives the corresponding actual
Morita--Verdier comparisons. These are coherent on strings of
Cartesian modifications and independent of common refinement among
existing presentations of the fixed finite etale diagram.
No existence of a presentation on an arbitrary coarser model is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $p_W:Z_W\to W_i$, $q_W:Z_W\to W_j$ for the finite etale
maps and $e$ for the indicated support inclusions. The span action is
\[
 \Phi_{K_{ij}(Z,W)}=
 e_{W_j,T_j,!}\,Rq_{W,*}\,p_W^*\,e_{W_i,S_i}^*.
\]
It preserves the profile by Lemmas~\ref{lem:etale-profile} and
\ref{lem:profile-support-closure}. Actual finite base change and
regular-module cancellation give
\[
 K_{jk}(Z_2,V)\star K_{ij}(Z_1,W)
 \simeq K_{ik}(Z_1\times_{X_j}Z_2,W\cap V).
\]
The new middle scheme is finite etale over $X$, so it is a
controlling span obtained by composing the specified generators.
The formula includes multiplicities as actual sheets,
not their Euler characteristics. In the first mixed formula use
$p_W:Z_W\to W_i$ and $q_W:Z_W\to W_j$ as above. In the second
use a separate span $X_j\leftarrow\widehat Z\to X_k$, with maps
$\widehat p_V:\widehat Z_V\to V_j$ and
$\widehat q_V:\widehat Z_V\to V_k$. Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_{jk}(C,E)\star K_{ij}(Z,W)
  &\simeq R_{ik}\bigl(e_{W_i,S_i,!}Rp_{W,*}q_W^*(C|_{W_j}),E\bigr),\\
 K_{jk}(\widehat Z,V)\star R_{ij}(C,E)
  &\simeq R_{ik}\bigl(C,e_{V_k,T_k,!}
       R\widehat q_{V,*}\widehat p_V^*(E|_{V_j})\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Rectangle--rectangle multiplication is the formula in
Theorem~\ref{thm:independent-curve-descent}.
All four are actual kernel projection and support base-change maps
with both outer algebra actions.

These formulas give a controlling family of spans and rectangles.
Before direct image, span--span integrands cancel the intermediate
regular algebra on each finite sheet. Mixed integrands give the
finite etale restrictions of the selected rectangle coefficient;
the two-rectangle case uses the bounded relative tensor of selected
coefficients. The finite maps have finite fibres, and support
immersions preserve constructibility. Thus these integrands are
bounded constructible before convolution. The same checks apply to
a coefficient action. The four formulas, followed by exactness in
each variable under finite cones and retracts, prove all the stated
boundedness and preservation assertions for the generated source.

It remains to verify the kernel counit, not merely its action.
For a span let $v:Z'\to Z$ be the Cartesian modification. Its
nearby algebra unit is an equivalence by actual proper nearby base
change, exactly as in Lemma~\ref{lem:curve-unit}:
\[
 H_Z\xrightarrow{\sim}Rv_*H_{Z'}.
\]
Support restriction uses complete inverse images. Proper support
base change consequently identifies
$T(K_{ij}(Z',W'))$ with $K_{ij}(Z,W)$.
For the target-side pullback, the square
\[
 \begin{matrix}
 Z'&\xrightarrow{q'}&X_j'\\
 v\downarrow&&\downarrow u_j\\
 Z&\xrightarrow{q}&X_j
 \end{matrix}
\]
is Cartesian by Lemma~\ref{lem:etale-cartesian-presentations}.
Put $\kappa'(z')=(pv(z'),q'(z'))$ on $Z'_{W'}$, with target
$S_i\times_k T_j'$, and write $v_W:Z'_{W'}\to Z_W$.
The actual graph and support base-change maps identify
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 U_j\star T(K_{ij}(Z',W'))
  &\simeq R\kappa'_!\bigl(F_{v_W}G_{v_W}H_{Z'_{W'}}\bigr),\\
 K_{ij}(Z',W')\star U_i
  &\simeq R\kappa'_!H_{Z'_{W'}}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first identification uses
$q'^*H_{X_j'}\simeq H_{Z'}$ and regular cancellation; the second
composes the source graph with $(p',q')$. Under these specified
kernel comparisons the map is exactly
\[
 R\kappa'_!\bigl(\epsilon_{v_W,H_{Z'_{W'}}}\bigr).
\]
The coefficient counit is the inverse of the pulled-back algebra
unit by the adjunction triangle identity, also after the complete
support restriction. Its pushforward is therefore an equivalence
of kernels. This identifies the actual map, in addition to its
two object values.
On rectangles the map is $1\boxtimes\epsilon_{E'}$, as before.
The controlling family and finite stable closure prove the counit
and preservation by $T$ for every generated kernel.

Inserting this same middle counit proves strong convolution; the
identity span has the actual algebra unit comparison. All maps are
proper or etale base-change maps and adjunction units/counits.
Their composition identities, together with the associators of
actual fibre products, provide coherence on strings. The proved
coefficient preservation gives the multi-object Morita realization.
The bounded integrands allow the actual tensor--Hom/Verdier argument
of Theorem~\ref{thm:kernel-morita-verdier}; finite etale exchange
identifies its additional graph mates and their traces.

Finally pull any existing diagram presentation to the canonical
finest base model. Lemma~\ref{lem:etale-profile} gives the finest
cover models and the same lifted markings. For presentations with
specified identical generic cover diagrams, their pullbacks agree:
a finite etale cover of the normal finest base is the normalization
in its generic finite cover, and hence is uniquely determined.
The same holds for the span covers and their maps. This compares
existing presentations, without asserting their existence on every
coarser model. The preceding Cartesian comparisons and Theorem A
therefore give common-refinement independence with their higher
cells. A distinguished coarse anchor was not used to define the
coefficient or span conditions.
\end{proof}



\section{A cyclic curve and its kernels}
\label{sec:actual-loop}
\subsection{Continuous holonomy and localization}
\begin{proposition}[A geometric cyclic test for Theorem A]
\label{prop:actual-loop}
There is an actual proper nodal curve model over $\mathbf C[[t]]$
with a profile having two incomparable integer-cut presentations,
and a geometric nearby-module coefficient with nontrivial continuous
loop holonomy.  It has a nonzero localization boundary and admits
a nonzero noninvertible localization kernel.  Its coefficient,
boundary and Verdier mate are preserved by Theorem~\ref{thm:profile-descent}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take the projective cubic
\[
 M_0:\quad xyz+t^3(x^3+y^3+z^3)=0
 \quad\subseteq\mathbf P^2_{\mathbf C[[t]]}.
\]
It is flat and proper.  Its generic fibre is smooth: a singular point
cannot have a zero coordinate by the three partial derivatives;
if all coordinates are nonzero, multiplying those derivatives gives
$1=-27t^9$, impossible in $\mathbf C((t))$.  The special fibre
$Y_0$ is the triangle of the three coordinate lines.  At $[1:0:0]$
put $h=1+y^3+z^3$, a unit, and $a=y$, $b=-z/h$.
Then $ab=t^3$ and the coordinate map has invertible Jacobian at
the node.  The other two nodes are identical by symmetry.
Thus all three nodes have thickness three in actual etale charts.

Two actual scheme presentations are provided by the blow-ups of
\[
 \mathcal I_1=\operatorname{im}(\mathcal O_{M_0}(-1)
       \xrightarrow{y}\mathcal O_{M_0})+(t),\qquad
 \mathcal I_2=\operatorname{im}(\mathcal O_{M_0}(-1)
       \xrightarrow{y}\mathcal O_{M_0})+(t^2).
\]
Near either node on the line $y=0$ these are exactly the cut ideals
$(a,t)$ and $(a,t^2)$, giving cuts $\{1\}$ and $\{2\}$.
Off those nodes the ideals are invertible: away from $y=0$ they
are the unit ideal, and at a smooth point of that component the
cubic equation expresses $y$ as a multiple of $t^3$ in a local
trivialization.  Hence these global projective blow-ups are the
stated integer-cut modifications and are incomparable.  The third
node is unchanged.  Their common finest refinement resolves all
three thickness-three nodes.  Its dual graph is a cycle with nine
vertices.  Mark the three valuations of the original coordinate
lines.  The six unmarked vertices form three two-component paths,
so this is a profile as in Definition~\ref{def:marked-profile}.
Choose its finite closed-support diagram to be generated by the
three closed unmarked chain blocks and the whole fibre. On $M_0$
these blocks are precisely its three nodes; on every finer
presentation they are their complete inverse images. Thus the
open $U$, each node support on $M_0$, and all localization triangles
used below are actually included in the chosen support diagram.

We construct holonomy on the actual special curve $Y_0$, not on
an abstract graph.  Normalize its three lines to three copies of
$\mathbf P^1$ and name their node preimages $0,\infty$ in cyclic
order.  For each positive integer $N$, glue $3N$ copies of
$\mathbf P^1$ in a cycle and map them to the three original lines
according to their index modulo three.  This gives a finite
morphism $Y_N\to Y_0$.  On the smooth locus it is a disjoint union
of identities; at every node it identifies the two branch parameters,
hence induces an isomorphism of completed local rings
$\mathbf C[[a,b]]/(ab)$.  A finite map of these noetherian curves
with that local completion is etale at the node.  Rotation by three
components makes it a $\mathbf Z/N$-torsor everywhere.
The covers are compatible under divisibility of $N$.

Let $\lambda=1+\ell$ for odd $\ell$ and $\lambda=1+4$ for $\ell=2$.
At precision $r$, use $N=\ell^r$ and the character
$\mathbf Z/\ell^r\to(\mathbf Z/\ell^r)^\times$ sending $1$ to
$\lambda$.  It is well defined since $\lambda^{\ell^r}=1$
modulo $\ell^r$.  The associated sheaves and their compatible
reductions give a rank-one continuous integral local system
$L_{\lambda,\bullet}$ on $Y_0$.  Its rationalization $L_\lambda$
has holonomy $\lambda\ne1$, and is constant on the smooth locus
$U$, the disjoint union of three copies of $\mathbf G_m$.  This construction
exhibits both the actual covers and a stable lattice; continuity
is not inferred from an arbitrary scalar label on a graph.

Put $F=H_{M_0}\otimes_Q L_\lambda$.  It has module $A_q$ at each node;
its local vertical coefficient is the trivial unipotent coefficient,
with the nontrivial holonomy in the horizontal gluing.  Choosing
trivializations on the normalization, arrange that a single chosen
node $q$ carries the nontrivial gluing factor.  Its attachment is
\[
 A_q\longrightarrow B_0\oplus B_\infty,\qquad
 w\longmapsto(\beta_0(w),\lambda\beta_\infty(w)).
\]
In degree zero it sends $1$ to $(1,\lambda)$, hence is nonzero.
The actual mapping identification for localization proves that the
$q$-summand of its boundary $i_*\bigoplus_{q'}A_{q'}\to j_!Q_U[1]$
is nonzero.  Pullback identifies its
fine coefficient with $H_{M'}\otimes_Q u^*L_\lambda$; inserting the
exceptional components retains this holonomy and boundary by the
proved full mapping comparison.  The same applies to its Verdier mate.
We assert this in the nearby-supported source; a global algebraic
generic-fibre lift of $L_\lambda$ is not needed or asserted here.

Finally let $E=j_!Q_U$, an $H_{M_0}$-module, and take the actual
diagonal kernel $K=\Delta_*E$ with its two $H_{M_0}$-actions.
Its action is $j_!j^*$, by the diagonal and open projection formulas.
The same formulas give $K\star K\simeq K$ as actual kernels.
It is nonzero, fixes $j_!Q_U$, and kills the nonzero object $i_*A_q$,
so cannot be invertible.  The complete inverse-image open $U$ gives
the corresponding kernel on either partial presentation with the
same calculation.  This verifies a noninvertible kernel and a
convolution on the same geometric test.  This supplies the concrete idempotent test for
Theorem~\ref{thm:profile-kernel-descent}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A noninvertible rectangular kernel}
\begin{corollary}[A noninvertible rectangle on the cyclic test]
\label{cor:actual-loop-rectangle}
On the actual curve of Proposition~\ref{prop:actual-loop}, let
$j:U\hookrightarrow Y_0$ be the union of the three smooth open
strata and put $E=j_!Q_U$.  The actual bimodule kernel
$P=E\boxtimes_Q E$ belongs to the geometric generated source and
is nonzero and noninvertible.  Its convolution is
\[
 P\star P\simeq R\Gamma_c(U,Q)\otimes_Q P.
\]
This kernel and its convolution descend from either of the two
integer-cut presentations in that proposition by the actual
comparisons of Theorem~\ref{thm:profile-kernel-descent}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The coefficient $E$ is in the profile category: it is constant on
marked open strata and zero on all closed blocks.  Its exterior
attachments are permitted to be zero.  On $U$ the nearby algebra
is $Q_U$, so extension by zero and the relative tensor projection
formula give $E\otimes_H^L E\simeq E$.  The rectangle calculation
of Proposition~\ref{prop:profile-kernel-control} therefore gives,
as an equivalence of actual kernels with both module actions,
\[
 (E\boxtimes E)\star(E\boxtimes E)
 \simeq R\Gamma_c(Y_0,E\otimes_H^L E)\otimes_Q(E\boxtimes E)
 \simeq R\Gamma_c(U,Q)\otimes_Q P.
\]
It also gives the actual action
$\Phi_P(M)=R\Gamma_c(U,j^*M)\otimes_Q E$.
The restriction of $P$ to $U\times U$ is the nonzero constant
kernel.  Moreover $U$ is the disjoint union of three copies of
$\mathbf G_m$, so $H_c^1(U,Q)=Q^{\oplus3}$ and
$H_c^2(U,Q)=Q(-1)^{\oplus3}$; in particular its convolution above
is nonzero.  For any node $q$, the nonzero profile object
$i_{q,*}A_q$ is killed by $\Phi_P$.  An invertible kernel would
induce an invertible action, which cannot kill this object.
Thus $P$ is noninvertible.  This argument does not infer a kernel
equivalence from equality of actions: the displayed convolution
was already computed in the actual kernel category.

For either modification $u:M'\to M_0$, the complete inverse-image
open is still $U$.  The ordinary support comparison identifies
$F_uE$ with $E'=j'_!Q_U$.  Form $P'=E'\boxtimes E'$ in that
presentation.  The actual rectangle pushforward and target counit
of Theorem~\ref{thm:profile-kernel-descent} give
$T_u(P')\simeq P$ and
$T_u(P'\star P')\simeq P\star P$.
Hence this is a rectangular-kernel test on the same actual curve
and presentations as the continuous holonomy and nonzero boundary,
not only a test by modification equivalences or diagonal kernels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A finite etale quotient and coupled kernels}
\begin{example}[A coupled cyclic-cover kernel on the actual triangular curve]
\label{ex:etale-cyclic-span}
Let $N$ be the cubic model of Proposition~\ref{prop:actual-loop}
over $\mathbf C[[t]]$, and let $\sigma[x:y:z]=[y:z:x]$.
The constant group $G=\langle\sigma\rangle\simeq\mathbf Z/3$
acts freely on $N$. Indeed the only projective fixed points of a
nonidentity element are its three eigenlines. On each the cubic
has value a nonzero scalar multiple of $1+3t^3$, a unit in the trait.
Thus none lies on $N$, on either the generic or the special fibre.
The permutation linearizes the ample bundle $\mathcal O_N(1)$.
The projective finite-group quotient can therefore be constructed
from its invariant section ring. Since the action is free and $G$
is finite etale, the resulting map $p:N\to X=N/G$ is a finite
etale $G$-torsor. Flatness, nodality and
smoothness of the generic fibre descend along this etale cover;
$X$ is an actual proper nodal model. Its special fibre is the nodal
one-gon quotient of the triangle, and its node has thickness three.
Mark its original component on the finest model; the two unmarked
components form a path returning to that marked component. Use the
base support diagram generated by this closed block and the whole
fibre, with its open complement. Take the cover family
$\mathrm{id}_X,p$ and the generic graph of $p$ and its transpose
as the additional span generators.
The lifted marking on $N$ is the three-marked-component marking
of Proposition~\ref{prop:actual-loop}. Here use the inverse-image
support diagram: the base node pulls back to the union of all three
node blocks, not to an individually selected one. This smaller
diagram still contains the full-node localization triangle used
below; it need not contain every extra support of that proposition.

Now write $p$ also for the special-fibre map. The graph kernel is
$\Gamma_p=(\mathrm{id},p)_*H_N$ and its transpose is
$\Gamma_p^t=(p,\mathrm{id})_*H_N$ on the corresponding products
over $k$; their actions are $Rp_*$ and $p^*$ respectively.
Here transposition exchanges geometric endpoints; it is distinct
from the supported Verdier dual, which keeps their order.
The actual torsor identity
$N\times_XN\simeq\coprod_{g\in G}N$, with its graph maps, gives
on the special-fibre products
\[
 \Gamma_p^t\star\Gamma_p\simeq
       \bigoplus_{g\in G}\Gamma_g,
 \qquad
 \Gamma_p\star\Gamma_p^t\simeq\Delta_*Rp_*H_N.
\]
The latter coefficient is $H_X\otimes_Q p_*Q$; its permutation
local system is retained, not replaced by a scalar degree.
Moreover $\Gamma_p$ itself is noninvertible. On the selected
coefficient categories $Rp_*$ has right adjoint $p^*$, and
$p^*Rp_*H_N\simeq H_N^{\oplus3}$, which has rank three rather than
one on each smooth stratum. Hence this adjoint composite is not
the identity and $Rp_*$ is not an equivalence; an invertible kernel
would induce one.
The action rotates components around the loop and supplies genuinely
coupled kernels. For example $\Gamma_\sigma$ on the product of two
different smooth component strata is supported on its graph, so is
nonzero and not lisse on that product. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:profile-kernel-control}, every original
same-model kernel is in the finite stable envelope of diagonal
support kernels and rectangles. The former vanish on this product
and the latter are lisse there; finite cones and retracts remain
lisse. Thus $\Gamma_\sigma$ is not already in that original generated
source.

Resolve the thickness-three node of $X$ by the actual canonical
integer-cut modification. Its pullback to $N$ resolves the three
nodes equivariantly. Theorem~\ref{thm:etale-span-descent} applies to
these Cartesian diagram presentations and preserves both displayed
kernel convolutions and their Verdier mates. For the coefficient
$F=H_N\otimes L_\lambda$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:actual-loop},
finite support base change identifies $Rp_*$ of its localization
triangle with that of $Rp_*F$. Its boundary remains nonzero:
$p^*Rp_*\delta_F\simeq\bigoplus_{g\in G}g^*\delta_F$ contains
$\delta_F$ as a direct summand. Thus the coupled kernel also tests
the actual nonzero supported boundary of the loop coefficient.
This theorem does not permit
contracting just one endpoint of a coupled graph as in
Example~\ref{ex:independent-endpoint-obstruction}.
\end{example}
